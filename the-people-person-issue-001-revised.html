HIRING TECHNOLOGY
Hire Smarter, Fairer in the Age of AI-Generated Applications
Image source: original photography for The People Person. A hiring manager at a modern desk reviewing a candidate file under bright, clinical office lighting.
THE SHORT VERSION
AI-generated resumes are flooding recruiters, forcing a structural rethink of how employers screen candidates. SHRM reports that structured AI interviews are emerging as a way to cut through the noise, boost fairness, and help HR teams hire smarter at scale.
THE DETAILS
• The volume of AI-generated job applications has reached a point where traditional screening methods cannot keep pace with the volume or sophistication of synthetic submissions.
• Structured AI interviewing tools are being adopted not just for efficiency but to standardize evaluation and reduce the risk of human bias in early-stage screening.
• For staffing firms placing hundreds of candidates weekly, the operational lift of verifying authentic applications is significant and the cost of getting it wrong is material.
• The firms moving fastest on structured, AI-aware screening are capturing trust premiums with both clients and candidates.
WHY IT MATTERS
If your screening process still assumes every applicant is a real person, it is already behind. The staffing firms investing in structured, AI-aware evaluation infrastructure now are building a competitive moat that will pay out in client retention and placement quality over the next two years.

REGULATION
New York Moves to Crack Down on Ghost Job Postings
Image source: original photography for The People Person. Two staffing professionals in conversation at a standing desk, early morning light.
THE SHORT VERSION
New York lawmakers are advancing a bill that would require employers to disclose whether advertised job openings are real, with steep fines for misleading or outdated postings. The legislation takes aim at the growing practice of posting positions with no intention of filling them.
THE DETAILS
• The proposed New York legislation requires employers to disclose whether a posted job opening represents an active, funded position or is being used for pipeline-building purposes.
• Steep fines are proposed for employers that maintain misleading, already-filled, or indefinitely open postings without clear disclosure.
• Staffing firms and RPO providers operating in New York may need to adjust job posting workflows and client communication protocols to ensure compliance.
• Other states are watching closely; a successful New York bill is likely to trigger copycat legislation in California, Illinois, and Massachusetts.
WHY IT MATTERS
For staffing firms and talent acquisition teams, ghost job legislation changes the compliance landscape overnight. The firms that proactively audit and clean their job boards now will be positioned as transparent, candidate-respecting partners when these disclosure requirements expand.

TALENT SYSTEMS
Everyone Wants a Referral Channel. Almost Nobody Builds the Part That Makes It Real.
Image source: original photography for The People Person. Two professionals shaking hands in a sunlit atrium lobby, the moment a referral is made.
THE SHORT VERSION
A detailed breakdown of why referral hiring, consistently the best-performing source of hire by time-to-fill, retention, and offer acceptance, still underperforms at most firms. The problem is not the concept. It is the infrastructure. A referral channel without structured follow-up, frictionless submission flows, and real-time status visibility for referrers is just a suggestion box with a payout promise. This is fundamentally an operationalization problem: everyone agrees referrals work, but almost nobody builds the systems that convert intent into placement.
THE DETAILS
• The 2026 affiliate-spend forecast is $13.81 billion, and most of it is wasted on programs that lack the infrastructure to convert referral intent into actual placements.
• The Power of Eight framework identifies the eight critical actions that separate high-performing referral programs from the rest: Trigger, Ask, Submit, Acknowledge, Review, Decide, Notify, and Reward.
• Three structural failures: no defined trigger moment (nobody asks when satisfaction is highest), no zero-friction handoff, and no feedback loop for referrers. Tracking and Terms must be established before Follow-through can become systematic.
• The fix involves assigning ownership, building a structured follow-up sequence triggered at referral submission, and giving referrers visibility into where their referral stands in the process.
WHY IT MATTERS
We break down the full referral engineering system, the three structural failures that kill referral channels, and the lightweight fix that requires zero custom software. The article covers the operationalization problem in detail, walks through the Power of Eight framework, and explains how Tracking, Terms, and Follow-through convert referral intent into measurable placement volume.

WORKFORCE POLICY
White House Issues Comprehensive Workforce Development Strategy
Image source: original photography for The People Person. A recruiter sitting alone at a quiet cafe table after hours, reviewing printed candidate profiles.
THE SHORT VERSION
President Trump's new workforce strategy aims to expand work-based learning, connect Americans to in-demand jobs, and prepare workers for the AI-driven economy. The comprehensive plan signals federal-level momentum behind skills-based hiring, apprenticeship expansion, and public-private training partnerships.
THE DETAILS
• The strategy emphasizes expanding work-based learning programs, with specific targets for apprenticeship participation across technology, healthcare, and advanced manufacturing sectors.
• Skills-based hiring is elevated as a federal priority, with new guidance encouraging agencies and contractors to drop degree requirements where competency assessments can demonstrate qualification.
• For staffing firms, the strategy creates tailwinds in sectors tied to federal workforce investment: IT modernization, healthcare staffing, and skilled trades placement.
• Public-private training partnerships are positioned as the primary delivery mechanism, creating new opportunities for staffing firms to serve as intermediaries between training programs and employer demand.
WHY IT MATTERS
For staffing firms, federal workforce policy is a demand signal. The firms that align their specialization strategies with the sectors targeted by this strategy -- IT, healthcare, advanced manufacturing -- are positioned to capture disproportionate placement volume as federal dollars flow into training and hiring pipelines.

THE PLAYBOOK
ONE USEFUL MOVE
The Referral Pipeline Audit (30 Minutes, One Spreadsheet)
Step 1: Pull the last 90 days of referral data. Count how many referrals were submitted, how many received a first response within 48 hours, and how many resulted in a placement.
Step 2: Calculate drop-off at each stage. Most staffing firms discover that a large share of qualified referrals never receive a follow-up after initial contact.
Step 3: For every referral still in limbo, assign a single owner and a 48-hour deadline for next action. Track completion.
Step 4: Pick the single biggest gap -- the stage with the highest drop-off -- and fix it before the next issue of this newsletter. Fix one handoff. Then measure the impact.
