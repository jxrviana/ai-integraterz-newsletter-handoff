REFRIGERANT

❄️ **[EPA Just Un-Killed Your R-410A Inventory](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2026/05/epa-hvac-refrigerants-r-410a-final-rule)**

[IMAGE — s1: A pallet of shrink-wrapped condensing units in a dim contractor warehouse, late-afternoon light through a half-open roll-up door, no people, no readable labels.]

**The Short Version:** EPA finalized a rule letting contractors keep installing HVAC units manufactured or imported before January 1, 2025 that run on R-410A. It takes effect July 27. The January 1, 2026 hard stop on installing older R-410A split systems is gone, and legacy equipment can be installed until existing supplies run out.

**The Details:**

- The relief applies only to units manufactured or imported before January 1, 2025. No new R-410A equipment is being built, and R-410A production is being phased down to 15% of current output by 2036.
- EPA had already signaled the deadline would be "deprioritized in response to concerns on the availability and cost of A2L refrigerant in the field."
- New York State kept its own January 1, 2026 deadline. R-410A systems cannot be installed there.
- Holland & Knight notes the related extensions: supermarket refrigeration and cold storage pushed to January 1, 2032, semiconductor manufacturing to 2030, refrigerated lab equipment to 2028. EPA projects $976 million in avoided engineering costs.

**Why It Matters:** Anybody sitting on R-410A stock just got its value back, and anybody who dumped it cheap last fall ate a loss that did not have to happen. The bigger shift is that A2L is now a competitive choice instead of a legal cliff. Shops that already trained and tooled up have to decide whether to keep charging the premium or defend share against holdouts still hanging legacy gear.

DEMAND

📞 **[HVAC Is The Most Booked Trade And The Slowest To Answer](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/)**

[IMAGE — s2: A desk phone lit up mid-ring on a cluttered dispatch counter, whiteboard schedule out of focus behind it, hard fluorescent office light, no people.]

**The Short Version:** Jobber's 2026 Home Service Trends Report, built on a December 2025 survey of 1,050 home service business owners, found HVAC is the most booked trade of any it surveyed — and the slowest to respond. Only 17% of HVAC businesses reply to a lead within one hour, the lowest figure of any industry in the study.

**The Details:**

- Jobber discloses its methodology, which is why we're citing it: 1,050 owners surveyed through Conjointly in December 2025, ±3 percentage points at 90% confidence, supplemented with anonymized Jobber platform data.
- 83% of HVAC businesses raised prices last year, the highest rate across trades. 71% say they are confident in their pricing.
- Over 60% earn $500K or more, 14% clear $2M, and nearly 40% expect significant revenue growth in 2026.
- 81.5% use AI, the highest adoption of any trade — but mostly for quoting, invoicing and communication. Only 40% use it for scheduling and dispatch.

**Why It Matters:** Being booked solid is exactly what makes a shop slow to pick up. The trade with the most demand pressure is the worst at answering it, and Jobber's own AI split shows where the gap sits — HVAC leads every trade at using software for paperwork and trails at using it for the response layer, which is the part that decides who gets the job.

Read more → https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/

M&A

📈 **[Blackstone Reportedly Paid 18.5x For 150,000 Memberships](https://homepros.news/champions-group-strikes-2-5-billion-blackstone-deal/)**

[IMAGE — s4: An empty glass-walled conference room high in an office tower at dusk, city lights below, one chair pushed back from the table, no people.]

**The Short Version:** Blackstone announced on February 17 that it was acquiring Champions Group, a residential HVAC platform running over 1,800 field technicians and 150,000 active members across tier-one metros. Homepros News, citing people familiar with the matter who requested anonymity, put the price at $2.5 billion on roughly $140 million of annualized EBITDA — about 18.5x.

**The Details:**

- Neither Champions Group nor Blackstone confirmed the financial details. The valuation and the multiple were never disclosed by the companies; both declined or did not respond to Homepros.
- The buyer vehicle is BXPE, Blackstone's perpetual private equity strategy. The deal was expected to close in the first half of 2026.
- Odyssey Investment Partners, the seller, and Champions management retain what Homepros describes as "significant" minority stakes.
- Advisors were William Blair, Piper Sandler and Baird. Homepros calls this the first platform-level residential HVAC transaction since Redwood Services' roughly $1.1 billion majority investment from Atlas Partners in May 2025.

**Why It Matters:** The number owners will quote each other is 18.5x, and nobody at either company has confirmed it. What was bought is not in dispute: 150,000 recurring memberships attached to 1,800 techs. A shop with the same revenue and no membership base does not get that multiple. A perpetual-capital vehicle also carries no five-to-seven-year exit clock, which changes what a rollover seller actually holds.

LEGAL

⚖️ **[Seven Makers, 90% Of The Market, One Lawsuit](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/hvac-manufacturers-face-second-suit-over-price-fixing-allegation/819562/)**

[IMAGE — s5: Unopened equipment cartons stacked to the ceiling down a distributor warehouse aisle, hard overhead light, receding perspective, no people, no readable labels.]

**The Short Version:** A second price-fixing suit was filed in April in Michigan federal court against Bosch, Carrier, Trane, Daikin, Lennox, Rheem and AAON — makers who control roughly 90% of the U.S. HVAC market through their subsidiaries. The named plaintiff is Richard Isom, owner of Air Tech Services, a Florida HVAC contractor. He is seeking class certification.

**The Details:**

- The first suit was filed in March in the same court by a consumer plaintiff, Alyssa Berg of Minnesota. The alleged conspiracy period begins no later than January 1, 2020.
- The complaint alleges that over six years commercial and residential systems cost 8% more than they would have in a competitive market — "billions of dollars extracted from direct purchasers."
- Evidence cited includes 40 documented instances of synchronized price increases, public announcements made through the ACHR News trade publication, and data sharing through AHRI.
- Carrier: "We deny the baseless allegations…and will fight it vigorously." Trane disputed the "baseless claims" and pledged a vigorous defense. Cooling Post's longer defendant list adds Johnson Controls entities, Mitsubishi Electric Trane, Viessmann, Allied Air Enterprises, Basx and Heat Transfer Products Group.

**Why It Matters:** The second plaintiff is a contractor, not a homeowner, which means dealers and installers could end up as class members with a claim on overcharges paid since 2020. Worth knowing where your equipment invoices back to 2020 actually live. And an allegation that price moves were signaled through the trade press changes how a person reads the next increase letter.

### Where The Next Twenty Jobs Come From

[IMAGE — s7: Three pairs of work boots lined up on a homeowner's front step — one muddy, one clean, one paint-spattered — shot from above in late-afternoon light, no people.]

In Jobber's survey, the top lead sources across home service were repeat customers (51%), word-of-mouth referrals (44%) and Google Search (36%). Every one of those is downstream of somebody else deciding to call you. Here are three channels you can work on a Tuesday, and they all run the same way: help first, ask second.

**1. Power partners.** These are the trades already standing in the house, looking straight at the problem, before the homeowner has called anyone about it — plumbers, electricians, roofers, general contractors, home inspectors, realtors, property managers, insurance agents and builders. A home inspector writes up a nineteen-year-old furnace three weeks before closing. A roofer is on a ladder next to a condenser that has been running loud since June. The referral arrives pre-trusted, because nobody collects three quotes on a recommendation from the guy already in their attic. **First move:** lead with what you can do for them — same-day diagnostic slots held for their clients, referrals of your own customers back to them, or twenty minutes with their crew on what a dying system looks and sounds like. Never open with "will you send us work."

**2. Niche creators.** Local home-improvement creators, regional lifestyle accounts, and the home-and-garden radio-adjacent personalities homeowners in your market actually trust. Follower count matters far less than geography and subject. **Fit test:** is the audience inside your service area, do they own their homes, and does this person already talk about house maintenance so an HVAC segment isn't a swerve? Three yeses, go. **First move:** propose something that helps their audience rather than a paid post that helps you — a "what your AC is trying to tell you" walkthrough, a free summer tune-up checklist their followers get, a live Q&A during the first heat wave. Give every creator a unique code or landing page so you know who actually produces.

**3. Podcast placements.** Local business shows, home-improvement shows, and regional real-estate podcasts — that last one matters because their audience stands in front of aging systems every week for a living. Thirty minutes of you explaining how to tell a repair from a replacement buys more trust than any ad slot on the same show. **First move:** pitch the host on what their listeners get, not on who you are. Name the segment, offer three angles and a one-sheet, and close every appearance with one soft call to action and a trackable link.
