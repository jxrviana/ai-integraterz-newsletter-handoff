**The Rundown:** FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee meets July 23â€“24 at the agency's White Oak campus to consider whether seven peptides belong on the 503A bulks list. **The Rundown:** DEA and HHS issued a fourth temporary extension of COVID-era telemedicine prescribing flexibilities, running January 1 through December 31, 2026. **The details:**

[IMAGE â€” s5: A rising line chart etched on the frosted-glass partition of a clinic hallway, out-of-focus staff moving behind it.]

**The Rundown:** A study of 33,607 commercially insured adults without diabetes found one-year persistence on high-potency weight-loss GLP-1s rose from 33.2% in 2021 to 60.9% in the first half of 2024. **The details:**

- One-year persistence by year: 33.2% in 2021, 34.1% in 2022, 40.2% in 2023, 60.9% in the first half of 2024.

- Mean proportion of days covered rose from 52.6% in 2021 to 72.8% in the first half of 2024. The share of patients reaching 80% adherence or better went from 30.2% to 55.5%.
- Persistence was defined as no gap longer than 60 days between the end of a days-supply and the next fill, within 365 days of starting.

**Why it matters:** Three of the four causes the authors name â€” dose escalation, side-effect management, structured lifestyle support â€” are protocol decisions a clinic controls, not properties of the drug. ### The Three Channels That Work When You Can't Run Ads

[IMAGE â€” s7: Three doorways cut into a single wall, marked with a stethoscope, a camera, and a microphone, minimal architectural illustration in the house palette.]

Platform policy takes large parts of paid acquisition off the table for this industry. What is left is not a consolation prize â€” it is three channels that compound, and they are all worked the same way: help first, ask second.

**1. Power partners.** These are the businesses that already have your customer at the moment he decides something has to change â€” primary care physicians, urologists, chiropractors, physical therapists, dietitians and nutritionists, gyms and personal trainers, med spas, and the HR or benefits lead at any large local employer. It works because the referral arrives pre-trusted; he is not evaluating you, he is taking his physical therapist's word for it. **First move:** open with what you can do for them â€” a men's-health talk for their members, a co-branded offer their clients get, or a referral of your patients to them. Never open with "will you refer to us."

**2. Niche influencers.** Fitness, longevity, and biohacking creators, plus the local athletic-community figures â€” gym owners, coaches, former athletes â€” who hold a room without holding a large following. Run the fit test before you spend anything: is the audience local or in-category, does it skew male 30â€“65, and does the creator already talk about health and self-improvement so that a men's-health mention is not a swerve? Three yes answers, pursue. **First move:** propose a collaboration that helps their audience rather than a paid post that helps you â€” a Q&A, a "what my bloodwork actually said" episode, a perk their community gets. Give every creator a unique code or landing page so you know who actually produces.

**3. Podcast placements.** Health-optimization and longevity shows for the clinical lead, local business shows for the owner. A men's health business sells trust before it sells anything else, and 30 to 45 minutes of the owner or medical lead teaching reaches the man searching "low energy" at 11pm better than any ad slot would. **First move:** pitch the host on what their listeners get, not on who you are. Name the segment, offer three angles and a one-sheet, and close every appearance with one soft call to action and a trackable link.
