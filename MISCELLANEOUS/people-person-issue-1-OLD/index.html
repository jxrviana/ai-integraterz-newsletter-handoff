📊 Staffing Limps Back To $180B

TL;DR: Staffing Industry Analysts’ March 2026 forecast, summarized by QX Global Group, projects US staffing revenue at $180.2 billion in 2026, up 1%, then $183.0 billion in 2027, up 2%. Both figures sit below the pre-pandemic baseline near $185.5 billion and well under the 2022 peak of $243.9 billion. Industrial staffing’s decline slowed to about 1% in 2025. CASE STUDY
📈 Same Team, 676% More Orders

TL;DR: Believe Resourcing, a global recruitment firm led by founder Angie Shamley, grew job orders from 59 in 2020 to 458 in 2024, a 676% increase, without proportional headcount growth, per a Recruiterflow case study. Recruiters moved from handling five roles each to 10–15 at once. Workflow automation saved the team 5,424 hours, roughly 226 working days, and about doubled overall productivity as the firm expanded to 20 staff worldwide.

TL;DR: Recruiterflow’s analysis of 2,100+ firms found the top 25% bill roughly $168,000 more revenue per recruiter each year than the rest. THE PLAYBOOK
 
Filling The Client Pipeline Without Ads

Most firms chase clients the expensive way: ads, cold lists, and a BD hire who burns out by month four. There’s a quieter way the top firms already run — three channels that put you in front of companies right before they need you. Here’s the whole method, no gate.

1) Power Partners. Someone is already in the room with the companies you want — before those companies ever open a req. Fractional HR and People-ops consultants, employment lawyers, PEOs, MSP/VMS advisors, business bankers, franchise consultants, M&A advisors. Their client is your client, and none of them fill roles, so you’re never competing. One good partner sends warm intros to companies actively adding headcount. The first move is help-first: bring them something for their clients — a comp benchmark, a hiring-market briefing, a candidate or client referral of your own — before you ask for a single introduction.

2) Niche Influencers. Every vertical has voices the hiring managers already follow — LinkedIn creators in your niche, HR and “future of work” writers, a well-known functional leader in the exact discipline you place. Their audience is your audience, so mentioning what you do isn’t a swerve; it’s on-topic. You don’t need a national name — you need the person your buyers actually read. The first move is a collaboration that helps their audience: go on their feed or newsletter and teach something real — comp trends, what’s moving in the hiring market — with a simple way for interested readers to reach you.

3) Podcast Placements. The founders and hiring managers you want spend their commutes with earbuds in. Founder and operator shows, the podcast for your niche’s decision-makers, HR and talent-leadership shows, local-business shows if you work a region. Thirty minutes of you teaching — not pitching — reaches the exact person who signs the fee agreement, and the recording keeps working as clips long after you’ve hung up. The first move is to pitch the host on their listeners, not on yourself: offer a segment their audience will actually use, close with one soft call to action, and use a trackable link so you know which shows produce.
