## Email body (markdown)

Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

Picture the Monday you're already halfway into: three proposals out, two clients asking for work that isn't in scope, and a prospect who wants a service you don't actually staff. You could hire — but the last hire took a quarter to pay for itself, and a slow stretch would turn it into a liability. So you turn the work away, or you quote it and quietly hope the freelancer delivers.

That squeeze — more demand than capacity, and clients now asking whether AI can just do it cheaper — is most of the job right now.

Here's the part that's easy to miss: the choice isn't only "turn work away" or "bloat payroll." Two levers add capacity and revenue without a full-time hire — partnerships that borrow demand and delivery you don't staff, and virtual assistants that take the low-value hours off your plate. Neither is new. What's changed is how many agencies now run them on purpose instead of by accident.

**What's working right now**

*Referral alliances between non-competing shops.* In an April 2025 [Forbes Agency Council piece](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesagencycouncil/2025/04/22/true-partnerships-16-agencies-most-important-sources-of-referrals/), Michael Fox of Corberry Digital described the arrangement plainly: a financial-consulting partner sends them clients who need marketing to scale, and they send back businesses that need financial optimization — then they co-host webinars to keep it warm. No ad spend, and the leads arrive pre-vetted by someone the client already trusts.

*White-labeling the service you don't want to staff.* Agencies increasingly resell SEO, paid media, email, or design through a partner and keep the client relationship, per [Promodo's breakdown](https://www.promodo.com/blog/white-label-partnership) — you offer the service now instead of hiring for it later.

*Delegating the hours that were never yours to work.* [Assist Virtual Partners](https://www.assistvirtualpartners.com/post/advanced-use-cases-for-virtual-assistants-in-marketing-and-operations) reports companies that handed lead-gen tasks to a VA recovered around 31.5 hours a month. On the PR side the leak is bigger: repetitive operational work eats 40–60% of practitioner time, per [PRovoke Media's 2025 survey](https://www.shadow.inc/resources/ai-for-pr-agencies).

**Your two plays this week**

**1) The partnership starter.** Pick one non-competing agency or vendor whose clients need what you sell — a web-dev shop with no paid-media arm, a PR firm with no content team. Send this:

> Hi [Name] — we both serve [audience], but we don't compete: you do [their thing], we do [your thing]. When your clients need [your service], I'd rather they hear your recommendation than find us cold — and I'll do the same for you. Worth 20 minutes to see where our client lists overlap?

Start with a plain referral. If it produces, graduate to a white-label arrangement so you keep the margin instead of waving the work goodbye.

**2) The VA starter.** Hand off the five tasks that don't need you: inbox and calendar triage, scheduling social posts, pulling the weekly client report, building first-draft outreach lists, and CRM updates. What makes it stick is a one-page delegation doc per task — the trigger, the steps, the tools and logins, what "done" looks like, and where to ask when stuck. If it won't fit on one page, the task isn't ready to hand off yet.

**The take.** The agencies pulling ahead aren't the ones with the biggest teams. They're the ones who stopped treating every job as either "my team does it" or "we pass." A partner list and a VA turn that binary into a system — and a system is exactly what you're selling when a client asks whether AI can do it cheaper.

## Staging notes

- **Placeholders to fill before send:** {{FIRST_NAME}}, {{SENDER_NAME}}, {{SENDER_TITLE}}, {{COMPANY_NAME}}, {{PHYSICAL_ADDRESS}}, {{UNSUBSCRIBE_LINK}}, {{PARTNERSHIPS_OFFER_LINE}}, {{RECRUITING_OFFER_LINE}}. Offer lines were left blank on purpose — Justin hasn't specified the partnerships-program and AI-Guy-recruiting one-liners yet.
- **JangoMail merge fields:** map {{FIRST_NAME}} to your JangoMail personalization field (e.g. `[FirstName]`) and set a fallback so a blank name reads cleanly — recommend defaulting to `there`, so the greeting becomes "Hi there,". Confirm the fallback renders before the full send; a broken merge tag on line one kills the open.
- **CTA mechanics:** this issue routes replies, not clicks — PARTNER and HIRE are keyword replies. Make sure the send-from address is a real, monitored inbox (not no-reply@), or the CTA dead-ends. If you'd rather route to forms/booking links, swap the two offer-line placeholders for links and adjust the sentence.
- **Compliance:** footer includes physical mailing address + unsubscribe, both required by CAN-SPAM. {{PHYSICAL_ADDRESS}} must be a real postal address. {{UNSUBSCRIBE_LINK}} should be JangoMail's system unsubscribe token so opt-outs are honored automatically.
- **Links:** four outbound source links in the body (Forbes, Promodo, Assist Virtual Partners, Shadow/PRovoke). Add UTM tags if you're tracking. All four were live and fetched on 2026-07-17.
- **Deliverability option for send #1:** on a fresh domain's first real send, consider stripping the hyperlinks and keeping attributions as plain text ("per a Forbes Agency Council piece, April 2025") — links return in issue #2 once inbox placement is proven. Decide after the seed check.
- **Footer honesty check:** the "came up in our research" line assumes a cold/compiled list. If Justin's list is actually opted-in from somewhere, swap in the true source ("you signed up at …") — the specific true reason always performs and complies best.
- **Formatting:** built mobile-first — short paragraphs, one blockquote (the outreach template). In JangoMail's HTML editor, render the template as an indented/quoted block so it's obviously copy-paste-able. Keep the two subheads ("What's working right now", "Your two plays this week") visually distinct.
- **Length:** email body runs ~610 words (inside the 500–700 target). Subject lines are unhyped by design (no "game-changing/unlock/revolutionize") per house voice.
- **Name:** masthead still says "Agency Insider" as a placeholder. Board top-pick for this niche is "The Retainer" — Justin to confirm before Issue #2.
