Good morning. Most partnership programs do not fail because the people involved dislike each other. They fail because the next referral depends on memory, timing, and a message nobody has written yet.

Preeminence is not being louder than everyone else. It is becoming the person who makes the customer decision clearer before anyone asks for the sale.

Here is the play. Stop treating referrals as a lucky byproduct of being good at your work. A referral channel is a commercial asset. It needs a clear promise, a person who can make the introduction, and a next step that is easy to execute.

Make the referral easy to activate

The partner does not need another vague request to keep you in mind. Give them a specific situation to notice, a short explanation to repeat, and one action to take. That is how a relationship becomes a channel without turning the relationship into a transaction.

Monday morning: Choose one partner. Write the exact customer situation that should trigger an introduction, the two-sentence explanation your partner can repeat, and the single link or reply action that completes the handoff. Send it before lunch.

Make the referral easy to activate

STORY IMAGE PLACEHOLDER · images/s1.jpg

The useful evidence: A partner can believe in your work and still never refer anyone if the behavior requires too much effort at the wrong moment. Behavior design gives you a useful lens: make the action clear, timely, and easy.

Prompt: what situation should trigger the referral? Script: what should the partner say? Handoff: where does the customer go next? Feedback: how will you tell the partner what happened?

Why it matters: The relationship is the raw material. The activation system is what makes the relationship commercially useful without making it feel forced.
