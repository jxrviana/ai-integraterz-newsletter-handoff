AI MEDIA
Q2 2026 Was the Quarter AI Media Started to Scale
Image source: original photography for Agency Insider. Photorealistic wide shot of a modern advertising agency office at dusk.
THE SHORT VERSION
The second quarter of 2026 marked the moment AI media moved from experiment to enterprise. OpenAI entered the advertising business, holding companies lined up as agency partners, and Google shipped new AI Mode ad formats at Marketing Live. The supporting infrastructure is now catching up with the ambition.
THE DETAILS
• Holding companies Dentsu, WPP, Publicis and Omnicom joined as agency partners, signaling institutional buy-in for AI media.
• At Google Marketing Live, the company delivered new ad formats in AI Mode including conversational discovery ads and highlighted answers.
• OpenAI declared it is "clearly in the advertising business now," with LiveRamp coming on board for first-party measurement.
• Supporting infrastructure around identity, measurement, and data connectivity is making AI media experiments easier to execute and scale.
WHY IT MATTERS
AI media is no longer a test budget line item. With holding companies formalizing partnerships and platforms shipping production-grade ad formats, agencies that have not built an AI media capability are now structurally behind the market.

AGENCY ECONOMICS
Forrester Predicts a 15% Agency Workforce Cut as Principal Media Surges
Image source: original photography for Agency Insider. Close-up photorealistic macro shot of a glass jar filled with glowing blue microchips.
THE SHORT VERSION
Forrester's 2026 predictions paint a stark picture: a 15% reduction in agency jobs will accelerate as AI and automation disrupt the labor-based economic model. Meanwhile, principal media, where agencies resell inventory with margins and guarantees, is forecast to account for nearly 33% of total agency billings.
THE DETAILS
• A 15% reduction in agency jobs is forecast for 2026, on top of an average 8% headcount cut in 2025.
• In 2026, principal media will account for nearly 33% of total agency billings as agencies shift from service fees to inventory resale.
• One global holding company CEO summarized the trajectory: "By 2028, we'll double profits and halve the people."
• 85% of US B2C marketing executives plan to review their media agencies in 2026, raising the stakes for every incumbent.
WHY IT MATTERS
The agency business model is being rewritten in real time. Independents that rely purely on labor-based pricing face margin compression from both sides: AI is reducing the value of headcount, while holding companies are scaling principal media at multiples independents cannot match without infrastructure investment.

AGENCY GROWTH
Everyone Wants a Referral Channel. Almost Nobody Builds the Part That Makes It Real.
Image source: original photography for Agency Insider. Warm photorealistic overhead flat lay of a wooden conference table with two coffee cups.
THE SHORT VERSION
Most businesses talk about referrals but never operationalize them as a genuine affiliate program. Meanwhile, US affiliate spending is forecast near $13.81 billion in 2026. The gap between knowing referrals work and actually building the system is where most agencies leave revenue on the table.
THE DETAILS
• Most businesses do not operationalize referral or partner programs, despite recognizing them as the highest-ROI acquisition channel.
• US affiliate spending is forecast near $13.81 billion in 2026, growing at nearly double the rate of overall US retail ecommerce.
• The Power of Eight framework identifies eight structural elements that turn ad-hoc introductions into a repeatable, trackable revenue channel.
• Execution comes down to three pillars: Tracking infrastructure, clear Terms and incentive design, and genuine Follow-through on every introduction.
WHY IT MATTERS
Most agencies spend disproportionately on outbound and paid acquisition while leaving their warmest pipeline, existing relationships and client networks, entirely unstructured. Building the referral infrastructure is the highest-ROI move most agency owners have not yet made.

CREATOR ECONOMY
Accenture Acquires Whalar as U.S. Creator Economy Spend Nears $44 Billion
Image source: original photography for Agency Insider. Photorealistic aerial drone view of a modern glass-walled boardroom.
THE SHORT VERSION
Accenture has agreed to acquire leading creator and social agency Whalar from Whalar Group, a deal that underscores how seriously the consulting giant is betting on the creator economy. Ad spend in the U.S. creator economy is expected to reach $43.9 billion in 2026, making it among the fastest-growing sectors in all of media.
THE DETAILS
• Ad spend in the U.S. creator economy is expected to reach $43.9 billion in 2026, per IAB data cited by Accenture.
• Whalar has executed more than $600 million in creator campaigns across over 40 countries and 15 languages.
• Accenture Song CEO Ndidi Oteh framed the deal around relevance: "Social is where brands are discovered, where modern commerce is happening."
• The acquisition signals that creator-led marketing is no longer experimental. It is becoming a core pillar of enterprise marketing strategy.
WHY IT MATTERS
Accenture does not make bets this size on unproven categories. The Whalar acquisition confirms that the creator economy has matured into an enterprise-grade channel. For independent agencies, the question is whether they build creator capabilities in-house or watch consultancies absorb the space.

THE PLAYBOOK
ONE USEFUL MOVE
Build a 30-Minute AI Token Audit for Your Agency
1. Pull your last 90 days of API billing from every AI provider your agency uses (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, etc.).
2. Categorize spend by client, by workflow type (creative, media planning, analytics, internal ops).
3. Identify any single client or workflow where token cost grew more than 50% month-over-month.
4. For each spike, ask: did the output quality or revenue from this client increase proportionally? If not, you have a margin leak.
5. Set a per-project token budget in your next SOW. Even a rough ceiling creates awareness.
