MANUFACTURING MILESTONE
First Solar Posts Record $2.1 Billion Half-Year as Gross Margin Hits 57 Percent
Image source: original photography for The Daily Sun. Vast solar panel manufacturing floor under high-bay industrial lighting, rows of thin-film modules moving along automated production lines.
THE SHORT VERSION
First Solar announced financial results for the second quarter of 2026, reporting just over $1 billion in net sales and a 57 percent gross margin. The Q2 figure led the company to a record first-half net sales total of $2.1 billion, while the 57 percent gross margin represents a 12-percentage-point increase over the prior year, also appearing to be a record.
THE DETAILS
• First Solar reported just over $1 billion in net sales for Q2 2026, driving a record first-half net sales total of $2.1 billion.
• The 57 percent gross margin rose 12 percentage points year-over-year, signaling strong pricing power and manufacturing efficiency.
• The company is balancing the upcoming Section 232 tariff decision while bringing new U.S. manufacturing facilities online.
• First Solar is advancing its next-generation CuRe and perovskite technologies alongside production scale-up.
WHY IT MATTERS
First Solar is the largest U.S.-based solar manufacturer. When its margins expand at this pace, it signals that domestic manufacturing can compete on economics, not just policy support. The CuRe and perovskite work also matters: the next generation of module efficiency is being built on American factory floors.

GRID DOMINANCE
Solar and Storage Account for 91 Percent of New U.S. Grid Capacity in First Half of 2026
Image source: original photography for The Daily Sun. Solar engineer at a substation at golden hour, looking out toward transmission lines and solar arrays stretching to the horizon.
THE SHORT VERSION
During the first quarter of 2026, solar and energy storage combined to account for 91 percent of all new electricity generation capacity added to the U.S. grid, according to data published by the Solar Energy Industries Association. Grid operators from California to Texas and across the Midwest registered record-breaking performance metrics, driven by competitive technology costs and rapid installation timelines.
THE DETAILS
• Solar and energy storage combined to account for 91 percent of all new electricity generation capacity added to the U.S. grid in Q1 2026.
• SEIA data indicates rapid deployment across major regional grids has driven a series of generation and capacity records during the first six months of the year.
• Solar and energy storage achieved several deployment and generation milestones, increasingly moving from emerging resources to primary grid components.
• Grid operators from California to Texas and across the Midwest registered record-breaking performance metrics.
WHY IT MATTERS
The energy transition is not a future forecast. It is a present-tense infrastructure build. The firms that positioned for it -- developers, EPCs, financiers, manufacturers -- are now operating at scale. The laggards are reading about it.

TALENT INFRASTRUCTURE
Your Solar Firm Is Growing. Your Hiring Infrastructure Is Not Keeping Up.
Image source: original photography for The Daily Sun. Solar installation crew working on a residential rooftop at midday, bright sun, clear blue sky.
THE SHORT VERSION
Solar is scaling faster than any energy sector in U.S. history. Installations are breaking records. Funding is pouring in. But the operationalization problem is the same across mid-sized solar firms: referral programs exist on paper, affiliate spend in the U.S. is forecast to reach $13.81 billion in 2026, and almost nobody builds the infrastructure to capture it. The gap between having a program and running one that produces is where most firms lose.
THE DETAILS
• The operationalization problem: most solar firms have referral and affiliate programs that exist on paper but lack Tracking, Terms, and Follow-through.
• U.S. affiliate spending is forecast to reach $13.81 billion in 2026, and the renewable energy sector is capturing almost none of that infrastructure.
• The Power of Eight framework identifies the structural elements that turn a referral program from a cost center into a growth engine.
• The fix: defined Tracking, clear Terms, and consistent Follow-through -- the same rigor applied to project delivery, now applied to hiring.
WHY IT MATTERS
The firms that build operational hiring infrastructure now will capture the talent that determines who wins the next five years of solar deployment. The article breaks down the full system, the structural failures that kill growth, and the lightweight fix that requires zero custom software.

CAPITAL MARKETS
Solar Corporate Funding Surges 131 Percent Year-Over-Year to $11.1 Billion in Q1 2026
Image source: original photography for The Daily Sun. Inside a solar panel distribution warehouse, pallets of panels under high-bay industrial lighting, forklift in midground.
THE SHORT VERSION
Corporate funding, public market financing, and debt investments in the global solar sector experienced 131 percent year-over-year growth to open the first quarter of 2026, according to Mercom Capital Group. The $11.1 billion raised across 53 deals represents a substantial volume growth from the $4.8 billion secured across 39 deals in the first quarter of 2025.
THE DETAILS
• The $11.1 billion raised across 53 deals represents 131 percent year-over-year growth, up from $4.8 billion across 39 deals in Q1 2025.
• Global venture capital funding for solar reached $1.1 billion across 17 deals in Q1 2026, up 74 percent quarter-over-quarter.
• Debt financing hit its highest level in more than a decade, signaling institutional confidence in project-level returns across the solar value chain.
• More capital means more projects, more demand for talent, equipment, and services across the entire solar value chain.
WHY IT MATTERS
The funding numbers confirm what the installation data already suggests: capital markets are betting heavily on solar. If you are anywhere in the solar value chain, from module manufacturing to installation crews to project finance, the addressable market just got meaningfully bigger.

THE PLAYBOOK
ONE USEFUL MOVE
The Solar Hiring Pipeline Audit (30 Minutes, One Spreadsheet)
Step 1: Pull the last 90 days of candidate data. Count how many candidates entered your pipeline, how many received a first response within 48 hours, and how many received an offer.
Step 2: Calculate drop-off at each stage. Most solar firms discover that a large share of qualified inbound candidates never receive a follow-up after initial contact.
Step 3: Run the same audit on your referral pipeline specifically. How many referrals were submitted in the last 90 days? How many received a status update within one week? How many referrers were told the final outcome?
Step 4: Pick the single biggest gap and fix it before the next issue of this newsletter. Fix one handoff. Then measure the impact.
