MERGERS
Omnicom Merges Golin and Ketchum Into a Single Entity
Image source: original photography for The Green Room. Wide tracking shot across an open-plan PR agency office at 8:15 AM, early morning light.
THE SHORT VERSION
In February 2026, Omnicom Public Relations Group announced it would merge Golin and Ketchum, two of its flagship PR agencies, into a combined entity. The restructuring is part of a broader holding company effort to consolidate agency brands, reduce operational overhead, and present a unified front to clients who increasingly question the value of paying multiple agency retainers across a single holding company portfolio.
THE DETAILS
• The merger was announced on February 12, 2026, and covered by PRovoke Media and marketech apac.
• The combined entity aims to reduce back-office costs, share technology infrastructure, and operate under a single P&amp;L.
• Golin and Ketchum together represent a significant portion of Omnicom's PR division, which reported approximately $1.5 billion in revenue for 2025.
• Industry analysts estimate the combined entity could reduce overhead by 15 to 20 percent within 24 months of the merger.
WHY IT MATTERS
This is not just another holding company reorganization. It signals that even the largest PR networks with decades of independent identity are no longer safe from consolidation pressure. For independent agency owners, the merger creates both threat and opportunity.

RETAINERS
Companies Are Quietly Replacing Agency Retainers With Direct Publishing Platforms
Image source: original photography for The Green Room. Tight shot of a PR director's workspace the night before a major pitch.
THE SHORT VERSION
A May 2026 report from citybiz documented a growing pattern: mid-market and enterprise companies are canceling traditional PR agency retainers and shifting those budgets to direct publishing platforms that bypass media gatekeepers entirely. This is not a replacement for crisis communications or strategic counsel, but it is eroding the media relations retainer that has been the financial backbone of many agencies for decades.
THE DETAILS
• The citybiz report identified dozens of companies in Q1 2026 alone that had either eliminated or materially reduced their PR agency retainers in favor of direct publishing platforms.
• The average retainer reduction was significant, with funds redirected to in-house content teams and publishing infrastructure.
• Companies are building owned audiences rather than renting access to reporters' inboxes, a structural shift in how organizations allocate communications budgets.
• The retainer model does not die all at once; it gets nibbled at the edges as companies reallocate portions of agency spend to direct publishing.
WHY IT MATTERS
Agencies that do not have a direct publishing offering, including content strategy, audience development, and measurement, are watching a revenue stream slowly migrate away from them. The agencies that survive will offer both: media relations for credibility and direct publishing for scale.

AGENCY GROWTH
Everyone Wants a Referral Channel. Almost Nobody Builds the Part That Makes It Real.
Image source: original photography for The Green Room. Two professionals at a sunlit cafe table, one gesturing with his phone.
THE SHORT VERSION
A detailed essay by a service business operator who reverse-engineered a client referral system that now generates over 20 percent of new business without paid acquisition or cold outreach. The piece is not about PR agencies specifically, but the mechanics transfer directly. Referral volume is a systems problem. You need a defined trigger moment, a near-zero-friction handoff mechanism, and a feedback loop.
THE DETAILS
• Define your trigger moment. In a PR agency, this might be the day a client gets a major placement or wins an award. That is when they are most likely to refer you, and most agencies never ask.
• Reduce the friction. 'Let me know if you know anyone who needs PR' is not an ask. 'Could you introduce me to the CMO at [specific company]? I will send you two lines you can forward' is an ask.
• Close the loop. When someone refers you, tell them what happened. Most referrers hear nothing and eventually stop sending people.
• The fix involves assigning ownership, building a structured follow-up sequence, and giving referrers visibility into outcomes.

INDUSTRY DATA
Cision's 2026 Industry Report Confirms AI Has Crossed From Toy to Tool Inside PR Agencies
Image source: original photography for The Green Room. Narrow hallway behind a conference venue, a door labeled PRESS.
THE SHORT VERSION
Cision released its 'Inside PR 2026' report on January 6, 2026. The report documents a decisive shift: AI adoption in PR workflows moved from experimental to operational in 2025, and by early 2026 more than 60 percent of agencies reported using AI tools daily for tasks including media monitoring, sentiment analysis, pitch drafting, and reporting.
THE DETAILS
• 62 percent of PR agencies reported daily AI tool usage in early 2026, up from 23 percent in early 2024 (Cision).
• Agencies in the top quartile of AI adoption reported an average account margin improvement of 8.3 percentage points year over year.
• AI adoption moved from experimental to operational across media monitoring, sentiment analysis, pitch drafting, and reporting workflows.
• The report flags a talent implication: junior staff roles historically filled by recent graduates doing media monitoring and clip reports are being partially automated.
WHY IT MATTERS
The Cision data confirms what many agency owners have felt: the AI conversation has moved past 'should we use it' to 'how fast can we integrate it without breaking client trust.' The margin advantage is the most important signal.

THE PLAYBOOK
ONE USEFUL MOVE
The Client Satisfaction Audit (Agency Edition)
Step 1: List every active client. Next to each name, write down the date of the last time someone at the partner or director level had a non-billable, non-deliverable conversation with them.
Step 2: Flag every client where that date is more than 60 days ago. These are your at-risk accounts.
Step 3: Schedule a 20-minute call with each flagged client this week. Ask three questions: 'What is working well?' 'What could be better?' 'What would make you a hero internally this quarter?'
Step 4: For the clients who tell you what would make them a hero, build a one-page scope addendum within 48 hours. Price it below your usual rate as a test. Track close rate.
