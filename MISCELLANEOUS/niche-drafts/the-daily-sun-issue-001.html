STATE MILESTONE
Utah Becomes First State Where Solar Is the Largest Single Power Source
Image source: original photography for The Daily Sun. Vast solar farm at dawn, rows of panels stretching toward the horizon under a gradient sky.
THE SHORT VERSION
In May 2026, Utah generated more electricity from solar than from any other individual fuel source, marking a first for any U.S. state. The Salt Lake Tribune reports that solar output surpassed natural gas, coal, and all other generation types during the month, driven by rapid utility-scale buildout and strong spring irradiance.
THE DETAILS
• Utah has added over 3 GW of solar capacity in the past three years, with the trend line still pointing upward.
• The milestone demonstrates that a historically fossil-dependent state can tip into solar-majority generation without grid instability.
• The achievement required transmission and storage investments that other states can replicate across the Mountain West, Southwest, and Great Plains.
• Other states are expected to cite Utah's May numbers in regulatory proceedings and renewable portfolio standard debates for the rest of 2026.
WHY IT MATTERS
If Utah can do it, the playbook is replicable. This is not a one-off anomaly. It is a proof point that solar-majority generation is achievable with existing technology and grid infrastructure, which changes the conversation in every state legislature and public utility commission considering renewable energy targets.

SEIA: Six Records Solar and Storage Crushed in the First Half of 2026
Image source: original photography for The Daily Sun. Solar engineer at a substation at golden hour, looking out toward transmission lines and solar arrays.
THE SHORT VERSION
The Solar Energy Industries Association published a midyear scorecard documenting six new records set by the U.S. solar and storage industry in the first half of 2026. The largest single quarter of utility-scale solar installations ever recorded occurred in H1 2026. Energy storage deployments also hit an all-time record, addressing the intermittency challenge that has historically capped solar's grid share.
THE DETAILS
• The largest single quarter of utility-scale solar installations ever recorded occurred in H1 2026.
• Energy storage deployments also hit an all-time record, addressing the intermittency challenge that has historically capped solar's grid share.
• Solar and storage together accounted for over 90 percent of new U.S. power generation capacity added in Q1 2026.
• Coal additions for new power generation capacity were zero during the period, with the remaining sliver split between wind and natural gas.
WHY IT MATTERS
The energy transition is not a future forecast. It is a present-tense infrastructure build. The firms that positioned for it -- developers, EPCs, financiers, manufacturers -- are now operating at scale. The laggards are reading about it.

TALENT INFRASTRUCTURE
Your Solar Firm Is Growing. Your Hiring Infrastructure Is Not Keeping Up.
Image source: original photography for The Daily Sun. Solar installation crew working on a residential rooftop at midday, bright sun, clear blue sky.
THE SHORT VERSION
Solar is scaling faster than any energy sector in U.S. history. Installations are breaking records. Funding is pouring in. But walk into any mid-sized solar developer or EPC and ask how they are handling recruiting, and the story is consistent: the referral program exists on paper, the careers page is live, and the recruiters are drowning. The fix is not a bigger recruiting budget. It is treating hiring infrastructure the same way you treat project delivery infrastructure.
THE DETAILS
• Referral programs at solar firms have no follow-up sequence. A project manager refers a skilled electrician and nobody follows up for two weeks, by which point the electrician took another offer.
• Candidate pipelines sit unmanaged while recruiters triage inbound applications, and the highest-quality referrals get treated the same as cold applicants.
• Offers go out late because nobody owns the timeline end to end, and the candidate accepts a competitor's offer that arrived three days earlier.
• The fix: defined workflows, assigned ownership, and real-time visibility -- the same rigor applied to project delivery, now applied to hiring.

FUNDING
Solar Corporate Funding Hits $16.9 Billion in H1 2026, Up 56 Percent
Image source: original photography for The Daily Sun. Inside a solar panel distribution warehouse, pallets of panels under high-bay industrial lighting.
THE SHORT VERSION
Global corporate funding for the solar sector reached $16.9 billion in the first half of 2026, a 56 percent increase over the same period last year, according to ET EnergyWorld. Venture capital, private equity, public market financing, and debt all contributed to the surge. Downstream project financing represented the largest share of the total.
THE DETAILS
• The $16.9 billion total represents a 56 percent year-over-year increase, an acceleration signal rather than incremental growth.
• Downstream project financing represented the largest share of the total, reflecting strong investor confidence in project-level returns.
• Venture capital and private equity flows into solar technology and development firms also increased, broadening the capital base beyond project finance.
• More capital means more projects, more demand for talent, equipment, and services across the entire solar value chain.
WHY IT MATTERS
The funding numbers confirm what the installation data already suggests: capital markets are betting heavily on solar. If you are anywhere in the solar value chain, from module manufacturing to installation crews to project finance, the addressable market just got meaningfully bigger.

THE PLAYBOOK
ONE USEFUL MOVE
The Solar Hiring Pipeline Audit (30 Minutes, One Spreadsheet)
Step 1: Pull the last 90 days of candidate data. Count how many candidates entered your pipeline, how many received a first response within 48 hours, and how many received an offer.
Step 2: Calculate drop-off at each stage. Most solar firms discover that a large share of qualified inbound candidates never receive a follow-up after initial contact.
Step 3: Run the same audit on your referral pipeline specifically. How many referrals were submitted in the last 90 days? How many received a status update within one week? How many referrers were told the final outcome?
Step 4: Pick the single biggest gap and fix it before the next issue of this newsletter. Fix one handoff. Then measure the impact.
