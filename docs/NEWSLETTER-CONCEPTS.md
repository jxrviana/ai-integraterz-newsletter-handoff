# Industry Newsletter Concept Board — DRAFT v1
*For Justin's review · niches pending confirmation · July 17, 2026*

## How to read this
One concept block per niche: names, positioning, the reader, the recurring beats an issue rotates through, sample subject lines in the house voice, and the AI Integraterz tier the audience most naturally routes to. The top-pick name is marked ★; everything here is swappable. Voice and structure follow the house style (reporter-not-seller, one idea taught well, single soft CTA) and the Gameday flagship/daily pattern already in production.

## 1. Recruiting & staffing agencies
**Names:** ★ The People Person · The Full Desk · Open Req · The Shortlist
**Positioning (1 line):** A weekly desk-side briefing for staffing and recruiting owners — more job orders, fewer fall-offs, a desk that runs without you.
**Reader:** Owner or managing partner of a small staffing/recruiting firm; lies awake over client concentration, recruiter ramp time, and deals that die after the offer.
**Recurring beats:**
- **The Desk** — business development: winning job orders, defending fees, firing bad clients
- **The Pipeline** — sourcing and screening craft, including where AI screening actually helps and where it embarrasses you
- **Ghost Stories** — fall-offs, ghosting, and the process fixes that prevent them
- **The Stack** — ATS and automation teardowns: what to automate in sourcing, scheduling, and follow-up (AI beat)
- **The Margin** — agency economics: splits, contract vs. perm mix, pricing structures
**Sample subject lines:**
- "The job order you should have turned down"
- "Why candidates ghost after the second interview"
- "A six-question screen that ends bad submittals"
**Route to revenue:** Tier 2 (Train + Support) — recruiting teams have recurring, repeatable workflows worth training on; heavier shops graduate to Tier 4 DFY.
**Cadence:** Weekly (Tuesday) — matches the weekly desk rhythm of job orders and submittals; daily engine only after the list warms, per the Gameday ramp.

## 2. Men's health (clinics / TRT / vitality)
**Names:** ★ The Alpha Mail · The Open Mail · The Male Room · Peak Practice
*(Note: the publishing house already runs a newsletter called "The Alpha" — worth a taste check on whether "The Alpha Mail" reads as family resemblance or internal confusion.)*
**Positioning (1 line):** A weekly operator's briefing for men's-health clinic owners — fill the schedule, keep patients on protocol, stay inside the compliance lines.
**Reader:** Owner or medical director of an independent men's health clinic; worries about ad compliance, no-shows, front-desk phones, and telehealth players undercutting the local model.
**Recurring beats:**
- **New Patients** — acquisition channels and offers that are working for clinics like his, taught plainly
- **The Front Desk** — phones, intake, no-shows: missed-call text-back, AI answering, and automated reminders (AI beat)
- **Stay on Protocol** — retention and follow-up systems that keep patients from drifting after month one
- **Compliance Corner** — ad-policy and claims guardrails, in plain English, no legalese
- **The Chart** — one practice metric explained per issue: show rate, patient LTV, reactivation
**Sample subject lines:**
- "Your front desk is leaking booked consults"
- "The no-show fix that isn't a deposit"
- "Why the first week after intake decides the year"
**Route to revenue:** Tier 4 (DFY) — clinic owners want outcomes, not homework, and this is the audience the Gameday client work already proves out; Tier 3 VA Integrator as the step-down.
**Cadence:** Weekly (Thursday) — mirrors the Gameday flagship spec that's already built for this exact audience; no reason to reinvent the send pattern.

## 3. Men's coaching
**Names:** ★ Man to Man · The Corner Man · The Full Roster · Second Wind
**Positioning (1 line):** A weekly read for men's coaches building full rosters and durable clients — without living on the content treadmill.
**Reader:** Solo or small-team coach for men (performance, mindset, fitness-adjacent); worries where next month's clients come from while delivering for this month's.
**Recurring beats:**
- **The Funnel** — what's actually filling rosters now: content-to-call pipelines, referrals, communities
- **The Close** — discovery-call craft, objections, and enrollment conversations that don't feel like sales
- **Keep Them** — retention, renewals, and tracking client results so the testimonial writes itself
- **The Back Office** — onboarding, check-ins, and content repurposing a coach can automate without losing the personal touch (AI beat)
- **The Positioning** — niche and offer sharpening; being the obvious choice for one kind of man
**Sample subject lines:**
- "The discovery-call question that ends bad fits early"
- "Your content isn't the problem. The offer is."
- "A renewal script for the month-five wobble"
**Route to revenue:** Tier 1 (Plan Session) — coaches are DIY-minded with smaller budgets; the natural graduation is Tier 3 (a VA running their follow-up and content system).
**Cadence:** Weekly — coaches are already drowning in content; one genuinely useful read a week beats noise, and it models the restraint we'd teach them.

## 4. MCA / business funding brokers
**Names:** ★ The Advance Copy · First Position · The Stip Sheet · The Funding Desk
**Positioning (1 line):** The weekly desk read for funding brokers — faster files, fatter renewal books, a calmer pipeline.
**Reader:** Broker or ISO shop owner; lives on speed, worries about lead cost and quality, stalled files, defaults, and funders changing appetite without warning.
**Recurring beats:**
- **Speed to Fund** — pipeline velocity from lead intake to funded, including instant-response and intake automation (AI beat)
- **The Stip Chase** — document collection and underwriting prep systems that stop files from dying in the folder
- **The Book** — renewals and portfolio management: where the durable margin actually lives
- **Funder Desk** — managing funder relationships and positioning deals so they clear
- **The Line** — the industry's regulatory and reputation weather, reported qualitatively, no doom
**Sample subject lines:**
- "The stip that stalls most files (and the fix)"
- "Renewals are the business. Here's the cadence."
- "First to respond usually funds the deal"
**Route to revenue:** Tier 4 (DFY) — speed-to-lead and follow-up automation run end-to-end is the obvious sell to a volume-driven, revenue-rich desk; Tier 2 for shops that want their closers trained on it.
**Cadence:** Weekly to start — this crowd's inbox is a war zone, so one high-signal read earns more trust than daily noise; ramp to 2×/week if engagement holds.

## 5. Marketing agencies
**Names:** ★ The Retainer · Scope Creep · The Deliverable · Above the Fold
**Positioning (1 line):** A margin-minded weekly for agency owners — keep the clients, kill the scope creep, price like you mean it.
**Reader:** Agency founder with a small team; worries about churn, capacity, and clients asking why AI can't do the deliverable cheaper.
**Recurring beats:**
- **Retainer Math** — pricing, packaging, and margin: what to productize and what to custom-quote
- **Scope Watch** — scope creep, client management, and the exact emails/scripts that reset boundaries
- **The Bench** — hiring, contractors, and capacity planning without bloating payroll
- **The New Stack** — AI inside agency delivery: what to automate, what stays human, and how to reprice when the hours drop (AI beat)
- **Client Kept** — retention, reporting that clients actually read, and renewal conversations
**Sample subject lines:**
- "A scope-creep email you can send today"
- "When the client asks, 'can't AI do this?'"
- "The retainer package that survives budget season"
**Route to revenue:** Tier 2 (Train + Support) — agency owners want to run the systems themselves (and quietly resell the capability); Tier 1 Plan Session as the low-friction entry.
**Cadence:** Weekly (Monday) — sets up the agency's week; owners plan capacity and client comms Monday morning.

## 6. AI agencies / AI automation agencies
**Names:** ★ Human in the Loop · Agents of Change · The Go-Live · The Workflow
*(Note: "human in the loop" is common industry vocabulary — used by at least one data-annotation firm and scattered podcasts — so it's distinctive as a masthead but not ownable as a phrase; flag for taste.)*
**Positioning (1 line):** The trade paper for AI-automation shop owners — what's booking, what's breaking, and what's actually worth building.
**Reader:** AAA founder a step past freelancing; worries about pipeline consistency, pricing builds that never end, and maintaining systems after the platform shifts underneath them.
**Recurring beats:**
- **The Pipeline** — what's booking calls for automation shops right now: offers, niches, proof formats
- **Ship Log** — teardown of one real client-automation pattern per issue, the house specialty (AI beat, natively)
- **The Maintenance Bill** — keeping deployed systems alive when models, APIs, and platforms change under you
- **Price It** — packaging and pricing: project vs. retainer vs. outcome structures
- **The Radar** — model and tool changes that matter to delivery, filtered hard for operator relevance
**Sample subject lines:**
- "Stop selling workflows. Sell the maintenance."
- "The client automation that breaks first (and why)"
- "A pricing structure for builds that don't end"
**Route to revenue:** Tier 2 (Train + Support) — peer-to-peer: production patterns, white-label support, and overflow capacity for shops that sell more than they can build.
**Cadence:** Weekly — the space moves daily but the reader is drowning in AI news; the value is the filter, not the volume.

## 7. HVAC companies
**Names:** ★ Warm Leads · The Cold Call · The Tune-Up · Full Blast
*(Note: "The Cold Call" is a great fit but shares its name with a well-known Harvard Business School podcast — kept as an alternate for that reason.)*
**Positioning (1 line):** A weekly briefing for HVAC owners on the money hiding in the phones, the board, and the shoulder season.
**Reader:** Owner-operator of a residential/commercial HVAC shop; worries about finding and keeping techs, seasonal cash swings, and after-hours calls going to whoever answers first.
**Recurring beats:**
- **Missed Calls, Missed Money** — phone coverage and after-hours capture: missed-call text-back, AI answering, booking flows (AI beat)
- **The Board** — dispatch and scheduling efficiency; getting one more job per truck per week the honest way
- **The Bench** — hiring, training, and keeping techs in a market where everyone's poaching
- **Shoulder Season** — smoothing the seasonal swing: maintenance agreements, memberships, off-peak offers
- **The Exit Watch** — valuations and the consolidation wave: what buyers look for, whether or not you ever sell
**Sample subject lines:**
- "After-hours calls are going to your competitor"
- "The maintenance-agreement pitch your techs will actually make"
- "What a missed call costs you in July"
**Route to revenue:** Tier 3 (VA Integrator) — owners are in trucks, not dashboards; someone running the missed-call and follow-up system for them is the natural fit, with Tier 4 DFY above it.
**Cadence:** Weekly, short — this reader skims on a phone between jobs; the Gameday daily-engine ramp can follow once the list is warm.

## 8. Solar companies
**Names:** ★ The Payback Period · The Bright Side · Full Sun · High Noon
*(Note: "The Bright Side" collides with the very large BRIGHT SIDE YouTube brand — kept as an alternate for that reason.)*
**Positioning (1 line):** A weekly operator's read for solar company owners — cheaper leads, cleaner installs, fewer cancels.
**Reader:** Owner or sales lead of a residential solar company; worries about lead cost, deals canceling before install, permitting drag, and financing partners changing terms.
**Recurring beats:**
- **The Lead Ledger** — acquisition channels and follow-up speed; automated speed-to-lead and setter workflows (AI beat)
- **Pipeline to Permit** — ops between the signed deal and the install: proposals, permitting, interconnection handoffs
- **The Cancel Rate** — keeping sold deals sold: expectation-setting, milestone comms, kitchen-table follow-through
- **The Crew** — install capacity, subcontracting, and scheduling around the backlog
- **The Forecast** — policy and financing weather, reported qualitatively and without panic
**Sample subject lines:**
- "Why sold deals cancel before install"
- "A proposal turnaround your setters can promise"
- "The follow-up window every solar lead deserves"
**Route to revenue:** Tier 4 (DFY) — high-ticket sales orgs with real ad spend want speed-to-lead and pipeline follow-up run for them; Tier 1 Plan Session as the hand-raiser entry point.
**Cadence:** Weekly — sales-heavy readers will tolerate more frequency later, but trust comes first; revisit 2×/week at the 90-day mark.
