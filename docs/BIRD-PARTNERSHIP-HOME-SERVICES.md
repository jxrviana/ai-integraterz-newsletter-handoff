## Email body (markdown)

Hi {{FIRST_NAME}} —

Think about the last three jobs you were genuinely glad to get.

Not the 11pm form fills. The ones where someone said, "Call these guys — they did mine, they're straight with you." You showed up half-sold. Nobody fought the estimate. They paid on time.

That job didn't come from an ad. It came from a handshake — yours, or somebody else's.

Here's what most owners miss: your next twenty customers already belong to someone you know. Not a stranger you have to buy on Google — a business that already sits next to your customer at the moment they need you.

Marketers have a name for it: the host-beneficiary relationship, which Jay Abraham popularized decades ago. Another business that has already earned your ideal customer's trust introduces you to them, and you make it worth their while (Source: Abraham.com; Deliberate Directions). The trust is already built. You're just borrowing it.

So here's a map you can draw on the back of an invoice — three kinds of partners, closest to furthest out.

**1. Who already has your customer the moment they need you.**
Realtors, insurance agents, property managers. A house closes, a claim gets filed, a rental turns over — and someone needs a trade they can trust that day. These people get asked "who do you use?" every week. Right now they're guessing. Be the name they give.

**2. Adjacent trades who see the problem before you do.**
The plumber is standing over a rusted-out water heater. The roofer is looking down at a fifteen-year-old condenser. The electrician sees the panel that won't carry the new system. They spot your next job first — and you spot theirs. Trade the leads both ways.

**3. Suppliers and manufacturers with programs already built.**
Your distributor and the brands you install often run co-op advertising and dealer-locator programs — shared marketing dollars, and homeowner leads routed to their listed contractors (Source: ACCA HVAC Blog). Most owners leave it on the table. Ask your rep what you qualify for.

The first move is a conversation, not a contract. Here's a message you can send today — copy it, swap the trade:

> "Hi [Name] — I run {{COMPANY_NAME}}; we handle [trade] here in [area]. You talk to homeowners who need someone reliable before I ever meet them, and I meet plenty who need what you do. I'd like to be the name you hand out — and send mine your way. Fifteen minutes this week to figure out how we make each other look good?"

Does it work? In Jobber's 2026 breakdown of HVAC referral programs, real shops run this as a system — one pays $30 for a service referral and $500 when it becomes an install; another rewards both sides of the introduction. In their comparison, referred leads closed at roughly three times the rate of paid ads, at a fraction of the cost (Source: Jobber). Warmer leads, cheaper, and they don't stop when you pause the ad spend.

The take: an ad rents you a stranger for one job. A partner sends you neighbors for years. One you shut off the month cash gets tight; the other compounds. Build three this quarter and you've got a lead source no competitor can outbid.

## Staging notes
- **Word count (body):** ~590 words — inside the 450–650 cold-blast target.
- **{{FIRST_NAME}} fallback:** set the merge default to `there` so a blank name renders "Hi there —". Never let it render "Hi ," or "Hi {{FIRST_NAME}} —". In Bird, configure the personalization variable's fallback/default value before send; run a seed test with the name field empty to confirm.
- **Placeholders to fill before send:** `{{FIRST_NAME}}` (+fallback), `{{SENDER_NAME}}`, `{{SENDER_TITLE}}`, `{{COMPANY_NAME}}`, `{{PHYSICAL_ADDRESS}}`, `{{UNSUBSCRIBE_LINK}}`, `{{PARTNERSHIPS_OFFER_LINE}}`, `{{RECRUITING_OFFER_LINE}}`. The in-script `[Name]`, `[trade]`, `[area]` brackets are meant to stay as brackets — they are the reader's copy-paste template, not merge fields. Do not point Bird's merge engine at them.
- **CTA routing:** reply-based, no links in the CTA. Set up inbox rules/tags so replies containing "PARTNER" vs "HIRE" route to the right follow-up. Confirm the send-from address can receive replies (not a no-reply mailbox) before launch.
- **Deliverability (cold blast):** body carries zero hyperlinks except the required unsubscribe — keep it that way; the reply CTA does the work. Sources are plain-text citations, not links, on purpose. Send from a warmed domain/IP, authenticate SPF/DKIM/DMARC, throttle volume, and scrub the list first. Subject + preview are paired to avoid a bait-and-switch open.
- **Compliance:** footer includes physical address, one-click unsubscribe, and an honest cold-outreach line (CAN-SPAM). Keep all three in every variant; suppress unsubscribes immediately.
- **Subject test:** rotate lines 1 and 3 as the A/B pair (both concrete, no hype). Line 5 is the punchiest fallback.
- **Formatting:** the markdown version bolds the three partner headers; the plain-text version numbers them `1) 2) 3)`. If Bird sends a single rich body, the markdown version is primary and the plain-text block is the multipart/alternative.
