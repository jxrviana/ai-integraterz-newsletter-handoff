### Story 1 — LEAD / DATA  · label `MARKET SHIFT`
- **Headline (blue link):** The Budget Is Quietly Moving From Ads to Partners
- **The Short Version:** The money that used to go to paid ads is shifting to partners and affiliates, and the platforms are publishing the receipts. US affiliate spend is forecast to hit $13.81 billion in 2026 &mdash; growing about twice as fast as ecommerce overall &mdash; while most B2B firms now expect their partner channel to out-earn last year.
- **The Details:**
  - US affiliate marketing spend is projected at **$13.81 billion in 2026, up 11.3%** &mdash; nearly double the growth rate of ecommerce as a whole ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-affiliate-marketing--how-ai-creators-reshaping-channel-2026)).
  - **Two-thirds of B2B companies expect partner revenue to beat last year by 30% or more** &mdash; a bet on ecosystems over ad spend ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-state-of-partner-ecosystems-2025/)).
  - The creator slice is climbing fast: Awin's creator-driven share rose from **15.9% to 19.5%** in a year ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-affiliate-marketing--how-ai-creators-reshaping-channel-2026)).
  - It's a forecast and an expectation, not booked revenue &mdash; the direction is clear; the exact size depends on who's counting.
- **Why It Matters:** Paid acquisition gets more expensive every quarter; a partner channel gets cheaper as it compounds. For an agency billing clients for growth, that's the difference between renting results and owning a source of them.
- Read more &rarr; https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-affiliate-marketing--how-ai-creators-reshaping-channel-2026

### Story 2 — THE PLAY  · label `THE PLAY`
- **Headline (blue link):** The Eight-Business Play That Costs Nothing
- **The Short Version:** Affiliate expert Amber Spears teaches a move she calls the Power of Eight: get eight non-competing businesses that all serve the same customer to commit, for one year, to promoting each other. No ad budget, no new product &mdash; just eight trusted businesses pointing their audiences at each other on purpose.
- **The Details:**
  - The rule is **non-competing but same-customer** &mdash; a wedding photographer, florist, venue, and planner all sell to the same bride ([Amber Spears](https://www.conquerlocal.com/podcast/partnering-for-profits-how-to-build-powerful-affiliate-relationships/)).
  - It runs on **"trust transfer"**: a referral works because the partner lends you credibility they already earned ([Spears](https://www.conquerlocal.com/podcast/partnering-for-profits-how-to-build-powerful-affiliate-relationships/)).
  - She credits the idea to health entrepreneurs **JJ Virgin and Dr. Mark Hyman**, who cross-promoted to each other's audiences.
  - Her contrarian rule on pay: **give a partner what a customer is worth to you anywhere else** &mdash; if a lead is worth $200, the partner who sends one should get near that.
- **Why It Matters:** Most owners already know eight businesses that share their customer. The reason it isn't a channel yet isn't strategy &mdash; it's that nobody built the tracking, the terms, or the follow-through.
- Read more &rarr; https://www.conquerlocal.com/podcast/partnering-for-profits-how-to-build-powerful-affiliate-relationships/

### Story 4 — CASE STUDY  · label `CASE STUDY`
- **Headline (blue link):** How One Newsletter Grew to 1.5 Million Readers on Referrals
- **The Short Version:** Morning Brew's co-founder has said the business newsletter went from about 100,000 to 1.5 million subscribers in roughly 18 months &mdash; and the engine was a referral program that rewarded existing readers for bringing in new ones. A referral loop, run deliberately, did what paid growth couldn't.
- **The Details:**
  - Readers earned tiered rewards for referring friends, each tracked with a unique link ([Tyler Denk](https://medium.com/the-mission/how-morning-brews-referral-program-built-an-audience-of-1-5-million-subscribers-3315482c1aa5)).
  - The jump &mdash; **roughly 100K to 1.5M in about 18 months** &mdash; came during the program's run ([Denk](https://medium.com/the-mission/how-morning-brews-referral-program-built-an-audience-of-1-5-million-subscribers-3315482c1aa5)).
  - A large share of new subscribers was credited to referrals, though that figure is the company's own and reported after the fact.
  - It's one winner, not a promise &mdash; plenty of referral programs go nowhere; this one had a product people wanted to share.
- **Why It Matters:** The mechanism isn't exotic. It's a tracked reason for the people who already like you to bring the next person &mdash; which is exactly what an affiliate program is, pointed at your customers instead of at strangers.
- Read more &rarr; https://medium.com/the-mission/how-morning-brews-referral-program-built-an-audience-of-1-5-million-subscribers-3315482c1aa5

### Utility give — the free thing  ·  heading: **The Partner Pitch**
> Pure give, on-theme, forwardable. The this-week first step of the Power of Eight (Story 2), made copyable.

**The play (this week):** Pull your last 20 clients. For each, list the non-competing businesses they already pay &mdash; their web person, their bookkeeper, their agency. The names that repeat are your first partners. Then send three of them this:

> *"Hi [Name] &mdash; your clients and mine are basically the same people, just at different moments. What if we made referrals official: you send me the ones who need [your thing], I send you the ones who need yours, and we track both sides so it's fair and it actually pays out. 15 minutes this week to map it?"*

**Why it works:** it leads with their upside, it's specific, and "tracked" removes the trust problem that kills most handshake deals.
