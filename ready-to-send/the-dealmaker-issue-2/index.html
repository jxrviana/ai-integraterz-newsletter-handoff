THE DEALMAKER
 
 
Turning relationships into revenue.
No. 002 · August 2026 · Jay Bell
The best salesperson you will ever hire only gets paid when you do.

They already know how to explain your value. They already have the trust of the person you want to reach. And they do not need a salary, a desk, or another software subscription. They need a reason to introduce you.

That salesperson is a good-fit customer, advisor, vendor, or peer in your existing network.
The Reframe
You already run referrals by accident.

Alignable surveyed more than 7,500 small-business owners. 85% said word of mouth was their number-one source of new customers. Nielsen found that 88% of people trust recommendations from people they know more than any other form of advertising.

The demand is already there. The missing piece is usually the operating system: who should refer, what should they say, where should the lead go, and how does everyone know what happened next?

Why It Pays
Why the economics are different.

A referred customer is not automatically better. But the research gives you a reason to take the channel seriously. Schmitt, Skiera, and Van den Bulte’s Journal of Marketing study found referred customers carried 16% to 25% higher lifetime value.

The market is moving in the same direction. eMarketer estimates US affiliate-marketing spend at about $13.81 billion in 2026, up roughly 11.3%. Forrester reports that about 67% of B2B companies expect partner revenue to grow 30% or more.

Amber Spears puts the balance plainly: “If you’re running on 100% paid media, you’re in trouble. And if you’re running on 100% affiliate, you’re in trouble.” The smart play is a channel mix where paid acquisition and partner-led acquisition strengthen each other.
What We Promote
Not every offer earns a partner.

We promote offers where the economics can support a partner: a clear customer result, a sensible commission, and enough margin to make the introduction worth repeating. The affiliate program is built around your business, your offer, and the partners who already serve the same buyer.
One Real Play
Your do-it-Monday play.
This Monday: Write down 10 businesses or professionals who already speak to your ideal customer. Circle the three whose customers feel the problem you solve most urgently. Then send one of them this question: “When one of your customers asks about [the problem you solve], what do you currently recommend?”

That answer tells you whether you have a potential partner, a competitor, or a gap worth filling. Start there.
How It Scales
It can scale — but don’t copy the headline.

Tesla grew for years with little traditional advertising. Airbnb has reported referral programs lifting bookings by about 25% in some markets. AG1 grew into an estimated $1.2 billion brand with a large commission-based creator channel. Those are possible examples, not promises or typical outcomes.

The transferable lesson is smaller: make the recommendation easy, make the reward clear, and build the follow-up so the channel does not depend on memory.
