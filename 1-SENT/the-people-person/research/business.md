## ANCHOR STATS (headline-strength, use these first)

**1. The US staffing market is bottoming near $180B in 2026, down from a $243.9B peak in 2022.**
Even at ~$180B it sits below the pre-pandemic 2019 level of $185.5B. This is the "reset, not recovery" story. *Very likely accurate as SIA's current view (High confidence the figure is SIA's; Moderate confidence on the exact dollar amount, since it is a forecast read via secondary).*
Source: SIA US Staffing Industry Forecast, March 2026 update, via qxglobalgroup (SIA origin, A; access B) — https://qxglobalgroup.com/rs/us/blog/us-staffing-market-size-forecast-180b

**2. 2025 was the trough, and the turn has started: US temp/contract staffing sales fell 8.5% to $113.5B in 2025, but Q4 rose and temp jobs ticked up again in mid-2026.**
Q4 2025 sales were $29.9B (+2.6% vs Q3); average weekly temp/contract headcount rose 65,000 quarter-over-quarter; the year-over-year decline narrowed to -6.1% from -7.5%. Temp-help employment then added 9,300 jobs in June 2026 even as overall hiring cooled. *Likely the industry has passed its low point (Moderate confidence: one more quarter of ASA data would confirm).*
Source: ASA Staffing Employment & Sales Survey, published 2026-03-30 (A, directly fetched) — https://americanstaffing.net/posts/2026/03/30/employment-and-sales-rebound-in-q4/

**3. Tech talent stays scarce even in a soft labor market: tech-occupation unemployment was 2.9% in June 2026 (3.1% in May) versus ~4.2% nationally.**
Net US tech employment (~9.6M in 2025) is projected to grow ~1.9% in 2026 to ~9.8M. This is the demand engine for IT/tech recruiting desks. *Very likely accurate (High confidence: CompTIA data corroborated across two monthly releases and Dice).*
Source: CompTIA Tech Jobs Report analysis, June 2026, via Dice (B, fetched) — https://www.dice.com/career-advice/comptia-tech-jobs-report---june-2026 ; CompTIA State of the Tech Workforce 2026 (B) — https://www.comptia.org/en-us/resources/research/state-of-the-tech-workforce-2026/

**4. Staffing is a churn machine: the industry ran a 376% annual turnover rate in 2025.**
The temporary/contract workforce effectively turns over almost four times a year (down from 416% in 2024). Vivid utility-segment stat that explains why owners live and die by pipeline. *Almost certain as reported (High confidence: ASA primary survey).*
Source: ASA Staffing Employment & Sales Survey, 2026-03-30 (A, directly fetched) — https://americanstaffing.net/posts/2026/03/30/employment-and-sales-rebound-in-q4/

*Strong alternates if you want a different fourth: the IT-staffing $27.2B figure (#8 below) or the recruiter-fee economics (#9).*

## THE FULL DATA SET (10 points)

**1. US staffing market ~ $180.2B in 2026, +1% (SIA, March 2026 update).**
Context: SIA revised 2026 down from its earlier $183.3B call (September 2025 update) as conditions stayed soft; it had the US at $184B in 2024. 2022 peak = $243.9B; 2019 pre-pandemic = $185.5B.
Source (A origin / B access): https://qxglobalgroup.com/rs/us/blog/us-staffing-market-size-forecast-180b · gated SIA original: https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/us-staffing-industry-forecast-march-2026-update

**2. Global staffing market ~ $619-620B in 2024, roughly flat in 2025 (SIA).**
Context: The US is the world's largest staffing market at $184B (2024), followed by Japan ($67.9B) and the UK ($50.8B); those three are about 49% of the global total. SIA downgraded its global forecast on tariff and macro uncertainty.
Source (A origin / B access): https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/sia-downgrades-global-staffing-market-forecasts · https://qxglobalgroup.com/rs/us/blog/global-staffing-market-trends/

**3. US temp/contract staffing sales fell to $113.5B in 2025, down 8.5% year over year (ASA).**
Context: ASA's survey-based measure of temporary and contract staffing sales. Note this is a narrower definition than SIA's ~$180B total US staffing (see "Definitions" below); the two are not directly comparable.
Source (A, directly fetched): https://americanstaffing.net/posts/2026/03/30/employment-and-sales-rebound-in-q4/

**4. Q4 2025 staffing sales rebounded to $29.9B, +2.6% quarter over quarter; weekly headcount up 65,000 (ASA).**
Context: First clear sequential improvement after a long decline; year-over-year drop narrowed to -6.1% (from -7.5% in Q3). ASA employed an average of ~2 million temp/contract workers per week in Q4.
Source (A, directly fetched): https://americanstaffing.net/posts/2026/03/30/employment-and-sales-rebound-in-q4/

**5. Staffing penetration rate 1.57% of total US nonfarm payrolls; ~2.5 million temp-help jobs (June 2026, BLS).**
Context: Temp-help employment is a classic leading indicator of overall hiring. It rose +9,300 in June 2026 even as total payroll gains slowed to ~57,000, i.e., temp turned up before the broader market. Penetration held at 1.56-1.57% across March-June 2026.
Source (A origin, BLS June 2026 Employment Situation, via corroborating secondaries): https://www.roberthalf.com/us/en/insights/research/june-2026-jobs-report-employers-add-57000-jobs · https://madisonresources.com/june-2026-jobs-report-broader-job-creation-cools-temp-stays-hot/ · BLS primary (gated to fetcher): https://www.bls.gov/news.release/pdf/empsit.pdf

**6. Industry turnover rate 376% in 2025, down from 416% in 2024 (ASA).**
Context: The temp/contract workforce turns over nearly four times a year. Explains the constant redeploy-or-die pressure on staffing desks.
Source (A, directly fetched): https://americanstaffing.net/posts/2026/03/30/employment-and-sales-rebound-in-q4/

**7. Recruiter placement fees: contingency 15-25% of first-year salary (20% most common); retained 25-35% of total comp, paid in thirds.**
Context: Retained search bills one-third at engagement, one-third at shortlist, one-third at placement, with typical engagement minimums around $80k-$100k for executive roles. Slow-moving craft standard, consistent across many recruiting sources; the 20% contingency benchmark is attributed to SHRM.
Source (C, trade/vendor, corroborated; fetched): https://www.pin.com/blog/recruitment-agency-commission-structures/ · https://www.recruiterslineup.com/contingency-recruiting-fee-structure/

**8. The 57 largest US IT-staffing firms generated $27.2B in 2025, down 4.4%; segment forecast to return to +1% growth in 2026 and 2027 (SIA).**
Context: TEKsystems (Allegis) led at $3.92B, then Insight Global at $3.28B; the top five hold ~29% share. Ends three straight years of IT-staffing revenue decline.
Source (A origin / B access): https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/it-staffing-report/largest-us-it-staffing-firms-generate-27.2b-in-revenue · corroboration (ranked-firm press releases): https://www.inspyrsolutions.com/2026-largest-it-staffing-firms/

**9. Temporary-staffing gross margin typically ~20-25% (range 14-41%); net margin ~8-15% after burden and overhead.**
Context: SIA's Gross Margin and Bill Rate Trends report is the origin; bill-rate markups over pay average roughly 35-41%. Useful for a "know your real margin" utility segment.
Source (SIA origin B / secondary C access): SIA report (gated) https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/gross-margin-and-bill-rate-trends-2025-update · secondary https://altline.sobanco.com/staffing-agency-markup-rates/

**10. Average time-to-fill ~44-45 days in 2025; IT/technical roles run longer at ~35-70 days (SHRM benchmarking, via secondary).**
Context: SHRM's 2025 recruiting benchmarking is the cited origin; time-to-fill varies widely by role (logistics/warehouse under ~30 days; finance and IT the slowest). Treat as a directional benchmark, not a precise figure.
Source (SHRM origin B / secondary C access): https://www.theresource.com/2025/10/27/time-to-fill-by-industry/

## DEFINITIONS (why ASA's ~$113B and SIA's ~$180B are both right)

ASA's $113.5B (2025) measures **temporary and contract staffing sales** from its member survey. SIA's ~$180B is **total US staffing revenue**, which adds place-and-search (direct hire) and retained search and uses a broader temporary-staffing definition. Do not subtract one from the other to "derive" the perm segment; the definitions differ and the result would be wrong.

## GAPS — what was NOT verified, and what would change the read

- **Clean segment dollar split (temp vs direct-hire vs retained vs RPO).** SIA holds the exact breakdown behind a paywall (403 to the fetcher), and ASA vs SIA definitions differ, so I did not derive a split. Flagged rather than guessed. `[OWNER: SIA member login would give the exact temp/direct-hire/retained split.]`
- **RPO market size is vendor-grade and unreliable.** Public figures (global ~$10.9B in 2024, US ~$4.8B in 2025) come from market.us, a low-grade research vendor whose forecasts (20%+ CAGR to 2034) are prediction-laundering. Everest Group is the real RPO authority but its numbers are gated. Do not headline an RPO dollar figure. Directional only: https://market.us/report/recruitment-process-outsourcing-market/ (C, vendor).
- **Executive-search market dollar size: dropped on purpose.** Five research mills gave US figures from $6.7B to $10.8B and global from $58B to $64B, none tracing to a credible primary (AESC not accessible). Too polluted to use. The retained-search *fee structure* (point 7) is solid; the market *size* is not.
- **SIA figures are forecasts accessed via secondary,** because staffingindustry.com blocks the fetcher. The ~$180B, $184B, $27.2B, and global $619B numbers all originate with SIA (A-grade issuer) but were read through quotes/extracts. Treat exact dollars as ±a few percent.
- **BLS temp-help level** was read through secondaries citing the June 2026 Employment Situation (all trace to the same A-grade BLS release); the direct BLS page and FRED both 403'd the fetcher.
- **Time-to-fill and gross-margin** exact numbers trace to SHRM and SIA originals read via secondary; the primaries are paywalled.
- **Not searched:** perm/direct-hire placement *volume* (counts), margin trends by specialty, and staffing-firm M&A/valuation multiples. Available on request.
- **What would move the conclusions:** a fresh ASA Q1 2026 release (due mid-2026) would confirm or break the "bottom is in" narrative in anchors 2 and 5; a direct SIA report pull would firm up the exact market-size dollars now carried at Moderate confidence.
