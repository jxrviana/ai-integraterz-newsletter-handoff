## TOP 5 PICKS (ranked)

1. **AI is thinning the entry-level roles recruiters place** — Stanford payroll data shows a 13% (now ~16%) relative employment drop for workers aged 22-25 in the most AI-exposed jobs, while older workers held steady or grew. The most credible, most discussed labor story of the year, and it hits the exact roles perm desks fill. *(Story 5)*
2. **LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant AI agent went global** — the platform recruiters pay for now automates sourcing and outreach itself, with LinkedIn claiming adopters review 62% fewer profiles and save 4+ hours per role. Both a tool to adopt and a margin threat to junior-heavy desks. *(Story 3)*
3. **Staffing returns to growth in 2026, but barely** — SIA forecasts +1% to $180.2B after three straight down years (-14%, -12%, -3%). The single most important planning number for an owner: the tailwind is thin, so 2026 is won on share, not market. *(Story 1)*
4. **Indeed and Glassdoor cut 1,300 jobs and named AI as the reason** — the job boards themselves are betting humans-out, AI-in. A vivid signal of where candidate sourcing and job-board ROI are heading. *(Story 4)*
5. **Pay-transparency mandates now cover ~17 states, and enforcement has started** — Massachusetts and New Jersey are auditing job postings in 2026. Immediately actionable: recruiters must post ranges, coach clients, and can turn comp benchmarking into a BD wedge. *(Story 8)*

Runners-up worth a card: Colorado gutting its AI hiring law (7), Illinois + California AI-hiring rules now in force (6), the DOL dropping the federal contractor rule (9), and the Kelly Services control sale (10).

## THE CANDIDATE STORIES

### 1. US staffing returns to growth in 2026, but the tailwind is thin
**Short version:** Staffing Industry Analysts forecasts the US staffing market will grow about 1% in 2026 to $180.2 billion, its first rise after three consecutive years of decline, with another ~2% pencilled in for 2027.

**Details:**
- SIA's March 2026 forecast projects +1% in 2026 to **$180.2 billion** and +2% in 2027, reversing declines of roughly -14% (2023), -12% (2024) and -3% (2025). [staffingindustry.com](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/us-staffing-to-see-modest-uptick-in-revenue)
- Even at $180B the market sits below its ~$243.9B 2022 peak; the forecast was delivered by SIA President Ursula Williams and research director John Nurthen at the Executive Forum in Austin on March 24, 2026. [staffingindustry.com](https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/us-staffing-industry-forecast-march-2026-update)
- Segments diverge: SIA has healthcare/locum tenens among the strongest (locum ~+5% in both 2026 and 2027) and IT staffing roughly flat-to-+1%, so the "growth" is uneven by desk. [staffingindustry.com](https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/us-staffing-industry-forecast-march-2026-update)

**Why it matters to a recruiting-firm owner:** a 1% market means you grow by taking share, not by riding the cycle; budget and headcount plans built on a "recovery" assumption are too optimistic.

**Primary source + grade:** SIA (Staffing Industry Analysts). **Grade A** (SIA data is the industry standard; the 2026/2027 figures are a *forecast* / model output, so treat the forward numbers as a projection). **Confidence: High** on the direction and the historical declines; the forward number is a projection, so **likely** rather than certain. *Note: 2022 "peak" figures vary by SIA vintage and market definition ($243.9B in the current series vs $224.7B in an older temp-only cut); use the March 2026 series for consistency.*

### 2. The labor market is "low-hire, low-fire": openings flat, quits near a multi-year low
**Short version:** The most recent JOLTS report shows job openings stuck at 7.6 million and the quits rate at 1.9%, meaning workers are staying put and employers are neither hiring nor firing much.

**Details:**
- May 2026 JOLTS (released June 30, 2026): job openings unchanged at **7.6 million**, hires unchanged at **5.2 million**, quits little changed at **3.1 million**, layoffs/discharges unchanged at **1.7 million**. [bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm)
- The **quits rate held at 1.9%** and has sat at or below 2% for nearly a year, well under the ~3% Great Resignation peak of early 2022, which signals low worker confidence to change jobs. [hiringlab.org (Indeed)](https://www.hiringlab.org/2026/06/30/may-2026-jolts-report-more-of-the-same/)
- Openings came in above the 7.3 million economists expected, so the market is soft but not collapsing; the June 2026 report is due August 4, 2026. [investinglive.com](https://investinglive.com/news/jolts-job-openings-7594m-vs-7300m-estimate-20260630/)

**Why it matters to a recruiting-firm owner:** low quits means low churn, which means fewer backfill reqs and slower perm pipelines; the openings that exist are harder-to-fill and higher-value, favoring firms that sell speed and access over volume.

**Primary source + grade:** BLS JOLTS. **Grade A** (official statistic), independently corroborated by Indeed Hiring Lab, Roth Staffing and Haver Analytics. **Confidence: High.** *Verification note: BLS pages blocked direct fetch (HTTP 403); figures were confirmed across the BLS release text surfaced in search plus three independent secondary reproductions that agree exactly.*

### 3. LinkedIn's "Hiring Assistant" AI agent went globally available
**Short version:** LinkedIn's first AI agent for recruiters, which sources candidates and drafts outreach on its own, moved from a small charter group to worldwide availability in late 2025, with more autonomous features added in early 2026.

**Details:**
- Announced September 3, 2025; globally available in English by end of September 2025 after a limited launch to charter customers in October 2024. [news.linkedin.com](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/hiring-assistant-globally-available)
- LinkedIn's own figures for early adopters: **4+ hours saved per role, 62% fewer profiles reviewed, and 69% higher InMail acceptance**; named users include Microsoft, Chewy, Expedia Group, Siemens, Wipro and AMD. [news.linkedin.com](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/hiring-assistant-globally-available)
- A February 2026 update added AI Follow-Ups, AI Applicant Targeting and Microsoft Teams collaboration, pushing the agent deeper into the sourcing workflow. [influencermarketinghub.com](https://influencermarketinghub.com/linkedin-ai-hiring-assistant/)

**Why it matters to a recruiting-firm owner:** the tool your researchers rely on now does first-pass sourcing and outreach itself; that is leverage for lean desks and, at the same time, a direct threat to the junior-recruiter/researcher headcount your margins may depend on.

**Primary source + grade:** LinkedIn press release. **Grade A** for the product and availability facts; the performance percentages are **vendor self-reported (Grade C)** and are LinkedIn's own charter-customer claims, not independently audited. **Confidence: High** on the launch; **Low-Moderate** on the productivity stats given the source's incentive.

**Details:**

**Primary source + grade:** TechCrunch reporting on the company memo. **Grade B**, corroborated by CBS News, HR Dive and Reuters (all tracing to Recruit Holdings' announcement, so treat the number and quote as one upstream source widely confirmed). **Confidence: High.**

### 5. AI is hitting the entry-level roles recruiters place hardest
**Short version:** A Stanford payroll study found early-career workers (22-25) in the most AI-exposed occupations saw a 13% relative decline in employment since late 2022, deepening to about 16% in later data, while older workers in the same jobs held steady or grew. Causation is still debated.

**Details:**
- Stanford Digital Economy Lab, "Canaries in the Coal Mine" (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen), published August 26, 2025 using ADP payroll microdata: a **13% relative employment decline for ages 22-25 in the most AI-exposed roles** (software, customer service, accounting), later updated to **~16% through October 2025**. [digitaleconomy.stanford.edu](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/)
- Over the same window, **workers 30 and older in high-AI-exposure categories grew 6-12%**, and the authors ruled out remote-work and education confounds. [time.com](https://time.com/7312205/ai-jobs-stanford/)
- The finding is contested: some analysts argue AI only "adds marginal pressure from late 2024," with a broader post-2022 hiring slowdown as the main driver. [forbes.com](https://www.forbes.com/sites/hessiejones/2025/11/24/ai-is-not-killing-entry-level-jobs/)

**Why it matters to a recruiting-firm owner:** junior developer, analyst and support roles are exactly what many contingent and perm desks fill; if that layer keeps shrinking, so does req volume there, and the "grow your own / hire-and-train" pitch to clients gets harder to sell.

**Primary source + grade:** Stanford Digital Economy Lab study. **Grade B** (named academic primary), corroborated by TIME, Fortune and CBS. **Confidence: Moderate** on the AI-causation interpretation (the authors say "consistent with," not "caused by," and reputable dissent exists); **High** that early-career hiring in these fields has contracted.

### 6. Illinois and California AI-hiring rules are now in force
**Short version:** Two of the largest states now regulate AI in hiring: Illinois' amended Human Rights Act took effect January 1, 2026, and California's automated-decision-system rules took effect October 1, 2025. Both make employers (and their staffing agents) liable for discriminatory AI outcomes.

**Details:**
- **Illinois HB 3773** (effective Jan 1, 2026) bars AI that produces a discriminatory effect in hiring and other employment decisions, requires notice to applicants when AI is used, and lets the Illinois Dept. of Human Rights levy civil penalties up to $5,000 per violation. [natlawreview.com](https://natlawreview.com/article/illinois-anti-discrimination-law-address-ai-goes-effect-1-january-2026)
- **California's Civil Rights Council rules** (final June 30, 2025; effective Oct 1, 2025) make an automated-decision system unlawful if it causes disparate impact, require four-year retention of ADS records, and warn that some AI assessments may be illegal disability inquiries. [mayerbrown.com](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/california-adopts-new-employment-ai-regulations-effective-october-1-2025)
- California's rules apply to "artificial intelligence, machine-learning, algorithms, statistics and/or other data processing" used in employment decisions, and reach agents acting for the employer. [jacksonlewis.com](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/californias-new-ai-regulations-take-effect-oct-1-heres-your-compliance-checklist)

**Why it matters to a recruiting-firm owner:** if you use AI sourcing or screening for Illinois or California roles, you likely have notice duties, record-keeping duties, and exposure for biased outputs; "the vendor's algorithm did it" is not a defense.

**Primary source + grade:** Named law-firm analyses (Mayer Brown, Jackson Lewis, Nat. Law Review) plus the statutes/regulations themselves. **Grade B**, well-corroborated. **Confidence: High** that both are in force; enforcement intensity is still developing (**Moderate** on how aggressively regulators will act in year one).

### 7. Colorado gutted and delayed its landmark AI hiring law
**Short version:** Colorado's first-in-the-nation AI Act, which would have imposed broad duties on employers using AI in hiring, was repealed and rewritten in May 2026 into a narrower regime and pushed to January 1, 2027, after two separate delays.

**Details:**
- The original Act was first delayed from February 1 to June 30, 2026 (SB 25B-004, signed August 2025), then repealed and replaced by SB 26-189, signed May 14, 2026, which pushed the effective date to **January 1, 2027**. [clarkhill.com](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/colorados-ai-law-delayed-until-june-2026-what-the-latest-setback-means-for-businesses/)
- SB 26-189 scrapped the broad "high-risk AI system" framework for a narrower focus on automated decision-making in consequential decisions and substantially reduced employer obligations. [littler.com](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/colorado-amends-its-artificial-intelligence-law-substantially-reducing)
- The slimmed law keeps three core duties when it lands: pre-use notice, an adverse-action process, and record retention. [theargroup.com](https://theargroup.com/blog/colorados-ai-act-what-employers-need-to-know-before-june-30-2026/)

**Why it matters to a recruiting-firm owner:** the AI-compliance goalposts keep moving; Colorado bought firms time, but the direction of travel (notice, adverse-action, records) is the same across states, so build the process once rather than chase each bill.

**Primary source + grade:** Littler and Clark Hill legal analyses (plus Proskauer, Hunton, Seyfarth). **Grade B**, heavily corroborated. **Confidence: High** on the current status; given two rewrites already, further change before 2027 is **likely**.

### 8. Pay-transparency mandates now cover most US employers, and enforcement has begun
**Short version:** A wave of state salary-range laws means roughly 17 states now require some form of pay disclosure, with several new mandates landing in 2025-2026 and the first real audits and penalties now being issued.

**Details:**
- New or expanded mandates: Illinois and Minnesota (Jan 2025), New Jersey (June 2025), Vermont (July 2025), Massachusetts (Oct 2025), Maine (Jan 2026), plus a refined California law effective Jan 1, 2026. [jacksonlewis.com](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/navigating-2026-pay-transparency-laws-and-employer-obligations)
- By one industry count, **17 states** now require salary information and roughly **65% of US employers** operate under some pay-transparency mandate (secondary estimate; state counts vary by how "posting" rules are defined). [netchex.com](https://netchex.com/blog/pay-transparency-laws-in-2026-what-multi-state-employers-need-to-change-in-job-postings/)
- **Massachusetts and New Jersey have moved to active enforcement** in 2026, issuing audits, warnings and penalties for non-compliant postings; a role performable from a covered state triggers that state's rule regardless of HQ location. [hunton.com](https://www.hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/several-states-enact-pay-transparency-laws-what-employers-need-to-know-in-2026)

**Why it matters to a recruiting-firm owner:** you and your clients must post good-faith ranges or risk penalties, remote roles pull in the strictest state's law, and comp-benchmarking is now a legitimate paid-advisory wedge to open client conversations.

**Primary source + grade:** Jackson Lewis and Hunton legal trackers. **Grade B** for the state-by-state effective dates and enforcement; the **17-states / 65% aggregates are secondary (Grade C)** and labeled as estimates because counts differ by methodology. **Confidence: High** on the specific state laws; **Moderate** on the round aggregate figures.

### 9. The federal government dropped the Biden-era independent-contractor rule
**Short version:** In May 2025 the US Department of Labor stopped enforcing the 2024 independent-contractor rule and reverted to older, more employer-friendly guidance, then moved in 2026 to formally rescind and replace it, though state tests still bind.

**Details:**
- On May 1, 2025 the DOL announced its Wage and Hour Division will **no longer enforce the 2024 independent-contractor rule**, reverting to 2008 Fact Sheet #13 and a 2019 opinion letter for classification. [dol.gov](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/whd/whd20250501)
- In early 2026 the DOL proposed a new rule to formally rescind and replace the 2024 regulation. [mayerbrown.com](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/dol-proposes-new-independent-contractor-rule-to-replace-biden-era-regulation)
- Two caveats: the 2024 rule still governs **private lawsuits**, and stricter state tests (for example California's ABC test) still apply, so exposure is uneven by geography. [natlawreview.com](https://natlawreview.com/article/us-department-labor-will-no-longer-enforce-independent-contractor-rule)

**Why it matters to a recruiting-firm owner:** firms that run 1099, IC, or gig-style models get federal breathing room, but the real classification risk sits at the state level and in private litigation, so do not read this as an all-clear.

**Primary source + grade:** US DOL press release. **Grade A** (government primary), corroborated by Mayer Brown and Nat. Law Review. **Confidence: High** on the enforcement change; the final replacement rule's shape is still open (**Moderate**).

## READING NOTE — keep the AI framing honest

The evidence supports "AI is reshaping specific segments and tasks," not "AI is destroying recruiting." The same Stanford authors call the overall labor impact modest outside the most-exposed entry-level roles; Forbes and others dispute even that; and SIA still shows healthcare and IT staffing growing. The strongest, most defensible angle for owners is reallocation and productivity pressure (which desks shrink, which tasks get automated, which firms pull ahead), not a doom narrative. Avoid any copy that implies recruiting is being wiped out; the data does not say that.
