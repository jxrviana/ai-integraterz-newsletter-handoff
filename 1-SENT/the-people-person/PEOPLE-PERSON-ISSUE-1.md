**MARKET**

**[Clients, Not Candidates, Are The Thing You're Short Of Now](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/us-staffing-to-see-modest-uptick-in-revenue)**

[IMAGE BRIEF — s1. Job: signal the market/economy story without clichés. A tight, moody overhead macro of one corner of an empty marble boardroom table, a single glass of water catching cold north-facing light, deep shadow, shallow depth of field, nothing else in frame, no text or lettering. Cold light, minimal. [SOLO MODE: visual brief sketch]]

**The Short Version:** Staffing Industry Analysts forecasts the US staffing market will grow about 1% in 2026 to $180.2 billion — its first rise after three straight down years. But even at $180B the market sits well below its 2022 peak, and owners now name winning clients, not finding candidates, as their number-one problem. The scarce resource flipped.

**The Details:**

- SIA projects +1% in 2026 to **$180.2 billion**, reversing declines of roughly -14% (2023), -12% (2024) and -3% (2025); another ~2% is penciled in for 2027. [staffingindustry.com](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/us-staffing-to-see-modest-uptick-in-revenue)
- Even at $180B the market sits below its **$243.9B 2022 peak** — and below even 2019's pre-pandemic $185.5B. This is a reset, not a recovery. [qxglobalgroup.com](https://qxglobalgroup.com/rs/us/blog/us-staffing-market-size-forecast-180b)
- Owners now rank **finding new clients as the top challenge — 23% of firms in 2025, up from 16% in 2024** — while "shortage of candidates" fell to 12% from 17%. [staffinghub.com](https://staffinghub.com/state-of-staffing/the-1-challenge-staffing-firms-face-in-2025-and-how-to-solve-it/)
- The turn is real but slow: Q4 2025 temp/contract sales rose to **$29.9B (+2.6% quarter over quarter)** and temp-help employment added 9,300 jobs in June 2026 even as broader hiring cooled. [americanstaffing.net](https://americanstaffing.net/posts/2026/03/30/employment-and-sales-rebound-in-q4/)

**Why It Matters:** A 1% market means you grow by taking share, not by riding a recovery — and the share is won on the demand side now. The scarce resource is a client ready to sign a req, not a candidate to fill one. Any 2026 plan built on a "rebound" is too optimistic. The firms that pull ahead are the ones with a repeatable way to open new client conversations, not the ones sourcing hardest.

**PLATFORMS**

**[LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant Now Sources Candidates On Its Own](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/hiring-assistant-globally-available)**

[IMAGE BRIEF — s2. Job: signal AI/automation moving into sourcing. A close macro of neatly coiled blue network cable on a matte charcoal surface, a single cool LED highlight catching the coil, extreme shallow focus, no devices, ports, or labels, no text anywhere. Cool blue, industrial. [SOLO MODE: visual brief sketch]]

**The Short Version:** LinkedIn's first AI agent for recruiters — Hiring Assistant, which finds candidates and drafts outreach by itself — moved from a small charter group to worldwide availability, and a February 2026 update pushed it deeper into the workflow. The tool your researchers rely on now does first-pass sourcing itself.

**The Details:**

- Announced September 3, 2025 and globally available in English by the end of that month, after a limited charter launch in October 2024. [news.linkedin.com](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/hiring-assistant-globally-available)
- LinkedIn's own early-adopter figures — **4+ hours saved per role, 62% fewer profiles reviewed, 69% higher InMail acceptance** — are vendor self-reported and not independently audited. [news.linkedin.com](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/hiring-assistant-globally-available)
- Named early users include Microsoft, Chewy, Expedia Group, Siemens, Wipro and AMD. [news.linkedin.com](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/hiring-assistant-globally-available)
- A **February 2026 update** added AI Follow-Ups, AI Applicant Targeting and Microsoft Teams collaboration, pushing the agent further into sourcing. [influencermarketinghub.com](https://influencermarketinghub.com/linkedin-ai-hiring-assistant/)

**Why It Matters:** This cuts two ways. For a lean desk it is leverage — first-pass sourcing without adding a researcher. For a junior-heavy desk it is a margin threat, because the entry-level sourcing work your model may depend on is exactly what the platform now automates. Either way, sourcing is commoditizing. The defensible work moves up the stack — to the client relationships and judgment a subscription cannot ship.

**REGULATION**

**[Pay-Transparency Laws Now Cover 17 States, And Enforcement Has Started](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/navigating-2026-pay-transparency-laws-and-employer-obligations)**

[IMAGE BRIEF — s4. Job: signal law, compliance, and enforcement. A quiet, formal photo of an empty wood-paneled hearing room — one empty chair at a long table, cold institutional daylight from high windows, no flags, plaques, or signage, no text anywhere. Formal, cold, architectural. [SOLO MODE: visual brief sketch]]

**The Short Version:** A wave of state salary-range laws means roughly 17 states now require some pay disclosure on job postings, and Massachusetts and New Jersey have moved to active enforcement in 2026 — issuing audits and penalties. On a separate track, Illinois and California AI-hiring rules are now in force, putting the staffing agent on the hook for biased automated screening.

**The Details:**

- New or expanded pay-transparency mandates landed across 2025-2026: New Jersey (June 2025), Massachusetts (October 2025), Maine (January 2026), plus a refined California law effective January 1, 2026. [jacksonlewis.com](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/navigating-2026-pay-transparency-laws-and-employer-obligations)
- By one industry count, **17 states** now require salary information and roughly **65% of US employers** operate under some mandate — a secondary estimate, since counts vary by how "posting" rules are defined. [netchex.com](https://netchex.com/blog/pay-transparency-laws-in-2026-what-multi-state-employers-need-to-change-in-job-postings/)
- Massachusetts and New Jersey are now **auditing and penalizing** non-compliant postings, and a role performable from a covered state triggers that state's rule regardless of your HQ. [hunton.com](https://www.hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/several-states-enact-pay-transparency-laws-what-employers-need-to-know-in-2026)
- Separately, **Illinois' amended Human Rights Act (effective January 1, 2026)** and **California's automated-decision rules (effective October 1, 2025)** make employers and their staffing agents liable for discriminatory AI screening. [natlawreview.com](https://natlawreview.com/article/illinois-anti-discrimination-law-address-ai-goes-effect-1-january-2026) · [mayerbrown.com](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/california-adopts-new-employment-ai-regulations-effective-october-1-2025)

**Why It Matters:** Two compliance duties just became client conversations you can own. Post good-faith ranges or your clients risk penalties, and remote roles pull in the strictest state's law — so comp benchmarking is now a legitimate paid-advisory wedge to open a business-development conversation, not just a checkbox. And if you run AI sourcing or screening for Illinois or California roles, you have inherited notice, record-keeping, and bias exposure whether or not you wrote the algorithm. "The vendor's tool did it" is not a defense.

**TECH TALENT**

**[In A Soft Market, Tech Talent Is Still Scarce: 2.9% Unemployment](https://www.dice.com/career-advice/comptia-tech-jobs-report---june-2026)**

[IMAGE BRIEF — s5. Job: signal scarce, in-demand tech talent and IT work. A close, warm-lit still of a dim server aisle shot as pure texture — rows of unlabeled matte-black racks with soft amber standby glows, deep shallow focus so no port or label is legible, no text anywhere. Dark, warm points of light, depth. [SOLO MODE: visual brief sketch]]

**The Short Version:** Even with hiring cooled across the economy, tech-occupation unemployment was 2.9% in June 2026 — against about 4.2% nationally — and net US tech employment is projected to keep growing in 2026. For IT and tech desks, the demand engine has not stalled; it has just gotten more selective.

**The Details:**

- Tech-occupation unemployment ran **2.9% in June 2026 (3.1% in May)** versus roughly **4.2% nationally**. [dice.com](https://www.dice.com/career-advice/comptia-tech-jobs-report---june-2026)
- Net US tech employment (~9.6 million in 2025) is projected to grow ~1.9% in 2026 to about **9.8 million**. [comptia.org](https://www.comptia.org/en-us/resources/research/state-of-the-tech-workforce-2026/)
- The 57 largest US IT-staffing firms generated **$27.2 billion in 2025 (down 4.4%)**, with the segment forecast back to +1% growth in 2026 and 2027 — ending a three-year decline. [staffingindustry.com](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/it-staffing-report/largest-us-it-staffing-firms-generate-27.2b-in-revenue)
- Nationally the market is "low-hire, low-fire": job openings held at **7.6 million** and the quits rate at **1.9%**, so the roles that do open are harder to fill and higher value. [bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm)

**Why It Matters:** If you run an IT or tech desk, the scarcity you built the business on is intact — clients still cannot fill these roles themselves, which is exactly the access you sell. In a low-hire market the play is not volume; it is being the firm that can produce the scarce candidate fast. Generalist desks feel the soft market. Specialist tech desks still hold a seller's hand in their niche — if they lean into it instead of chasing req count.

**CONSOLIDATION**

**[A Founding-Family Staffing Giant Just Sold Control For $106 Million](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/it-staffing-report/kelly-announces-new-controlling-shareholder-board-members)**

[IMAGE BRIEF — s6. Job: signal ownership, a deal, a handover. A restrained still life on a dark leather desktop — two unbranded brass keys resting beside a closed leather portfolio, hard low window light throwing long shadows, no monogram, engraving, or text. Still life, hard light, quiet "handover" symbolism. [SOLO MODE: visual brief sketch]]

**The Short Version:** The trust of Kelly Services' founding family sold its controlling voting stake to Hunt Equity Opportunities in early 2026 for about $106 million, handing board control of the roughly $4.3-billion-revenue firm to new private ownership. A legacy leader changing hands at a modest price is a vivid marker of compressed valuations — and of active buyer appetite anyway.

**The Details:**

- The founding-family trust sold its entire **92.2% of Kelly's Class B voting stock to Hunt Equity Opportunities for $106.0 million**; the deal closed January 30, 2026 and gave Hunt board control. [staffingindustry.com](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/it-staffing-report/kelly-announces-new-controlling-shareholder-board-members)
- A **$15.2 million earnout** kicks in if market cap reaches $1.2 billion within 48 months; the terms are documented in Kelly's investor filing. [ir.kellyservices.com](https://ir.kellyservices.com/node/25881/pdf)
- Consolidation continued elsewhere in 2026: Atlantic International acquired Circle8 to form a **~$1.2 billion** workforce platform, and Synergie took a majority stake in Agilus. [privsource.com](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/staffing-hr/2026)
- Kelly reports roughly **$4.3 billion** in annual revenue, so this was a legacy leader changing hands at a modest headline price. [staffingindustry.com](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/it-staffing-report/kelly-announces-new-controlling-shareholder-board-members)

**Why It Matters:** A founding family exiting a legacy leader at a modest multiple tells you two things about this market: valuations are compressed, and buyers are active anyway. If you are weighing an exit, a roll-up, or an acquisition, the window is open — but pricing discipline is tight. The firms that command a premium are the ones that are not wholly dependent on the owner to bring in business; a book of revenue that only the founder can reproduce is the first thing a buyer discounts.

### One free thing: a five-step play to reopen a past client this week

[IMAGE BRIEF — UTILITY (s7). Job: illustrate the hands-on "reopen a past client" play. A warm, natural still of a hand resting on an open card file of blank index cards on a wooden desk, morning light, no legible writing on any card and no text in frame. Hands and object, warm, practical. [SOLO MODE: visual brief sketch]]

The temporary and contract workforce turned over 376% in 2025 — nearly four times in a year ([americanstaffing.net](https://americanstaffing.net/posts/2026/03/30/employment-and-sales-rebound-in-q4/)) — and the average role takes about 44 to 45 days to fill ([theresource.com](https://www.theresource.com/2025/10/27/time-to-fill-by-industry/)). Your fastest pipeline is not a cold prospect; it is a client who already bought from you once and a candidate already in your system. Here is a concrete play, no tools to buy. It is the same give every issue: pure utility, no ask.

**1. Pull the "placed once, never again" list.** Filter your ATS or CRM for clients you placed exactly one role with in the last 24 to 36 months and have not invoiced since. That short list is your warmest cold outreach anywhere.

**2. Lead with a candidate, not a check-in.** Skip "just circling back." Send one specific, currently available candidate who fits a role that client actually hires for. That gives them a reason to reply, instead of a favor to grant.

**3. Time it to their req pattern.** Note when you placed the first role. Many seats are backfilled on a rough annual cycle, so reach out ahead of the anniversary — before the chair is already filled, not after.

**4. Work the near-miss candidates in parallel.** For every open or recurring role, your database already holds last time's runner-up. Redeployment is the highest-leverage sourcing most desks are not doing. Shortlist three from your own records before you post the job.

**5. Book the next touch before you hang up.** Follow-up does not die on the first call; it dies because nobody schedules the second. End every conversation with a dated next step, in the calendar, so a busy week cannot quietly erase it.

*None of this needs a new platform — just one focused hour and the list you already have. Run it once a week and the past-client roster stops being a graveyard and starts being a pipeline.*
