MARKET SHIFT

The Budget Is Quietly Moving From Ads to Partners

Image source: The Referral Channel

The Short Version: The money that used to go to paid ads is shifting to partners and affiliates, and the platforms are publishing the receipts. US affiliate spend is forecast to hit $13.81 billion in 2026 — growing about twice as fast as ecommerce overall — while most B2B firms now expect their partner channel to out-earn last year.

The Details:

  • US affiliate marketing spend is projected at $13.81 billion in 2026, up 11.3% — nearly double the growth rate of ecommerce as a whole (eMarketer).
  • Two-thirds of B2B companies expect partner revenue to beat last year by 30% or more — a bet on ecosystems over ad spend (Forrester).
  • The creator slice is climbing fast: Awin’s creator-driven share rose from 15.9% to 19.5% in a year (eMarketer).
  • It’s a forecast and an expectation, not booked revenue — the direction is clear; the exact size depends on who’s counting.

Why It Matters: Paid acquisition gets more expensive every quarter; a partner channel gets cheaper as it compounds. For an agency billing clients for growth, that’s the difference between renting results and owning a source of them.

THE PLAY

The Eight-Business Play That Costs Nothing

Image source: The Referral Channel

The Short Version: Affiliate expert Amber Spears teaches a move she calls the Power of Eight: get eight non-competing businesses that all serve the same customer to commit, for one year, to promoting each other. No ad budget, no new product — just eight trusted businesses pointing their audiences at each other on purpose.

The Details:

  • The rule is non-competing but same-customer — a wedding photographer, florist, venue, and planner all sell to the same bride (Amber Spears).
  • It runs on “trust transfer”: a referral works because the partner lends you credibility they already earned (Spears).
  • She credits the idea to health entrepreneurs JJ Virgin and Dr. Mark Hyman, who cross-promoted to each other’s audiences.
  • Her contrarian rule on pay: give a partner what a customer is worth to you anywhere else — if a lead is worth $200, the partner who sends one should get near that.

Why It Matters: Most owners already know eight businesses that share their customer. The reason it isn’t a channel yet isn’t strategy — it’s that nobody built the tracking, the terms, or the follow-through.

CASE STUDY

How One Newsletter Grew to 1.5 Million Readers on Referrals

Image source: The Referral Channel

The Short Version: Morning Brew’s co-founder has said the business newsletter went from about 100,000 to 1.5 million subscribers in roughly 18 months — and the engine was a referral program that rewarded existing readers for bringing in new ones. A referral loop, run deliberately, did what paid growth couldn’t.

The Details:

  • Readers earned tiered rewards for referring friends, each tracked with a unique link (Tyler Denk).
  • The jump — roughly 100K to 1.5M in about 18 months — came during the program’s run (Denk).
  • A large share of new subscribers was credited to referrals, though that figure is the company’s own and reported after the fact.
  • It’s one winner, not a promise — plenty of referral programs go nowhere; this one had a product people wanted to share.

Why It Matters: The mechanism isn’t exotic. It’s a tracked reason for the people who already like you to bring the next person — which is exactly what an affiliate program is, pointed at your customers instead of at strangers.

QUICK HITS
 

The Partner Pitch

The play (this week): Pull your last 20 clients. For each, list the non-competing businesses they already pay — their web person, their bookkeeper, their agency. The names that repeat are your first partners. Then send three of them this:

“Hi [Name] — your clients and mine are basically the same people, just at different moments. What if we made referrals official: you send me the ones who need [your thing], I send you the ones who need yours, and we track both sides so it’s fair and it actually pays out. 15 minutes this week to map it?”

Why it works: it leads with their upside, it’s specific, and “tracked” removes the trust problem that kills most handshake deals.
