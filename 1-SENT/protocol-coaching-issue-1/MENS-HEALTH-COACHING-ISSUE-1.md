**TESTOSTERONE**

**[Testosterone Lost Its Heart Warning. The Same Trial Flagged Fractures.](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20250710/removal-of-boxed-warning-clears-testosterone-of-heartrelated-risk-but-concerns-remain)**

**The short version:** On February 28, 2025, the FDA ordered a class-wide labeling change that stripped the boxed cardiovascular warning off every testosterone product — a warning that had been there since 2015. The basis was TRAVERSE, a 5,246-man trial that found no rise in major cardiac events: 7.0% on testosterone versus 7.3% on placebo. In its place, a narrower blood-pressure warning.

**The details:**

- The same trial that cleared the heart risk recorded *more* fractures in the testosterone group, not fewer (clinical-fracture hazard ratio 1.43; all-fracture 1.52) — an unexpected, single-trial signal.
- It also logged more atrial fibrillation (3.5% versus 2.4%), which is why Cleveland Clinic's Steven Nissen and others still counsel caution rather than an all-clear.
- TRAVERSE studied middle-aged and older men with diagnosed low testosterone and existing or high cardiovascular risk — the safety read applies to treating a diagnosis, not to recreational or high-dose use.

**Why it matters:** The number-one fear your clients arrive with — "won't this wreck my heart?" — just lost its federal warning label. That is real, and worth saying plainly. But the fracture and rhythm signals did not vanish; the pill-sellers simply skip them. Being the operator who states both is the kind of trust an ad cannot buy.

**MARKET**

**The short version:** On September 10, 2025, Hims launched a men's-health testosterone category built around KYZATREX, an FDA-approved oral testosterone from partner Marius Pharmaceuticals, with compounded enclomiphene and injectables slated for 2026. The company's own release points to an estimated 20 million U.S. men with low testosterone. For the full year, Hims reported $2.35B in revenue, up 59%.

**The details:**

- KYZATREX is an actual FDA-approved oral product, not a supplement — the DTC giants are now in prescription testosterone, not just ED pills.
- The 20-million-men figure is Hims's own market estimate, quoted from its launch release, not an independent count.
- The $2.35B (and 59% growth, with $128.4M net income) is total company revenue across men's, women's, and weight-loss lines — Hims does not break out a testosterone-only number.

**Why it matters:** A public company this size can put a testosterone pill in front of millions of men it has already paid to acquire, at near-zero added cost. No independent coach outspends that, and "access to testosterone" is no longer a differentiator worth naming. What a subscription structurally cannot deliver — the labs read in context, the accountability, the person who notices when a client goes quiet — is exactly what is left to sell, and it happens to be the whole job.

**METABOLIC**

**[On A GLP-1, Up To 39% Of The Weight Lost Is Muscle](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20251114/muscle-matters-the-challenge-of-preserving-lean-mass-during-obesity-treatment)**

**The short version:** In the pivotal trials, about 39% of the weight lost on semaglutide (STEP-1) and about 25% of the weight lost on tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1) was lean mass, not fat. The drug decides how much a client eats; it does not decide whether what he loses is fat or muscle. The interventions that protect the muscle are the ones a coach already sells.

**The details:**

- The two protective levers, straight from the clinical guidance: roughly 1.2 to 1.6 grams of protein per kilogram of bodyweight per day, and strength training at least three times a week.
- One honest caveat to teach, not hide: the "lean mass" measured on a body scan includes body fluid, so a client's true muscle loss is usually somewhat smaller than the headline fraction.
- The clients are already here — a growing share of a men's-health roster is on Wegovy, Zepbound, or Ozempic, and most arrived with no plan for the muscle going out with the fat.

**Why it matters:** The shortcut did not make the coach obsolete. It created a job only the coach can do: keeping a rapid weight loss from becoming frailty. The medication handles appetite; the coach is the reason the weight that comes off is fat and the strength stays. That is a clean, evidence-backed pitch, and it sits in your lane without touching a prescription pad.

**POSITIONING**

**[Why An "In-Range" Man Still Feels Off](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/testosterone-deficiency-guideline)**

**The short version:** A man can sit squarely inside the lab's "normal" band and still feel exhausted, foggy, and flat — and the guidelines explain why. The harmonized normal range for healthy young men runs from 264 to 916 ng/dL, a band whose ceiling is more than three times its floor. And the diagnostic bodies never treated the number as the whole story.

**The details:**

- The American Urological Association uses total testosterone "below 300 ng/dL as a reasonable cut-off," confirmed on two separate early-morning draws — one afternoon reading is not a diagnosis.
- The AUA is explicit that testosterone deficiency "is only made when patients have low total testosterone levels combined with symptoms and/or signs." The number without the symptoms is not a diagnosis; the symptoms without the number are not either.
- That "normal" 264–916 ng/dL range was built from healthy young men — a population statistic, not a personal set point. A 45-year-old at 320 is technically "in range" while sitting near the floor of a band defined by 25-year-olds.

**Why it matters:** "Your labs are normal" is where the doctor's visit ends and the client's frustration begins. It is usually true and rarely the whole truth — the range is wide, population-built, and by the guidelines' own design only means something read against how the man actually functions. That gap is yours to own, not by promising a magic number the science does not support, but by tracking energy, training, sleep, and recovery over time — the context a single lab flag was never meant to carry. Diagnosis and prescribing stay with his physician; the honest interpretation is the coach's.

**BUSINESS**

**[The Coach Who Stopped Renting Access By The Month](https://theptdc.com/how-to-make-money-as-online-trainer)**

**The short version:** Coach Jim Gazzale moved off a $95-a-month plan and rebuilt his offer as a fixed-price, twelve-week program organized around a defined outcome, priced at $1,500. He reports the pipeline grew afterward — and the change was the package and the price frame, not more marketing spend. The lesson is the mechanism, not one coach's numbers.

**The details:**

- A monthly-access plan sells time; a twelve-week outcome program sells a finish line — which filters for committed clients and lets you charge for the result instead of the hours.
- This is a single self-reported example (grade C), so take the move, not the figure, as proof: reframing "rent me by the month" into "here is the transformation and the price" is the copyable part.
- The context that makes it urgent: professional coaching crossed $5.34B in revenue and 122,974 practitioners in 2025, up 15% since 2023 (ICF/PwC). An undifferentiated monthly plan is the easiest thing in that crowd to undercut.

**Why it matters:** When the room is filling with coaches and a $2.35B pill company (see above), the losing position is "access to a coach, billed monthly" — that is a commodity, and commodities compete on price. The premium position is a specific man, a specific outcome, and a price that reflects it. You are not selling cheaper access than Hims; you are selling the thing access alone never delivers.

### One free thing: the muscle-protection protocol you can hand a GLP-1 client this week

[IMAGE BRIEF — UTILITY (s7): A simple, naturalistic still life on a wooden kitchen counter — an unbranded shaker bottle, a cooked chicken breast on a white plate, and one dumbbell — bright natural light, no labels and no text in frame.]

Up to 39% of the weight a client loses on a GLP-1 can be lean mass. Here is the clinical guidance, boiled down to something that fits on an index card. It is the same give every issue: pure utility, no ask.

**1. Set a daily protein floor: 1.2 to 1.6 g per kilogram of bodyweight.** Hit it every day, not just training days.

| Bodyweight | Daily protein target |
|---|---|
| 70 kg (154 lb) | 84–112 g |
| 90 kg (198 lb) | 108–144 g |
| 110 kg (242 lb) | 132–176 g |

**2. Lift at least three times a week.** In the largest study to date — 147,374 people — 90 to 120 minutes of strength training a week tracked with the lowest death risk, and the benefit stopped climbing above that window. Three sessions of 30 to 40 minutes clears it. More is not the goal; consistency is.

**3. Coach the trend, not the scare.** On a body scan, "lean mass" includes water, so a single reading overstates true muscle loss. Watch the trajectory over weeks, keep protein and lifts steady, and do not let one number spook a client off the plan.

*This is training and nutrition coaching, not medical advice. The medication and its dosing stay between the client and his prescriber.*
