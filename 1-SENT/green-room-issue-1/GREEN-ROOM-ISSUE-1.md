### How To Get Clients Booked On Shows Worth Being On

[IMAGE â€” s7: A row of identical folding chairs against a foam-panelled wall in an empty studio, one chair pulled slightly out of line, cool overhead light.]

Every agency with a placement record built it the same boring way. There is no version of this that runs on relationships alone.

**1. Build the target list around one client, not around the charts.** The shows worth pitching are the ones already booking guests who look like this client. Pull the guest histories from five or six shows in the client's category, add the shows their competitors have appeared on, and add anything the client's own customers have mentioned by name. Then prioritise: fit first, reachability second, chart position never â€” 69.7% of hosts in the Buzzsprout survey find guests through a personal network, so any show where someone on your team has a real connection goes to the top of the list. **First move:** open a sheet, list twenty shows pulled from three adjacent shows' guest histories, and mark the five you could reach without a cold email.

**2. Pitch the host on their audience, not your client's rÃ©sumÃ©.** A pitch that gets a yes answers four things in under 150 words: why this show, why this guest specifically, what the host's listeners get out of the episode, and exactly what you need and how long it takes. Name the episode you want made, not the client you want placed. And be straight about size â€” the same survey ranks a guest's audience size 14.4%, eighth of nine booking criteria, and no dataset in our research showed that a notable guest lifts downloads. Fit and visible preparation are what these hosts said they weight. **First move:** rewrite your standard pitch so the first sentence is about the host's audience and the last sentence is a date, a duration and a link.

**3. Run it as a system, not as a scramble.** At a 3.2% booking rate it takes roughly 31 pitches to land one placement, which is a workload, not a mood. Batch the outreach into one standing sitting a week per client, follow up exactly twice â€” seven days and fourteen days out â€” then stop and move on. Keep a four-column pipeline (pitched / replied / booked / recorded) for each client so you can see a placement gap forming six weeks before the client sees it. **First move:** put a weekly outreach block in the calendar for the next eight weeks and treat it as a client meeting.
