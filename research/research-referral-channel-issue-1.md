## BLUF

There is more than enough verified 2025-2026 material to fill Issue 1 without touching a single content-mill statistics page. The strongest editorial spine is a simple, true story an agency owner will feel in their own P&L: money is moving out of paid ads and into partner and affiliate channels, and the platforms and brands are publishing the numbers. US affiliate spending is forecast at $13.81B in 2026, growing at 11.3%, nearly double the rate of ecommerce overall (eMarketer, B2). B2B firms expect partner revenue to outpace last year by wide margins (Forrester, B2). Vendor surveys and named case studies (Morning Brew, GymShark, Dropbox) show the same direction of travel, though each vendor number needs its label.

Amber Spears is real, verifiable, and quotable. She co-founded East 5th Avenue (2017), teaches a named method, and gave concrete frameworks on the record: the "Power of Eight," a three-part program model, and "trust transfer." Her firm's headline revenue claims are self-reported and should be labeled as such, but her teaching ideas are clean enough to build a utility segment and the lead "one real play" around.

One tradecraft note up front: a stat circulating as "84% of B2B conversions start from a referral (Forrester)" does not trace to any Forrester primary. Do not use it. Details in the Rejected Claims section.

Recommended confidence: it is **almost certain** the "budget is shifting toward partners/affiliates" thesis is directionally correct (multiple independent A/B and vendor sources agree), and **likely** that the specific vendor percentages overstate the effect for the average business (selection bias in who answers these surveys). High confidence on the macro direction; Moderate on any single vendor figure.

## FULL CANDIDATE STORY LIST (graded)

### 1. Affiliate ad spend to hit $13.81B in 2026, outpacing ecommerce
- **Category:** [MKT] macro anchor
- **Source:** eMarketer / EMARKETER, "FAQ on affiliate marketing: How AI and creators are reshaping the channel in 2026" — https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-affiliate-marketing--how-ai-creators-reshaping-channel-2026
- **Date:** Published April 28, 2026; figures cite eMarketer's September 2025 forecast.
- **Grade:** B2 (established analyst). The 2026 figure is a forecast (projection), so treat the growth number as modeled, not booked.
- **Verified facts (all on the fetched page):**
  - US affiliate spending is forecast at "$13.81 billion in 2026, up 11.3% from $12.42 billion in 2025."
  - That 11.3% growth "significantly outpaces overall US retail ecommerce expansion of 6.7%."
  - The affiliate channel is estimated to generate roughly "$241.03 billion in US ecommerce sales in 2026" (note: sales driven, a different measure than the $13.81B of ad spend).
- **Why an agency owner cares:** The channel is growing at nearly double the rate of the ecommerce it rides on. If a client is spending on Meta and Google and ignoring affiliate/partner, they are underweight in the fastest-growing acquisition line.
- **Angle:** "The acquisition channel growing twice as fast as everything else, and why most of your clients underuse it."

### 2. AI and creators are quietly rewriting how affiliate works
- **Category:** [TREND] / same source as #1
- **Source:** eMarketer (same URL as #1), citing its October 2025 report plus Awin, Sensor Tower, and Wildfire Systems data.
- **Date:** Published April 28, 2026; underlying data Dec 2024-June 2025 and full-year 2025.
- **Grade:** B2 for eMarketer's framing; the Awin and Sensor Tower figures are single-sourced through eMarketer (B3).
- **Verified facts (fetched):**
  - On Awin's network, creator revenue share rose "from 15.9% to 19.5% year-over-year."
  - Compounding effect: "by the eighth integration with the same YouTube creator, affiliate link clickthrough rates reach 1.8x."
  - "Shopping-related queries on ChatGPT grew faster than any other query type between December 2024 and June 2025."
  - Consumer genAI shopping use: price comparison 54%, deal finding 41%, review checking 41% (attributed to Sensor Tower and Wildfire Systems).
- **Why an agency owner cares:** Two shifts at once. Creators are becoming the affiliate channel, and AI assistants are becoming a discovery surface that affiliate/partner content can occupy while paid search cannot. Both are services an agency can sell.
- **Angle:** "Your clients' next affiliate isn't a coupon site, it's a YouTuber on their eighth video, and maybe ChatGPT."

### 3. Forrester: two-thirds of B2B firms expect partner revenue to jump 30%+
- **Category:** [MKT] B2B partner credibility
- **Source:** Forrester, "Continued Growth In Scale And Complexity: The State Of Partner Ecosystems In 2025," by Kathy Contreras (VP, Principal Analyst) — https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-state-of-partner-ecosystems-2025/
- **Date:** July 7, 2025. Underlying: Forrester's Partner Ecosystem Marketing Survey (sample size not disclosed in the blog).
- **Grade:** B2 (established analyst, primary survey). Caveat: these are decision-makers' expectations, not measured outcomes.
- **Verified facts (fetched):**
  - "Sixty-seven percent of those surveyed plan for their indirect revenue... to grow above or significantly above that of last year's (i.e., they expect growth to be over 30% greater than the previous year)."
  - "Two-thirds... expect partner-influenced revenue to grow above or significantly above" last year's, also framed as over 30%.
  - A majority expect the number of partners in their ecosystems to expand, with the highest growth in technology partners, distribution partners, and digital routes to market.
- **Why an agency owner cares:** Buyers are betting on partners for growth. That means "become a partner in someone's ecosystem" is now a distribution channel an agency can build for a client, not a nice-to-have.
- **Angle:** "The channel your clients' competitors are funding right now, and how an agency plugs into it."

### 4. Brands now pull a double-digit slice of revenue from affiliate
- **Category:** [MKT] adoption / vendor survey (label clearly)
- **Source:** impact.com, "State of Affiliate Marketing Research Report 2025" — https://impact.com/research-reports/state-of-affiliate-marketing/
- **Date:** 2025. Survey of 1,523 respondents across 8 countries (818 marketers, 284 publishers, 421 creators).
- **Grade:** C3. This is a real survey with a sizable sample, but impact.com sells partnership-management software, and respondents skew toward brands that already run partner programs. Selection bias inflates the averages. Label it as impact.com's own report every time it is used.
- **Verified facts (fetched):**
  - "74% of brands generate 11-30% of their total revenue from affiliate marketing."
  - "59% of brands plan to allocate at least 25% of their affiliate budgets to creator partnerships."
  - 97% of brands and 96% of creators report using AI in their partnership programs.
  - 94% of brands are experimenting with or planning to adopt alternative attribution models within the year.
  - Leading brands work with 3-4 diverse partner types rather than one channel.
- **Why an agency owner cares:** If a large share of program-running brands already pull 11-30% of revenue from affiliate, an agency client with no program is leaving a known revenue line on the table.
- **Angle:** "The revenue line 74% of program-running brands already have, and yours doesn't." Present with the vendor caveat visible; the honesty is a credibility asset for a newsletter selling exactly this.

### 5. HubSpot's own numbers: a $42B partner opportunity by 2030
- **Category:** [MKT] agency-on-a-platform
- **Source:** PPC Land, "HubSpot's $42B partner bet: who wins when agents run the CRM," reporting an IDC white paper commissioned by HubSpot — https://ppc.land/hubspots-42b-partner-bet-who-wins-when-agents-run-the-crm/
- **Date:** Article June 15, 2026. Research authored by IDC, "sponsored white paper" (the article's own words), commissioned by HubSpot.
- **Grade:** C3. Trade-press reporting of a vendor-commissioned projection. Grade the modeler: IDC-for-HubSpot, forward-looking, so it is a projection, not a fact.
- **Verified facts (fetched):**

- **Angle:** "The platform is telling you where the money is. Partners keep more of the HubSpot economy than HubSpot does." Note: I could not confirm the widely-quoted "$6 in partner revenue for every $1 of HubSpot revenue" in any fetched source, so do not print that specific multiplier; use the $42B-by-2030 figure instead.

## AMBER SPEARS (utility segment + tactical-play source)

**Verdict: real, verifiable, and usable.** She is a genuine named expert with on-the-record frameworks across multiple independent podcasts. Her firm's biggest revenue claims are self-reported marketing and are labeled as such below. Do not print those as neutral facts; do quote her frameworks.

**Who she is (corroborated across 3 independent named sources):**
- Co-founder of East 5th Avenue, an affiliate-management agency and training company she started with Alona Rudnitsky in 2017. Also associated with the SPEARS Method, Affiliate Accelerator, and the Mimosa/Four Rooms Mastermind. (East 5th Avenue site; DigitalMarketer podcast; Hustle & Flowchart.)
- Origin story (Hustle & Flowchart, fetched): she moved into marketing while "handwriting copy and struggling financially," took over a struggling affiliate department paying 10% commission, and built it up. She now positions as a speaker, trainer, and consultant.
- **Self-reported results (LABEL AS SUCH, do not present as verified):** "helps clients do over $150 million in business a year" (Hustle & Flowchart). East 5th Avenue's marketing elsewhere claims 2,500+ companies trained, 8M+ leads, and $530M+ in affiliate revenue generated. These are the company's own figures with no independent audit. Grade D for the numbers; fine to attribute as "by her own account," not as fact.

**Quotable ideas we can teach from (grade C3, named-expert method):**

1. **The Power of Eight** (the strongest, and the recommended lead play). From the Conquer Local podcast, episode 719 (fetched, dated May 29, 2024): eight complementary businesses serving the same customer avatar commit to cross-promoting each other for a full year. Her verbatim description: *"If one person was invited on the podcast, they also recommended the other seven. If one person was doing a live event, the other seven would promote and show up and speak and support."* She attributes the pattern to health-space creators like JJ Virgin and Dr. Mark Hyman.

2. **Trust transfer is the mechanism** (Conquer Local, fetched, verbatim): *"It works because you have that transfer of trust where people that you look to as subject matter experts... you're going to take their word when they endorse a software or tool or person as gold."* Useful framing: affiliate/referral is not discount-hunting, it is borrowed credibility.

3. **Pay affiliates your customer-acquisition cost** (Conquer Local, fetched): *"If you're acquiring your customers online already or you know what your cost is to acquire a customer, I would just give that to your affiliates."* A concrete, contrarian compensation rule most program-builders get wrong by underpaying.

4. **The three-part program model** (Conquer Local, fetched): a program succeeds on (a) program design based on your real unit economics rather than copying a competitor's payout, (b) a dedicated internal person with sales skill running it daily, and (c) the right external partner selection and process to keep them promoting long-term.

5. **"Business with a small b vs a capital B"** (Hustle & Flowchart, fetched, verbatim): *"These kind of handshake deals are just not the proper way to do business, it's business with a small b, not a capital B."* Her argument for systematizing partnerships with real tracking and terms instead of informal favors. This is, not coincidentally, the exact gap the house offer fills.

6. **Affiliate defined** (DigitalMarketer podcast ep. 165, fetched, verbatim): affiliate marketing is *"a beautiful blend of traffic, sales, leads, PR, and relationships."*

**Honesty note:** her frameworks are opinion-and-method, not measured findings, so they carry C3 (a credible named practitioner, self-interested). That is the correct grade for a "here is how an expert thinks about it" teaching segment. It is not the grade for a statistic, and none of her numbers should be printed as statistics.

## THE TACTICAL PLAY (the "one real play this week" lead)

**Play: run the Power of Eight (start the first version this week).**
- **Source / provenance:** Amber Spears on the Conquer Local podcast, ep. 719, May 29, 2024 (fetched); she credits the pattern to health-space creators including JJ Virgin and Dr. Mark Hyman. Grade C3 (named-expert method).
- **The full play:** identify eight businesses that serve your exact same customer but do not compete with you, and get all eight to commit to promoting each other for a year. When one gets a podcast slot, they recommend the other seven. When one runs an event, the other seven show up and promote it. It is a closed loop of borrowed trust.
- **Why it works (Amber's mechanism, verbatim):** "transfer of trust where people that you look to as subject matter experts... you're going to take their word when they endorse a software or tool or person as gold."
- **The this-week version (a concrete first step the reader can do Monday):** open your CRM or your last 20 client engagements. Write down every other vendor those clients already pay who is not a competitor (the design shop, the fractional CFO, the CRM consultant, the PR firm). Pick the three you would happily send a client to. Send each a two-line message proposing one reciprocal introduction this month. That is the seed of a Power of Eight, and it costs nothing but the email.
- **Why it leads the issue:** zero budget, real source, and it is the honest small version of the thing the house offer productizes. It earns the reader's trust before card 3 asks for anything.

## CONFIDENCE + GAPS

**What I am confident in:**
- The macro thesis (budget shifting toward affiliate/partner channels) is **almost certain** and rests on independent A/B and vendor sources that agree: eMarketer (B2), Forrester (B2), impact.com (C3), plus named case studies. High confidence.
- Amber Spears is a real, quotable expert with on-the-record frameworks. **Almost certain.** High confidence on the frameworks existing and being hers; Low confidence on her self-reported revenue figures (single-sourced, self-interested).
- The eMarketer, Forrester, and HubSpot/IDC figures are quoted verbatim from fetched pages.

**Gaps and caveats (what would change the picture):**
- **Vendor tilt.** Three of the strongest single numbers (impact.com 74%, HubSpot/IDC $42B, the Morning Brew 30%) come from parties who sell the thing being measured. The direction is corroborated; the magnitudes are probably high. If an editor wants a fully independent number for a load-bearing claim, lean on eMarketer and Forrester and treat the rest as color.
- **Survivorship.** All three case studies are winners. Most referral programs do not 40x anything. If the issue implies "do this and you get Dropbox numbers," that is the survivorship trap. Frame cases as what is possible, not what is typical.
- **Recency splits.** eMarketer (2026), Forrester (2025), impact.com (2025), HubSpot/IDC (2026) are fresh. GymShark's £556M is FY2023 (2+ years old; later figures near £600M exist). Dropbox is 2010. Label the old ones.
- **Not fetched to primary this session:** the Dropbox numbers and the GymShark figures were taken from consistent secondary reporting, not re-fetched to a single primary. They agree across independent outlets, but if either becomes load-bearing, pull the filed accounts (GymShark, Companies House) or Drew Houston's original deck (Dropbox) before printing.
- **Not searched:** I did not chase down a clean primary for the Shopify partner-economy figures (roughly $12.5B partner revenue, 700K+ partners, and a "67% of agencies now earn 30%+ from app/extension work" claim). These are promising and very on-brand for an agency audience, but they were snippet-only and conflicting (app revenue vs total economic impact are different measures). Worth a dedicated trace if you want a sixth data-driven story, likely for Issue 2.
- **Amber second-framework corroboration:** the Power of Eight and trust-transfer quotes are from one fetched interview (Conquer Local). Her bio and other framing are corroborated across three sources, but the specific Power of Eight mechanics rest on that single episode. It is a method, not a fact, so single-sourcing is acceptable for a teaching segment; just attribute it to her plainly.
