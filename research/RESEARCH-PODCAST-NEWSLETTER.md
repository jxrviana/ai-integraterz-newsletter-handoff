## 1. YouTube Is Now The Podcast Platform

- **Category:** PLATFORMS
- **Source:** Edison Research at SSRS — *The Infinite Dial 2026* — https://www.edisonresearch.com/the-infinite-dial-2026/
- **Supporting (for platform-share figures):** Broadcast Dialogue — https://broadcastdialogue.com/2026-infinite-dial-highlights-continued-podcast-online-audio-growth/ · SiriusXM Media — https://www.siriusxmmedia.com/insights/the-infinite-dial-takeaways-for-advertisers
- **Date:** Fieldwork January 2026; presented March 12, 2026 (Broadcast Dialogue Mar 12, 2026; SiriusXM Media Apr 1, 2026)

**TL;DR raw material**
- 58% of Americans 12+ (167 million) consumed a podcast in the last month — an all-time high. *(Edison, verified on primary page)*
- 45% of Americans 12+ (130 million) consumed one in the last week. *(Edison, primary)*
- 80% of Americans 12+ (230 million) have ever listened to or watched a podcast. *(Edison, primary)*
- 57% of podcast consumers have **both** listened to and watched podcasts; only 21% listen exclusively, 2% watch exclusively. *(Edison, primary)*
- Among weekly podcast consumers, 39% use YouTube most often, vs. Spotify 20% and Apple Podcasts 11%. **[SECONDARY]** *(Edison's own public summary page does not publish this split; both Broadcast Dialogue and SiriusXM Media report it independently from the same presentation.)*
- Methodology: national survey, 2,050 respondents age 12+, SSRS Opinion Panel (RDD + address-based sampling), weighted to U.S. 12+ census. *(Edison, primary)*

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** If a host is still treating YouTube as the place they dump a leftover video file, they have their distribution upside-down: the single largest podcast destination in America is a video platform with a recommendation engine, and RSS is now the secondary surface for most of the potential audience. The 57% "both watch and listen" figure is the operational number — it means video isn't a separate product, it's the same episode meeting the same person in a different room.

**Pillar:** 1 (Platform and distribution shifts)

## 2. Apple Finally Ships Video — And Video Ads

- **Category:** PLATFORMS
- **Source:** Apple Newsroom — https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/02/apple-introduces-a-new-video-podcast-experience-on-apple-podcasts/
- **Date:** February 16, 2026
- **Verification:** Primary source, fetched directly.

**TL;DR raw material**
- Apple Podcasts introduced a native video podcast experience built on HLS (HTTP Live Streaming), with adaptive quality, full-screen horizontal viewing, offline video downloads, and seamless switching between watching and listening.
- For the first time, creators can **dynamically insert video ads, including host-read spots**, into Apple Podcasts episodes.
- Apple does not charge hosting providers or creators for podcast distribution via RSS/MP3 or HLS video.
- Apple will impose impression-based fees on **participating ad networks** for dynamic ad delivery in HLS video later in 2026.
- Launch support from Acast, ART19, Triton's Omny Studio, and SiriusXM (SiriusXM Media, AdsWizz, Simplecast); more providers to follow.
- Beta in iOS 26.4 / iPadOS 26.4 / visionOS 26.4 as of the announcement, with full rollout to iPhone, iPad, Apple Vision Pro and web in spring 2026.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** Apple was the last major holdout keeping "podcast" synonymous with "audio file," and it just stopped. For a small show, the practical consequence is that the video version you cut for YouTube now has a second monetizable home — but only if your host supports HLS, which makes your hosting provider a strategic choice rather than a commodity for the first time in years.

**Pillar:** 1 (Platform and distribution shifts)

## 3. Your Podcast Is Now On A Television

- **Category:** PLATFORMS
- **Source:** Westwood One / Cumulus Media & Signal Hill Insights, *Podcast Download — Spring 2026* — https://www.westwoodone.com/blog/2026/06/18/four-months-after-netflixs-podcast-launch-62-of-the-weekly-podcast-audience-are-aware-that-netflix-now-has-podcasts-the-smart-tv-podcast-audience-grows-19-according-to-cumulus-media-and-sig/
- **Date:** June 18, 2026
- **Verification:** Primary publisher of the research, fetched directly. **[VENDOR]** — Cumulus Media/Signal Hill are commercial audio businesses, but methodology is disclosed.

**TL;DR raw material**
- 38% of weekly podcast consumers now use a smart TV for podcasts, up from 32% — a 19% year-over-year increase.
- Smart TV accounts for 11% of all podcast listening time.
- Platform "use most": YouTube 44%, Spotify 14%, Apple 5%.
- Four months after Netflix launched podcasts, 62% of weekly podcast consumers are aware they can get podcasts on Netflix; 76% are aware video streaming services offer podcasts generally.
- Ideal ad load rose to 4.7 minutes per 60-minute podcast in 2026, up from 3.6 minutes across 2017–2023.
- 92% say they "listen" to podcasts; just 8% say they "only watch."
- Methodology: 600 weekly podcast consumers, fielded April 21–27, 2026, by Quantilope.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** Two useful and opposing facts sit in the same study: the screen is arriving (smart TV up 19%, nearly 4 in 10 using one) but the behaviour is still overwhelmingly auditory (92% say they listen). That argues against expensive cinematic production and for the cheap stuff that survives being half-watched from a sofa — legible framing, big captions, and audio that still works when nobody's looking. The ad-load number is a quiet gift: listeners will now tolerate roughly a minute more advertising per hour than they did three years ago.

**Pillar:** 1 (Platform and distribution shifts)

## 4. Fifty Percent Of Listeners, Twenty-Seven Shows

- **Category:** PLATFORMS
- **Source:** Podnews, reporting Edison Podcast Metrics UK presented at The Podcast Show London — https://podnews.net/update/uk-podcast-metrics-consolidation
- **Date:** May 25, 2026
- **Verification:** Fetched directly. **[SECONDARY]** — Podnews is reporting Edison's conference presentation; the underlying UK Podcast Consumer 2026 report was not yet published at time of writing.

**TL;DR raw material**
- In 2023, reaching 50% of all UK podcast listeners required advertising across the top 43 shows.
- By 2025, reaching the same 50% required only the top 27 shows.
- Direct quote: "In 2023, to reach 50% of all podcast listeners, you would need to advertise on the top 43 shows. But two years later, you only need the top 27 shows to reach half of all podcast listeners."
- Data previewed from the forthcoming UK Podcast Consumer 2026 report; a US edition was scheduled for release the following week.
- Separate analysis by Adam Bowie presented at the same event: 72% of hosts in the top 25 UK shows are male, vs. 49% of the British population.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** This is the number that explains why growth feels harder than the industry's cheerful topline stats suggest — the audience is expanding *and* concentrating at the same time, so a rising tide is genuinely lifting a smaller number of boats. The strategic read for an independent show is that competing for general reach is a losing game, and the defensible position is a specific audience that the top 27 cannot serve.

**Pillar:** 1 (Platform and distribution shifts) — also speaks to pillar 3 (discovery reality check)

## 5. Podcast Ads Hit $2.86 Billion

- **Category:** MONEY
- **Source:** Radio Ink, reporting the IAB/PwC Internet Advertising Revenue Report FY2025 — https://radioink.com/2026/04/16/iab-digital-audio-grew-10-in-2025-as-podcasts-near-3b/
- **Primary report landing page (loads, data gated behind download):** https://www.iab.com/insights/internet-advertising-revenue-report-full-year-2025/
- **Date:** April 16, 2026
- **Verification:** **[SECONDARY]** — Radio Ink fetched directly and used for all figures; the IAB/PwC full report is a gated PDF download.

**TL;DR raw material**
- U.S. podcast advertising revenue reached $2.862 billion in 2025, up 17.6% year over year.
- Total digital audio (podcasts + streaming music + streaming radio) hit $8.4 billion, up 10.2% from $7.6 billion in 2024.
- Digital audio holds 2.8% of total internet advertising revenue.
- Digital audio's 10.2% growth beat display (9.8%) but badly trailed digital video (25.4%) and social (32.6%); overall internet advertising grew 13.9%.
- For scale: podcast ad revenue was $105.7 million in 2015.
- Approximately 71% of U.S. podcast creators now produce video versions of their shows, which the report flags as a growing problem for revenue classification.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** The headline is growth, but the honest story is the share number — podcasting is growing while losing ground to video and social, which are pulling the same advertiser dollars faster. That 71% video figure is the tell: the money is quietly migrating to a category where podcasts get counted as "video," which is exactly where an independent host should want their inventory sitting.

**Pillar:** 2 (Monetization)

## 6. Spotify Just Halved The Bar

- **Category:** MONEY
- **Source:** Spotify Newsroom — https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-01-07/spotify-partner-program-updates/
- **Corroborating:** TechCrunch — https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/07/spotify-lowers-monetization-threshold-for-video-podcasts/
- **Date:** January 7, 2026
- **Verification:** Primary source fetched directly, plus independent TechCrunch confirmation of the threshold table.

**TL;DR raw material**
- Engaged audience requirement cut from 2,000 to **1,000** listeners on Spotify (based on plays over the last 30 days).
- Consumption-hours requirement cut from 10,000 to **2,000** hours over the last 30 days.
- Published-episode requirement cut from 12 to **3** episodes all-time.
- From April 2026, creators can remove, replace and add host-read sponsorships inside video episodes, schedule those changes ahead of time, and track sponsorship performance.
- A forthcoming Spotify Distribution API will let creators publish and monetize video on Spotify from their existing host without re-uploading — launch partners Acast, Audioboom, Libsyn, Omny and Podigee.
- Spotify states that in the U.S., loyal fans stream nearly 20x more of a show than casual listeners and are 2.5x more likely to still be there after six months.
- TechCrunch reports video podcast consumption on Spotify has "nearly doubled" since the program launched in January 2025.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** A show that publishes three episodes and holds a thousand engaged listeners is now inside a real payout program — that is genuinely within reach of a first-year independent podcast, which was not true twelve months ago. The dynamic host-read sponsorship tooling matters more than the threshold, though: it means a small show can sell the same ad slot again after a deal ends, turning a back catalogue into recurring inventory instead of a graveyard.

**Pillar:** 2 (Monetization)

## 7. Small Shows Convert Nearly As Well As Big Ones

- **Category:** MONEY
- **Source:** PPC Land, reporting Magellan AI's first Podcast Measurement Benchmark Report — https://ppc.land/podcast-ads-convert-at-5-22-what-magellan-ais-q1-data-actually-shows/
- **Related primary (fetched, separate report):** Magellan AI Q1 2026 advertising benchmarks — https://www.magellan.ai/news-insights/podcast-advertising-benchmarks-q1-2026
- **Date:** June 22, 2026 (measurement report); May 2026 (Q1 spend benchmarks)
- **Verification:** **[SECONDARY]** for the measurement/conversion figures — PPC Land fetched directly; Magellan's own measurement report page was not reachable as a data page. The Q1 *spend* benchmark page was fetched directly and is primary. **[VENDOR]** — Magellan AI sells podcast advertising analytics.

**TL;DR raw material**
- Average response rate 2.29% (share of unique reached listeners who visited an advertiser site); lead conversion 9.74%; purchase conversion 5.22%.
- By show rank: Top 500 shows 2.29% response / 5.20% purchase conversion; mid-tier (501–3000) 2.20% / 5.38%; long-tail (3000+) 1.63% / **5.23%**.
- By ad type: host-read 2.45% response, programmatic 1.93%, produced non-host-read 1.51%.
- By format: video 2.49% response vs. RSS-only 1.39%; video lead conversion 11.27% vs. 5.65% RSS-only.
- Campaigns measured Jan 1 – Mar 31, 2026 via Magellan's pixel-based attribution platform (campaign count not disclosed).
- Separately, from the directly-fetched Q1 2026 spend report: podcast ad spend +28% year over year; 95,612 episodes analysed; 1,318 brands advertised on podcasts for the first time; average advertiser ad load fell to 8.24% from 8.79% in Q4 2025.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** This is the single most useful sales argument a small show has ever been handed: long-tail podcasts ranked 3000+ convert purchases at 5.23%, statistically indistinguishable from the top 500's 5.20%. Reach is where big shows win; conversion is not. Pair that with host-read beating programmatic by 27% on response and the pitch to a direct sponsor writes itself — and 1,318 brands bought their first podcast ad last quarter, which is the prospect list.

**Pillar:** 2 (Monetization)

## 8. What A Good Show Actually Gets

- **Category:** GROWTH
- **Source:** Buzzsprout Podcast Stats — https://www.buzzsprout.com/stats
- **Date:** Data updated June 2026
- **Verification:** Fetched directly. **[VENDOR]** — Buzzsprout is a hosting company reporting on its own hosted shows; it discloses the population and its measurement standard.

**TL;DR raw material**
- Median episode (top 50%): **27 downloads** in the first 7 days.
- Top 25%: 98 downloads in the first 7 days.
- Top 10%: 412 downloads.
- Top 5%: 1,015 downloads.
- Top 1%: 4,605 downloads.
- Population: 113,575 active podcasts, approximately 82.8 million monthly downloads in the reporting period.
- Statistics comply with IAB Podcast Measurement Technical Guidelines 2.2.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** Almost every host badly overestimates what "normal" is, and the resulting despair kills more shows than bad audio ever has — 98 downloads in a week is genuinely a top-quartile episode. The caveat worth printing honestly: this is Buzzsprout's hosted base, which skews independent and self-serve, so it describes the world a small show actually lives in rather than the industry's headline averages.

## 9. Your Listeners Will Follow You Anywhere

- **Category:** GROWTH
- **Source:** Sounds Profitable, *The Podcast Atlas*, via Podnews press release — https://podnews.net/press-release/podcast-atlas
- **Report landing page (loads, PDF gated):** https://soundsprofitable.com/research/
- **Date:** Released June 29, 2026; keynoted at VidCon June 26, 2026
- **Verification:** Podnews press release fetched directly. **[SECONDARY]** for the underlying report, which is a gated PDF. **[VENDOR]** — Sounds Profitable is industry-funded research, but sample and sponsors are disclosed.

**TL;DR raw material**
- 73% of podcast listeners would follow a creator from audio to video.
- 71% would follow a creator from long-form content to short-form clips.
- 84% of clip consumers say podcast clips lead them to become regular listeners at least sometimes.
- 87% of podcast newsletter subscribers say the newsletter leads them to listen to episodes.
- Survey of more than 5,000 U.S. consumers.
- The study frames five territories: audio podcasts as "the trust engine," video as the engagement layer, short-form clips as the discovery mechanism, social as reach, and newsletters as the owned direct relationship.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** The fear that stops most hosts from cutting clips or starting a newsletter is cannibalisation — that a clip replaces the episode. The data says the opposite: 84% of clip viewers convert to listening at least sometimes, and newsletters are the highest-converting surface in the whole study at 87%. For a solo operator, the ranking here is a to-do list in priority order, and the newsletter sits at the top of it.

**Pillar:** 3 (Audience growth and discovery)

## 10. What Hosts Actually Want From A Guest

- **Category:** GUESTS
- **Source:** Buzzsprout, *Podcast Guest Survey 2026* — https://www.buzzsprout.com/blog/get-booked-on-podcasts
- **Date:** 2026
- **Verification:** Fetched directly. **[VENDOR]** — Buzzsprout survey; sample size disclosed (n=271), fielding method not disclosed.

**TL;DR raw material**
- What matters most when booking a guest: topic relevance 87.8%, guest expertise 62.0%, clear episode idea 34.3%, timely angle 26.6%, prior guest experience 24.0%, personalized pitch 18.5%, referral 17.7%, **audience size 14.4%**, simple logistics 10.7%.
- Where hosts find guests: personal network 69.7%, social media 28.4%, cold email pitch 28.0%, referrals from past guests 28.0%, podcast guest platform 22.5%, PR agency 19.9%, listener 18.1%, event or conference 13.7%, online communities 11.8%.
- Sample: 271 respondents.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** Two findings should reorganise how a host runs their booking pipeline. First, guest audience size ranks dead last but one at 14.4% — hosts are not booking for reach, they are booking for fit, which means the "get a big name" strategy is solving a problem hosts say they don't have. Second, 69.7% of bookings come from personal network while cold email delivers 28% — the pipeline is a relationship asset that compounds, and the shows with full calendars built theirs deliberately.

**Pillar:** 4 (Guests and booking) — the offer-setup pillar

## 11. Almost Nobody Charges Guests

- **Category:** GUESTS
- **Source:** Podseeker — *Do Podcast Guests Get Paid? What 16,590 Pitch Threads Reveal* — https://www.podseeker.co/blog/do-podcast-guests-get-paid
- **Supporting (same vendor, separate dataset):** https://www.podseeker.co/podcast-pitch
- **Date:** Data from May 2026; page last updated July 4, 2026
- **Verification:** Both pages fetched directly. **[VENDOR]** — Podseeker sells podcast pitching software; the dataset is its own customer base, which it states explicitly.

**TL;DR raw material**
- 16,590 real pitch threads analysed, drawn from active Podseeker customers (internal and trial accounts excluded).
- **99.4% of podcast appearances involved no fee at all.**
- Only 0.6% — 105 hosts — mentioned a guest fee.
- Where fees do exist: smaller shows charge $49–$100 as "production" or "feature" fees; mid-tier shows $200–$500; larger shows $1,000–$2,500, typically requiring in-studio recording.
- From Podseeker's separate pitch dataset: 8,757 pitches sent through the platform produced an overall booking rate of 3.2%.
- Stated caveat: this is one platform's customer base, not an industry census, and reflects shows that actively receive pitched guest inquiries.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** Pay-to-play guesting gets talked about as though it were an industry norm; at 0.6% it is a rounding error, and a host who starts charging guests is opting out of 99.4% of the market for a few hundred dollars. The more actionable number is the 3.2% booking rate — roughly 31 pitches per booking — which is the real cost of a guest calendar when you do it yourself, and the reason a systematic pipeline beats ad-hoc outreach.

**Pillar:** 4 (Guests and booking) — the offer-setup pillar

## 12. Riverside Wants To Write Your Newsletter

- **Category:** TOOLS
- **Source:** TechCrunch — https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/30/podcasting-platform-riverside-enters-the-newsletter-publishing-game/
- **Date:** June 30, 2026
- **Verification:** Fetched directly.

**TL;DR raw material**
- Riverside launched an AI tool that converts existing podcast and video recordings into newsletters, sent from inside the app; newsletters can also be written from scratch.
- New AI features: draft cut generated from a finished recording, social hooks and post content, and AI video enhancement trained on conversational video podcasts to improve lighting, depth and sharpness.

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** The tooling market has independently concluded what the Podcast Atlas data (item 9) measured — that the newsletter is the highest-value owned surface a show has — and is racing to collapse the distance between recording an episode and mailing one. For a solo host, the relevant question is no longer whether to run a newsletter but whether to run it inside the recording stack or keep the list somewhere they own outright.

## 13. The Podcast Company That Grew 321%

## Recommended five

1. **Small Shows Convert Nearly As Well As Big Ones** (item 7, MONEY) — the long-tail purchase-conversion figure of 5.23% vs. the top 500's 5.20% is the most commercially useful fact in this entire research set, and no one in the audience's inbox is telling them this.
2. **What Hosts Actually Want From A Guest** (item 10, GUESTS) — audience size ranks second-to-last at 14.4% while 69.7% of bookings come from personal network; this reframes guest booking as a pipeline problem, which is exactly the ground our offer stands on.
3. **YouTube Is Now The Podcast Platform** (item 1, PLATFORMS) — 39% vs. Spotify's 20%, from the most authoritative recurring study in the industry. Table stakes for issue #1 credibility.
4. **What A Good Show Actually Gets** (item 8, GROWTH) — the 27-download median is the single most reassuring and most shareable number available, and it earns enormous goodwill from a demoralised reader.
5. **Almost Nobody Charges Guests** (item 11, GUESTS) — 99.4% no-fee plus a 3.2% booking rate quantifies the cost of a guest calendar, which sets up the offer without pitching it.

**Pillar spread check:** platform item ✓ (item 1), monetization item ✓ (item 7), guests/booking items ✓✓ (items 10 and 11), growth item ✓ (item 8).

**Lead with: item 7, "Small Shows Convert Nearly As Well As Big Ones."**

Reasoning: it is the only story here that is simultaneously new, counterintuitive, and immediately monetisable for the reader. Items 1 and 8 are broadly known or reassuring rather than surprising; item 7 tells a host with 400 downloads that their advertising inventory converts as well as Joe Rogan's, which is both true per the data and the kind of claim that gets a first issue forwarded. It also opens the door to items 10 and 11 in the same issue — if conversion isn't the constraint, guest quality and calendar consistency are, which is precisely where the offer lands.

## Rejected sources and why

**Rejected outright — SEO stat listicles with untraceable numbers.** These dominate this niche and recycle each other's figures without attribution: `talks.co` (podcast-statistics, podcast-discovery-stats, booking-services, how-much-do-podcasters-make), `podrewind.com`, `podcastino.com`, `digitalapplied.com`, `marketingltb.com`, `sqmagazine.co.uk`, `electroiq.com`, `newmedia.com`, `learningrevolution.net`, `loopexdigital.com`, `amraandelma.com`, `searchlab.nl`. Numbers appear without a study, sample, or date.

**Rejected — unattributed case-study economics.** `influenceflow.io` carried a specific and appealing story (health podcast: 0 to 15,000 monthly listeners, $12k/$34k/$89k sponsorship over three years) with no named show, no methodology and no source. `thoughtleaders.io` claimed video podcasts command 40–60% higher sponsorship rates than audio at equivalent size, again unsourced. Both were exactly the kind of number issue #1 would have been embarrassed by.

**Rejected after checking — the "Chartable 0.75% cross-promo conversion" figure.** A search summary attributed a cross-promotion new-listener acquisition rate of ~0.75% to Chartable via `thepodcastconsultant.com`. I fetched that page: the number is not there, and the page's actual quantified claims ("30–100 new listeners per swap") are presented as typical ranges rather than tracked data. The Chartable attribution appears to be fabricated somewhere upstream. **No verified cross-promotion benchmark exists in this research set.**

**Rejected — user-generated content.** `vocal.media` "What We Learned from Analyzing 1 Million Podcast Downloads" is a self-publishing platform with no editorial verification.

**Rejected — gated marketing dressed as research.** `podmatch.com/report` renders unfilled template variables (e.g. `{{aereport_pageload.data.output_buzz_level}}`) rather than data, and locks its figures behind a quiz.

**Rejected — market-size projections from paywalled research firms.** `marketintelo.com` ($0.3bn to $0.7bn podcast guest booking market) and Precedence Research ($115.57bn by 2035) publish no visible methodology. The widely-repeated "$1.12 billion podcast guest booking market growing 13.8% annually" claim traces to this class of source and should not be used.

**Partially rejected — Podseeker's agency pricing.** Podseeker's *own* pitch-thread datasets are disclosed and usable (items 11). Its booking-agency price ranges ($1,500–$3,000/month etc.) on the same site name no agencies, no sample and no collection date, and are set against the company's own product — those figures are excluded.

**Deprioritised, not used — Acast CPM guide** (https://www.acast.com/en/news-and-insights/how-much-does-podcast-advertising-cost). It loads and is a real ad-industry source, but is dated November 20, 2023 with light 2026 updating and thin methodology.

**Stale, excluded despite appearing in 2026 search results:** the PodMatch "PodValue" press release on Podnews is from February 22, 2022, not 2026.

## Gaps and caveats for the editor

- **CPM benchmarks by show size could not be verified to an authoritative 2026 source.** Every CPM page surfaced was either an SEO listicle or a vendor guide without methodology. Item 7's response-and-conversion data by show tier is the better-evidenced substitute and should be used in place of CPM claims. **Do not publish a CPM range from this research.**
- **No verified data on whether notable guests move downloads.** This was a specific brief request under pillar 4. Multiple pages assert it; none showed a traceable dataset. Item 10's finding that hosts rank guest audience size at 14.4% is the closest verified evidence, and it points the other way.
- **No verified cross-promotion or guest-swap benchmark**, for the reason described in the rejected list above.
- **Two Infinite Dial platform-share figures rest on secondary reporting** (items 1) because Edison's public summary page does not publish the 39/20/11 split. Two independent outlets report it identically from the same presentation, which is reasonable but worth knowing.
- **The Sounds Profitable Podcast Discovery Playbook 2026 PDF is publicly available** at https://soundsprofitable.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/The-Podcast-Discovery-Playbook-2006.pdf but could not be parsed in this environment. It very likely contains the best available discovery-channel breakdown and is worth a manual read before finalising any discovery story.
- **A useful additional discovery source** that loads and was fetched: Forbes, July 7, 2026, covering Sounds Profitable/JAR Podcast Solutions — https://www.forbes.com/sites/frankracioppi/2026/07/07/podcast-listeners-use-youtube--social-media-for-podcast-discovery/ (40% discovered their favourite podcast on YouTube; 61% via YouTube plus social combined; 64% receive personal recommendations and 72% act on them; 60% of social discoveries came from organic content shared by someone they follow vs. 33% sponsored). Sample size not disclosed in the article — hence held as a backup rather than a numbered candidate.
