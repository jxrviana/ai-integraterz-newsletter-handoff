## 1. EPA Just Un-Killed Your R-410A Inventory

- **Category:** REFRIGERANT
- **Source:** NAHB (National Association of Home Builders) — "EPA Finalizes Refrigerant Rule Update to Allow Older HVAC Unit Installation"
  https://www.nahb.org/blog/2026/05/epa-hvac-refrigerants-r-410a-final-rule
- **Corroborating source:** Holland & Knight — "EPA Finalizes Changes to Technology Transitions Provisions"
  https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/epa-finalizes-changes-to-technology-transitions-provisions
- **Date:** May 26, 2026 (both)

**TL;DR raw material**
- EPA published a final rule allowing continued installation of HVAC units manufactured or imported before January 1, 2025 that use R-410A. (NAHB)
- The rule becomes effective **July 27, 2026**. (NAHB)
- Previously contractors faced a **January 1, 2026** deadline prohibiting installation of older R-410A split systems; the amended rule removes that hard stop and permits installation until existing supplies are exhausted. (NAHB)
- EPA had already noted the deadline would be "deprioritized in response to concerns on the availability and cost of A2L refrigerant in the field." (NAHB)
- No new R-410A units are being manufactured, so supplies will deplete; R-410A production is being phased down to **15% of current output by 2036**. (NAHB)
- **New York State maintains its own January 1, 2026 deadline** — R-410A systems cannot be installed there. (NAHB)
- Holland & Knight confirms: the deadline for installing new residential/light-commercial AC or heat pump systems was removed for legacy pre-2025 equipment. Related extensions: supermarket refrigeration and cold storage warehouses pushed to January 1, 2032; semiconductor manufacturing to January 1, 2030; refrigerated lab equipment to January 1, 2028. Projected savings: **$976 million in engineering costs**.

**Why it matters (ANALYSIS):** Any owner sitting on R-410A stock just got that inventory's value restored — and any owner who dumped it at a discount in late 2025 ate a loss that did not have to happen. It also means the A2L transition is now a competitive choice rather than a legal cliff, so the shops that already trained and tooled up have to decide whether to keep charging the premium or defend share against holdouts still installing legacy gear.

**Pillar:** 1 — Regulation and compliance

## 2. Seven Makers, 90% Of The Market, One Lawsuit

- **Category:** REFRIGERANT / LEGAL
- **Source:** Facilities Dive — "HVAC manufacturers face second suit over price-fixing allegation" (Robert Freedman, Lead Editor)
  https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/hvac-manufacturers-face-second-suit-over-price-fixing-allegation/819562/
- **Corroborating source:** Cooling Post — "US manufacturers face price-fixing lawsuit"
  https://www.coolingpost.com/world-news/us-manufacturers-face-price-fixing-lawsuit/
- **Date:** May 7, 2026 (Facilities Dive); April 1, 2026 (Cooling Post)

**TL;DR raw material**
- A second suit was filed in **April 2026** in Michigan federal court by **Richard Isom, owner of Air Tech Services, a Florida HVAC contractor** — i.e. a contractor plaintiff, not just consumers. Seeks class certification. (Facilities Dive)
- The first suit was filed **March 2026** in the same court by a consumer plaintiff (Alyssa Berg, Minnesota). (Facilities Dive; Cooling Post)
- Defendants: **Bosch, Carrier, Trane, Daikin, Lennox, Rheem, and AAON** — controlling approximately **90% of the U.S. HVAC market** through subsidiaries. (Facilities Dive). Cooling Post's longer defendant list adds Johnson Controls entities, Mitsubishi Electric Trane, Viessmann, Allied Air Enterprises, Basx and Heat Transfer Products Group.
- Alleged conspiracy period: beginning **no later than January 1, 2020**. (Facilities Dive; Cooling Post)
- Alleged price impact: over six years, commercial and residential systems cost **8% more** than in a competitive market — "billions of dollars extracted from direct purchasers." (Facilities Dive)
- Evidence cited in the complaint: **40 documented instances of synchronized price increases**, public announcements made via the ACHR News trade publication, and data sharing through AHRI. (Facilities Dive; Cooling Post)
- Manufacturer responses — Carrier: "We deny the baseless allegations…and will fight it vigorously." Trane: disputed the "baseless claims" and pledged a vigorous defense. (Facilities Dive)

**Why it matters (ANALYSIS):** The named plaintiff in the second case is an HVAC contractor, which means dealers and installers — not just homeowners — may end up as class members with a claim on overcharges paid since 2020. Separately, an allegation that manufacturers signaled price moves through the trade press and the industry's own institute is the kind of thing that changes how an owner reads the next price-increase letter.

## 3. DOE Moves To Freeze Future Efficiency Mandates

- **Category:** REGULATION
- **Source:** Utility Dive — "DOE wants to 'permanently end' appliance efficiency requirements" (Robert Walton, Senior Editor)
  https://www.utilitydive.com/news/doe-proposes-rule-permanently-end-appliance-mandates/824334/
- **Date:** July 2, 2026

**TL;DR raw material**
- DOE issued a **notice of proposed rulemaking on July 2, 2026**, announcing plans to "permanently end home appliance and equipment mandates," framed as a methodological update. (Utility Dive)
- It changes the **process** for developing future energy conservation standards — it does not repeal existing ones. Changes include altered test procedures, a new definition of "significant energy savings," and new economic thresholds. (Utility Dive)
- Comment deadlines: **30 days** for the NOPR after Federal Register publication; **60 days** for a separate request for information on analytical methodologies. (Utility Dive)
- Equipment in scope includes **furnaces** among other home appliances. (Utility Dive)
- Andrew deLaski, executive director of the Appliance Standards Awareness Project, called it an "obstacle course of restrictions" hindering DOE's congressional mandate. (Utility Dive)
- The Association of Home Appliance Manufacturers endorsed it; CEO Kelly Mariotti: "The natural next step is for Congress to act—to amend the Energy Policy and Conservation Act." (Utility Dive)
- Assistant Secretary Audrey Robertson framed the proposal around "affordability" and "consumer choice." (Utility Dive)

**Why it matters (ANALYSIS):** For a decade the planning assumption has been that minimum efficiency only ratchets upward, which is what justified stocking and selling up the efficiency ladder. If future ratchets get procedurally throttled, the premium-efficiency upsell loses some of its regulatory tailwind and has to stand on payback math alone.

**Pillar:** 1 — Regulation and compliance

## 4. Tariffs Are Now The Line Item Nobody Budgeted

- **Category:** OPERATIONS
- **Source:** ACCA HVAC Blog — "HVAC tariffs: What contractors can do about prices going up"
  https://hvac-blog.acca.org/hvac-tariffs-what-contractors-can-do-about-prices-going-up/
- **Corroborating source:** Money — "What's Causing HVAC Prices to Rise in 2026" (Liliana Hall; editor Julia Glum)
  https://money.com/whats-causing-hvac-prices-to-rise-in-2026-and-how-much-more-you-should-expect-to-pay/
- **Date:** May 18, 2026 (ACCA); June 5, 2026 (Money)

**TL;DR raw material**
- **50% tariff** on items made entirely or almost entirely of steel, aluminum, and copper. (ACCA)
- **25% flat tariff** on imported HVACR equipment substantially made of those materials. (ACCA)
- Mexican-made equipment: tariff rate jumped from roughly **8% to roughly 25%**. (ACCA)
- Chinese imports: Section 232 and Section 301 tariffs combined at **30% or higher**. (ACCA)
- ACCA's operational counter-move, with a number attached: contractors who offer financing on **every** job see **35% financed sales** versus **17%** for those who present it selectively. (ACCA)
- National average HVAC system cost is approximately **$7,500**, with a typical homeowner range of **$5,000 to $28,000**; pre-2020 systems were "well under $10,000." (Money)
- Money attributes rising prices to four drivers: the AIM Act refrigerant transition, tariffs on steel/aluminum/copper, the skilled technician shortage, and supply chain disruption.

**Why it matters (ANALYSIS):** Tariff exposure now varies by where a given brand builds, which turns supplier selection into a margin decision rather than a loyalty one. The ACCA financing figure is the more actionable half: presenting payment options on every estimate rather than selectively roughly doubles the financed-sale rate, which is a process change, not a price change.

**Pillar:** 4 — Business operations and economics

## 8. HVAC Is The Most Booked Trade And The Slowest To Answer

- **Category:** DEMAND
- **Source:** Jobber — 2026 Home Service Trends Report (**VENDOR RESEARCH — methodology disclosed**)
  https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/
- **Supporting source:** Jobber HVAC Industry Trends (source-attributed data table)
  https://www.getjobber.com/academy/hvac/hvac-industry-trends/
- **Date:** Survey fielded December 2025; trends page updated April 29–30, 2026

**Methodology (disclosed):** 1,050 home service business owners surveyed via Conjointly, December 2025, ±3 percentage points at 90% confidence, supplemented with aggregated anonymized Jobber platform data.

**TL;DR raw material**
- **Only 17% of HVAC businesses reply to leads within one hour — the slowest of any industry surveyed.** (Jobber Trends Report)
- HVAC pros are **"the most booked"** among all trades, with many reporting limited availability or turning work away. (Jobber Trends Report)
- **83% of HVAC businesses raised prices last year — the highest rate across trades**; 71% report confidence in their pricing. (Jobber Trends Report)
- Over **60% of HVAC businesses earn $500K or more**; **14% exceed $2M**. Nearly **40% expect significant revenue growth in 2026**. (Jobber Trends Report)
- **81.5% of HVAC businesses use AI**, the highest adoption rate of any trade — mostly for quoting, invoicing, and communication. Only **40%** use it for scheduling and dispatch. (Jobber Trends Report / Jobber HVAC trends)
- Top lead sources across home service: repeat customers **51%**, word-of-mouth referrals **44%**, Google Search **36%**, Facebook **28%**, lead-gen sites (Angi, Thumbtack) **26%**, Google Local Services Ads **21%**. (Jobber)
- Customer selection criteria: quality **65%**, price **56%**, reviews **49%**. (Jobber)

**Why it matters (ANALYSIS):** These two findings sit in direct tension and that tension is the story: the trade with the most demand pressure is also the worst at responding to it, because being booked solid is exactly what makes a shop slow to pick up the phone. The AI adoption split reinforces it — HVAC leads every trade in using AI for paperwork and lags in using it for the dispatch and response layer where the lost revenue actually is.

## 9. Homeowners Will Pay More For Speed — And Wait Until It Breaks

- **Category:** DEMAND
- **Source:** Contracting Business — "Aging Homes Drive HVAC and Repair Spending in 2026, Housecall Pro Survey Finds"
  https://www.contractingbusiness.com/industry-news/news/55377868/aging-homes-drive-hvac-and-repair-spending-in-2026-housecall-pro-survey-finds
- **Corroborating source:** The Hardwire News — "The 2026 HVAC Financing Gap"
  https://www.thehardwirenews.com/hvac-financing-contractor-payment-plans-2026/
- **Date:** May 14, 2026 (Contracting Business); June 11, 2026 (Hardwire)
- **VENDOR RESEARCH FLAG:** Underlying data is Housecall Pro's "The State of Home Service Spending" report — a field-service software vendor. Methodology disclosed: 1,100+ U.S. homeowners, adults 18+, pre-targeted as homeowners and screened as primary or joint decision-maker for household maintenance, balanced by age, gender and region against U.S. Census benchmarks. Reported here via two independent trade outlets rather than the vendor's own page.

**TL;DR raw material**
- **72% would pay more for emergency resolution within 24 hours.** (Contracting Business)
- **58% contact contractors after an emergency system failure**; **51% delay calling until minor issues worsen**; **41% reported postponing repairs that later became costlier**. (Contracting Business)
- **79% plan to repair or replace at least one major home system in 2026**; **20%** name HVAC specifically. Plumbing 28%, appliances 25%, exterior 23%, electrical 18%, roofing 14%. (Contracting Business)
- **77% say rising costs cause delays or reduced project scope**; **68% seek additional quotes when pricing exceeds expectations**; **62% say financing options encourage them to move forward**. (Contracting Business; the 62% also appears in Hardwire)

**Why it matters (ANALYSIS):** Half the market is deliberately waiting for failure, and three-quarters of them will pay a premium to be rescued inside 24 hours — which means after-hours capacity is a pricing lever, not just a service cost. The 68%-seek-more-quotes figure is the counterweight: whoever answers first is quoting against two or three others, so speed only converts if the payment option is on the table in the same conversation.

## 11. Washington Is About To Pay For Your Next Apprentice

- **Category:** LABOR
- **Source:** ACCA HVAC Blog — "Workforce Pell Grants: an HVAC contractor's guide"
  https://hvac-blog.acca.org/workforce-pell-grants-an-hvac-contractors-guide/
- **Corroborating source:** ACCA HVAC Blog — "New federal bill could slash your HVACR training costs by 50%"
  https://hvac-blog.acca.org/new-federal-bill-could-slash-your-hvacr-training-costs-by-50/
- **Date:** June 17, 2026 (Pell); April 29, 2026 (bill)

**TL;DR raw material**
- Workforce Pell expands federal financial aid eligibility to **short-term training programs**, including HVACR — traditionally Pell was limited to longer-term degree programs. (ACCA)
- **Federal regulations were finalized May 18, 2026; the program launches summer 2026 (July).** States determine which specific programs qualify, and implementation will be gradual — few programs qualify immediately at launch. (ACCA)
- Separately, the **Employer-Directed Skills Act (H.R. 4049 / S. 3846)** would provide **up to 90% federal reimbursement** for training costs; amounts vary and are determined locally based on funding availability. (ACCA)
- Key limitation: it applies **only to new employees, not upskilling current staff**. Employers must commit to hiring individuals upon successful training completion, and participants must be employer-selected and approved by local workforce boards. (ACCA)
- Status: **not yet voted on**. Similar versions have been introduced before without passage. WIOA reauthorization, which could carry it, passed the House 378-26 in 2024 but stalled in the Senate. (ACCA)
- ACCA's recommended contractor actions: sign ACCA's WIOA Action Alert, engage local training providers to shape program standards, and build contractor-to-institution pipelines. (ACCA)

**Why it matters (ANALYSIS):** Workforce Pell is real and live this month; the 90% reimbursement bill is not law and should not be planned around. The practical move is the unglamorous one ACCA names — get in front of your state's program-approval process now, because states decide which local programs qualify and shops with existing school relationships will shape that list.

**Why it matters (ANALYSIS):** The residential replacement market is being deferred, not destroyed — homeowners are stretching equipment life and buying down the efficiency ladder while commercial books records.
