## CANDIDATE ITEMS

### 1. The coaching profession crossed $5.34B and 122,974 coaches, and it is crowding fast
- **Summary:** The ICF/PwC 2025 Global Coaching Study puts worldwide coaching revenue at $5.34 billion with 122,974 practitioners, a 15% jump in coach count since 2023 (which itself was up 54% on 2019). More coaches are entering every year, which is exactly why undifferentiated "I coach men's health" positioning gets harder to sell.
- **Why it's valuable to a coach-operator:** It reframes a growth problem as a positioning problem. The room is expanding and filling at the same time, so the operators who win are the ones who name a specific man and a specific outcome, not the generalists.
- **Source URL:** https://coachingfederation.org/blog/coaching-industry-continues-global-growth-with-5-34-billion-usd-revenue-new-research-reveals/
- **Source-quality note:** A2. Industry body's own study, conducted by PwC (10,000+ participants, 127 countries), published Sept 16, 2025. Primary source, freshest number in this set. Scope caveat: this is ALL professional coaching (executive, life, business, health blended), not a men's-health-coaching market figure. Do not present it as the size of the men's-health niche.
- **Suggested use:** Data point / cold-open stat with a positioning hook.

### 2. A coach who repackaged $95/month into a $1,500 twelve-week program
- **Summary:** Coach Jim Gazzale moved off a low-cost $95/month model to a structured $1,500 per-twelve-weeks premium program built around a defined outcome, and reports adding four to five new clients a month. The change was the package and the price frame, not more marketing spend.
- **Why it's valuable to a coach-operator:** It is a concrete, copyable pricing-and-packaging move with a before/after a reader can act on this week. Selling a finite transformation at a real price beats renting access by the month, and it filters for committed clients.
- **Source URL:** https://theptdc.com/how-to-make-money-as-online-trainer
- **Source-quality note:** C3. The PTDC (Personal Trainer Development Center) is a credible fitness-business publication; author Lou Schuler is a named, established writer. The result is the coach's own self-report, and the article is pandemic-era (references 2020). Pricing psychology is a slow-moving craft principle, so the age is acceptable; the number is a single self-reported data point, so present the mechanism, not the exact revenue, as the lesson.
- **Suggested use:** Case-study story / pricing-and-packaging tactic.

### 3. Top coaching operators hit $300+ per client per month, and here is how
- **Summary:** Two-Brain Business, which tracks revenue metrics across thousands of coaching gyms, reports that top operators consistently earn $300+ ARM (average revenue per client per month), versus a market where many undercharge. The highest-ARM operators start every client with a long-form assessment or motivational interview and a one-on-one on-ramp of eight sessions to six weeks, then layer in premium services.
- **Why it's valuable to a coach-operator:** It gives a specific dollar benchmark to price against and a named mechanism to reach it. Raising revenue per existing client is faster than chasing new leads, and the on-ramp doubles as the retention driver in item 4.
- **Source URL:** https://twobrainbusiness.com/numbers-2/
- **Source-quality note:** C2. Two-Brain (founder Chris Cooper) runs the largest microgym-business mentorship and publishes an annual State of the Industry report drawn from 10,000+ gyms; this ARM article is dated Oct 13, 2020 and cites their internal Dashboard plus partners (Wodify, Arbox) without disclosing the sample on the page. Context is gym/microgym, which transfers to coaching but is not identical. Benchmark is business-metric, slow-moving; flag the 2020 vintage.
- **Suggested use:** Data point + tactic (raising client value).

### 4. 87% of well-onboarded clients are still there at six months
- **Summary:** Dr. Paul Bedford, the fitness industry's "Retention Guru," found in his One Million Strong study that 87% of members onboarded correctly remained active after six months; his broader research base spans millions of members across thousands of clubs. Effective onboarding means goal-setting at the start and structured check-ins through the first 90 days (30, 60, 90 days), not a welcome email.
- **Why it's valuable to a coach-operator:** Retention is the cheapest growth there is, and this names the exact 90-day window and the check-in cadence that protects it. For reference benchmarks, personal-training studios run around 80% annual retention and health clubs around 71% (AFS/IHRSA), so a deliberate onboarding system is a real edge, not table stakes.
- **Source URL:** https://www.glofox.com/blog/gym-onboarding/ (reports the figure and attributes it to Bedford's study)
- **Source-quality note:** C2. The 87% traces to a named researcher (Dr. Paul Bedford) with a large dataset, corroborated across multiple industry outlets. Caveat: I am citing the reported figure via Glofox (dated Nov 12, 2020), not the primary study PDF, which I did not open. Retention benchmarks from AFS/IHRSA (studios 75.9%, PT studios 80%, clubs 71.4%) are undated on their page, so treat those as directional. See https://member.afsfitness.com/content/why-your-retention-rate-key-understanding-fitness-business-success
- **Suggested use:** Data point + retention tactic. Pairs with item 3 as the flagship story (see Analyst's Read).

### 5. The group-challenge model that let one coach serve 340+ clients across 19 countries
- **Summary:** Coach Gil Mesina runs group challenges roughly four times a year, about 30 clients per group (up from 20), and has trained 340-plus clients across 19 countries using automated systems and word-of-mouth from before/after photos. The group format is how he scaled past the one-to-one time ceiling without hiring a large team.
- **Why it's valuable to a coach-operator:** It is a documented path out of the solo trap: cohort-based delivery multiplies revenue per hour and turns client results into the acquisition engine. For a men's-health coach, a recurring "8-week reset" cohort maps cleanly onto this.
- **Source URL:** https://theptdc.com/how-to-make-money-as-online-trainer
- **Source-quality note:** C3. Same source as item 2 (PTDC, Lou Schuler, named coach, self-reported figures, ~2020). Client counts are the coach's own; present the model as the takeaway rather than the specific totals as proof.
- **Suggested use:** Case-study story / scaling-past-solo tactic.

### 6. What coaches actually charge, and what they actually earn
- **Summary:** Precision Nutrition's income data puts typical pricing at $100-200 per monthly client, with successful coaches carrying 50-100+ clients at once; part-time coaching (1-5 hours/week) runs $450-1,550/month and full-time (30-40 hours/week) $2,300-6,000+/month. It is a rare public pricing benchmark from a company that has certified over 100,000 coaches.
- **Why it's valuable to a coach-operator:** It anchors pricing conversations in real ranges instead of guesswork, and the client-load numbers expose the ceiling that pushes coaches toward group or premium models (items 3 and 5).
- **Source URL:** https://www.precisionnutrition.com/nutrition-coaching-how-much-money-can-you-really-make
- **Source-quality note:** C3, with a vendor caveat. Precision Nutrition is a genuine domain expert, but it sells the certification these coaches buy, so it has an incentive. The page says the figures come from "data collected from hundreds of nutrition coaches" and "our survey of recent graduates" but publishes no sample size, method, or date, which caps credibility at 3. Present as directional benchmarks, not census data.
- **Suggested use:** Data point (pricing/packaging). Label the source as the certifier's own survey.

### 7. A coach who built a $600K practice serving busy professional men, with a four-person team
- **Summary:** Tobias Young, an IFBB Pro, runs an online coaching business focused on helping business professionals and entrepreneurs transform their bodies, reporting roughly $600K/year (about $18K/month) with a four-person team built over 20+ years. It is a documented example of a male-focused coach who scaled past solo by hiring, not just by working more hours.
- **Why it's valuable to a coach-operator:** The audience match is close: busy professional men are the men's-health coach's buyer. It shows a realistic, non-viral path (small team, long runway) rather than an overnight number, which is more credible to a discerning reader.
- **Source URL:** https://www.starterstory.com/ideas/online-fitness-coach/success-stories
- **Source-quality note:** C3. Starter Story publishes direct founder interviews, so the figures are first-person self-reported with no external audit. Use the shape of the story (male-professional niche, small team, staged growth); treat the exact revenue as self-reported. Prefer this over flashier "$500 to $984K in a year" entries on the same page, which are too good to present in a credibility-first newsletter.
- **Suggested use:** Case-study story / scaling-past-solo, male-audience match.

### 8. Specializing narrowed the market and widened the margin
- **Summary:** Coach Nelao Nengola exited unprofitable in-person training and rebuilt around a specific specialization and a fully online, location-independent model, ending up with clients across three continents. Narrowing the "who" is what made the business portable and premium, not generalizing to serve everyone.
- **Why it's valuable to a coach-operator:** Positioning is the lever the ICF data (item 1) says matters most in a crowding market. A men's-health coach who picks a specific man (for example, fathers over 40 rebuilding after burnout) can charge more and market cheaper because the message finally lands on someone.
- **Source URL:** https://theptdc.com/how-to-make-money-as-online-trainer
- **Source-quality note:** C3. Same PTDC source and caveats as items 2 and 5; this item is lighter on hard numbers and carries the positioning principle rather than a metric. Pair it with the ICF market data for a data-backed "why specialize" argument.
- **Suggested use:** Tactic (positioning/niche). Lightest on numbers in the set; use for the argument, not for a stat.

## ANALYST'S READ (the one cross-corroborated finding)

**Structured, high-touch onboarding is very likely the highest-leverage single move available to a solo men's-health coach, because it raises retention and revenue-per-client at once.** Confidence: Moderate-to-High. Two independent sources converge on the same mechanism: Bedford's retention research (87% of well-onboarded members active at 6 months) and Two-Brain's multi-thousand-gym dataset (top operators reach $300+ ARM by opening with a long assessment on-ramp of eight sessions to six weeks). Independent origin, same conclusion, which is real corroboration rather than an echo.

Why not High: both featured figures date to 2020 and originate in the gym/club context, not men's-health coaching specifically, and I cite a secondary report of Bedford's study rather than the primary PDF. What would raise it to High: the primary Bedford study document, plus one men's-health-coaching-specific dataset showing the same onboarding effect. This is the story I would build the issue around, because it is actionable, corroborated, and ties three items together (3, 4, and the pricing logic in 6).
