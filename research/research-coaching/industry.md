## BLUF

The men's-health market is being de-risked, expanded, and invaded at the same time, and all three moves are documented in real regulatory and financial primary sources from the last 18 months. The FDA stripped the heart-attack boxed warning off testosterone (Feb 2025, A-grade primary), an FDA expert panel then recommended opening testosterone to age-related low-T and dropping its controlled-substance status (Dec 2025, multi-source), and Hims launched FDA-approved oral testosterone on the way to $2.35B in 2025 revenue (company primary). Meanwhile the cheap compounded-GLP-1 pipeline that a lot of programs ran on was shut off by the FDA (2025), peptides like BPC-157 just cleared an FDA advisory-committee vote toward legal compounding again (July 2026), and brick-and-mortar TRT franchises are consolidating local markets (Gameday: 400+ clinics). Every load-bearing number below traces to a primary or reputable trade source; the popular "11 million prescriptions / 50% surge" stat does NOT survive tracing, so it is excluded and flagged in Gaps.

**Strongest three for the issue:** Item 1 (FDA drops the heart-risk warning), Item 3 (Hims sells oral TRT, $2.35B company), Item 2 (FDA panel wants to expand who qualifies). Together they are one story: the product got safer on paper, the eligible market got bigger, and a $2B competitor walked in.

## The candidate items

### Item 1 — The FDA took the heart-attack warning off testosterone
- **Headline (working):** "The Boxed Warning Is Gone: What the FDA's Testosterone Reversal Means for Your Intake Call"
- **Summary:** In February 2025 the FDA ordered a class-wide labeling change removing the boxed warning about increased cardiovascular risk from every testosterone product, citing the TRAVERSE trial, which found similar heart-event rates on testosterone versus placebo (7% vs 7.3%). The agency replaced it with a narrower blood-pressure warning, and experts including Cleveland Clinic's Steven Nissen still flagged higher rates of atrial fibrillation (3.5% vs 2.4%) and nonfatal arrhythmias in the trial.
- **Why it's in the issue:** The number-one objection a coach hears ("isn't TRT going to give me a heart attack?") just lost its federal warning label. That changes the intake conversation, but the honest operator also knows the AFib signal did not vanish, and saying so builds trust the DTC ads skip.
- **Source:** https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-issues-class-wide-labeling-changes-testosterone-products (primary) · corroboration: https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20250710/removal-of-boxed-warning-clears-testosterone-of-heartrelated-risk-but-concerns-remain
- **Source quality:** A-grade regulator primary, corroborated by medical trade press and Urology Times/Pharmacy Times reporting the same change independently.
- **Suggested use:** LEAD STORY.
- **Calibration:** Almost certain the change happened (High confidence, FDA primary). The lingering arrhythmia signal is real and should be stated, not buried.

### Item 2 — An FDA panel wants to open TRT to age-related low-T and deschedule it
- **Headline (working):** "The FDA Is Quietly Redrawing Who Counts as a Testosterone Patient"
- **Summary:** In December 2025 an FDA expert panel of urologists and endocrinologists recommended expanding the testosterone indication beyond "classical hypogonadism" to men with symptoms and documented low levels regardless of cause, and separately urged removing testosterone's Schedule III controlled-substance status. Earlier, in April 2025, the FDA had already asked manufacturers to seek an expanded indication for low libido in idiopathic hypogonadism.
- **Why it's in the issue:** If the label expands to age-related low-T, the addressable population stops being a narrow disease group and becomes millions of ordinary aging men, which is exactly the coach's client. Descheduling would cut prescribing friction. Neither is final yet, and a coach who understands that the panel is a recommendation, not a rule, sounds more credible than one who calls it done.
- **Source:** https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20251210/fda-panel-supports-expanding-population-indicated-for-testosterone-therapy · corroboration: https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-panel-calls-for-easing-restrictions-on-testost.html and https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-asks-testosterone-therapy-firms-to-seek-expanded-indication.html
- **Source quality:** B-grade, two independent specialist outlets (Healio, RAPS) reporting the same December 2025 panel plus the separate April 2025 FDA request.
- **Suggested use:** TREND PIECE (forward-looking).
- **Calibration:** Almost certain the panel made the recommendation (High). Whether the FDA formally expands the label or deschedules: likely over time, not yet decided (Moderate confidence, it is a non-binding panel).

### Item 3 — Hims is now selling FDA-approved oral testosterone, on the way to $2.35B
- **Headline (working):** "Your New Competitor Is a $2 Billion Public Company With a Testosterone Pill"
- **Summary:** On September 10, 2025 Hims launched a men's-health testosterone category built around KYZATREX, an FDA-approved oral testosterone from partner Marius Pharmaceuticals, alongside compounded enclomiphene and injectables slated for 2026, and its release cites "an estimated twenty million men in the U.S." with low testosterone. Hims reported full-year 2025 revenue of $2.35B, up 59% year over year, with net income of $128.4M.
- **Why it's in the issue:** The DTC giants are no longer just doing ED pills, they are doing TRT with a real FDA-approved oral product and a nine-figure ad budget behind it. A coach's differentiation cannot be "access to testosterone" anymore; it has to be the coaching, the labs read in context, and the accountability Hims cannot deliver at that scale.
- **Source:** https://investors.hims.com/news/news-details/2025/Hims-Launches-New-Category-in-Mens-Health-with-Exclusive-Branded-Oral-Testosterone-and-Expanded-Personalized-Treatments/default.aspx (launch) · https://investors.hims.com/news/news-details/2026/Hims--Hers-Health-Inc--Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx (financials)
- **Source quality:** A-grade company primary (investor relations / financial reporting). Note: the "twenty million men" figure is Hims's own stated estimate, and $2.35B is total company revenue (men's + women's + weight loss), not a testosterone-only number.
- **Suggested use:** LEAD or HOUSE-ADJACENT competition story (pairs naturally with the card-3 house block on why a coach beats a pill subscription).
- **Calibration:** Almost certain (High confidence, company financial reporting).

### Item 4 — The FDA shut off the cheap compounded-GLP-1 pipeline
- **Headline (working):** "The $200 Semaglutide Era Is Over: What the GLP-1 Compounding Shutdown Did to Metabolic Programs"
- **Summary:** After declaring the tirzepatide shortage resolved in December 2024 and semaglutide in February 2025, the FDA ended enforcement discretion for compounding both drugs, with the last deadlines passing by May 2025. Compounded versions commonly ran $100 to $300 a month; brand-name GLP-1s can exceed $1,000 a month, and drugmakers sent cease-and-desist letters to telehealth sellers.
- **Why it's in the issue:** Any men's-health or metabolic coach whose weight-loss track leaned on cheap compounded semaglutide just lost the price point that made it work. The clients are still there and still want the outcome, so the opening is a program that is not built on a compounded-drug arbitrage that regulators can close overnight.
- **Source:** https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-clarifies-policies-compounders-national-glp-1-supply-begins-stabilize (primary) · corroboration: https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/fda-resolves-semaglutide-shortage
- **Source quality:** A-grade regulator primary, corroborated by a law-firm regulatory advisory and NCPA/Pharmacy Times reporting the same deadlines.
- **Suggested use:** DATA POINT / market-shift story.
- **Calibration:** Almost certain (High confidence, FDA primary plus multiple legal advisories).

### Item 5 — Testosterone use is climbing fastest among YOUNG men
- **Headline (working):** "It's Not Just 55-Year-Olds Anymore: Testosterone Use Jumped 120% in Men Under 24"
- **Summary:** A peer-reviewed PLOS ONE analysis of prescription-drug-monitoring data from 25 states found testosterone-treated patients rose 27% overall between 2018 and 2022 (from about 1.22M to 1.66M in the reporting states), with the steepest jumps in the youngest cohorts: up 120% under age 24, 86% for ages 25 to 34, and 58% for ages 35 to 44. Roughly three-quarters of the 35-to-44 growth happened after 2020.
- **Why it's in the issue:** The testosterone client is getting younger and is walking in framed around "optimization" and performance, not disease. That is a different sale and a different duty of care than a 60-year-old with classic symptoms, and the coach who names that shift out loud sounds like they actually read the data.
- **Source:** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0309160
- **Source quality:** B-grade peer-reviewed primary (PLOS ONE, published Aug 2024). Scope caveat: "people treated" in 25 reporting states, not a national prescription count.
- **Suggested use:** DATA POINT (the striking-number card).
- **Calibration:** High confidence in the 2018-2022 trend (peer-reviewed). Likely (Moderate) that the direction has continued into 2026, given the DTC expansion in Items 2 and 3.

### Item 6 — BPC-157 and other peptides just cleared an FDA advisory-committee vote
- **Headline (working):** "Peptides Get a Real Regulatory Path Back, With an Asterisk"
- **Summary:** On July 23, 2026 the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted to recommend allowing compounding of BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, and MOTS-c, with more peptides voted on the following day. The vote is non-binding and the FDA still decides, and STAT reported that a majority of the panelists who voted yes have ties to the peptide industry.
- **Why it's in the issue:** Peptides sit in a gray zone that a lot of coaches refer clients into without knowing the actual legal status. The honest read for the issue: a real compounding pathway may be reopening, it is a recommendation and not a rule yet, and the conflict-of-interest flag is part of the story, not a footnote to hide.
- **Source:** https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/23/fda-panel-okays-peptides-compound-pharmacies-bpc-157-kpv/ · context: https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/the-peptide-reclassification-everyone-s-talking-about-a-pharmacist-s-take-on-what-rfk-jr-s-announcement-actually-means
- **Source quality:** B-grade (STAT News, respected health journalism) for the July 2026 vote; the earlier "14 of 19 peptides" framing traces to a Feb 2026 HHS announcement and should be labeled pending, not final.
- **Suggested use:** TREND / regulatory piece.
- **Calibration:** Almost certain the committee voted as reported (High). Roughly even chance to likely that the FDA finalizes reclassification on the recommended peptides (Moderate confidence, non-binding vote, industry-tied panel).

### Item 7 — Men's-health clinics are franchising at unusual speed
- **Headline (working):** "The TRT Franchise on the Next Exit: How Brick-and-Mortar Is Carving Up Your Local Market"
- **Summary:** Forbes reported in February 2026 that Gameday Men's Health, which began franchising in 2022, operates 400+ clinics across 48 states and Canada with 1,000+ locations in the pipeline and no reported closures. The in-person model bundles TRT, peptides like sermorelin, GAINSWave for ED, vitamin injections, and medical weight loss, pitched on same-day labs and immediate treatment.
- **Why it's in the issue:** A coach's local market is being colonized by standardized clinics with same-day-lab convenience and franchise marketing budgets. The counter is not speed, it is the relationship and the ongoing coaching a walk-in-clinic transaction does not include, which is worth naming explicitly for the operator reading.
- **Source:** https://www.forbes.com/sites/tanyaakim/2026/02/20/the-mens-wellness-clinics-scaling-a-new-concierge-like-model/
- **Source quality:** C-grade. Forbes contributor byline (lower editorial control than Forbes staff), and the clinic-count figures are company-provided; treat as directional, not audited.
- **Suggested use:** TREND piece on competitive dynamics.
- **Calibration:** Likely accurate in direction (Moderate confidence, single outlet relaying company-provided numbers). Verify the exact clinic count before quoting it as fact.

### Item 8 — Anyone can now buy a glucose monitor without a prescription
- **Headline (working):** "Your Client Already Bought a CGM at CVS: The OTC Metabolic-Data Wave"
- **Summary:** The FDA cleared Dexcom's Stelo in March 2024 as the first over-the-counter continuous glucose monitor, and Abbott's Lingo followed (cleared June 2024, priced at $49 for a 14-day sensor), both aimed at people without diabetes for general wellness. Dexcom guided to roughly $40M in Stelo sales by the end of 2024, and CGM use among non-diabetics is a documented growth trend.
- **Why it's in the issue:** Metabolic-health clients now show up already wearing a sensor and holding two weeks of their own glucose curves. That is a cheap new on-ramp for a coach and a more data-literate client, but it also means the coaching has to interpret the data, not just recommend the device.
- **Source:** https://www.medtechdive.com/news/abbott-lingo-rollout-us-otc-cgm/726330/ · https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-sells-stelo-over-the-counter-cgm/725310/
- **Source quality:** B-grade specialist trade press (MedTech Dive), product facts and pricing verifiable against the vendors' own pages.
- **Suggested use:** DATA POINT / adjacent-demand story (diversifies the issue away from pure TRT).
- **Calibration:** High confidence on the product facts and pricing. Moderate on the scale of non-diabetic adoption (directional trend, not a hard installed-base number).

## Gaps and caveats (what was NOT verified, and what would change the picture)

- **No verified top-line market-size dollar figure.** The TRT-market and men's-health-market figures circulating online ($2.03B to $2.73B TRT; $1.42T to $2.88T "men's health & wellness") come from GlobeNewswire / ResearchAndMarkets / MarketIntelo press-release projections. These are D-grade, definitionally bloated (the trillion-dollar figure folds in food, tourism, and personal care), and were excluded as load-bearing. If Justin wants a headline market-size number, it needs a paid IBISWorld/Grand View report or a flagged `[OWNER: buy/verify market-size figure]`.
- **The popular "11 million testosterone prescriptions in 2024 / 50% surge since 2019" stat did not survive tracing.** It traces only to content-mill pages (singlecare, wewillcure), not a primary. It was replaced with the peer-reviewed PLOS ONE figures (Item 5). Do not print the 11M number without a primary source.
- **Items 2 and 6 are recommendations, not final FDA rules.** The testosterone-indication expansion, descheduling, and peptide reclassification are all pending. Writing them as done would be a factual error and an easy one for a sharp reader to catch.
- **Hims does not break out testosterone-specific revenue.** The $2.35B is total company revenue; do not attribute it to men's health alone.
- **Item 7's clinic counts are company-provided via a Forbes contributor.** Directional, not audited. Verify the exact number before it goes in a headline.
- **The FDA testosterone-labeling page returned a bot block on direct fetch;** its content is confirmed via the FDA search index plus three independent trade reports (Healio, Urology Times, Pharmacy Times), so the A-grade holds, but the specific page did not render for us at write time.
- **Minor date ambiguity on the Dec 2025 FDA testosterone panel** (reported around Dec 10-12, 2025); cited as "December 2025."
- **What would most change this brief:** a final FDA action on Item 2 or Item 6 (turns "likely" into "confirmed"), or a Hims testosterone-segment revenue disclosure (would give a real dollar size to the competitive threat).
