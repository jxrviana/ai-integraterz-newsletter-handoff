## 1. Your Cost To Win A Customer Just Jumped 40%

- **Category:** SALES
- **Source:** Wood Mackenzie — "US residential solar customer acquisition costs set to spike 40% in 2026 before gradual decline"
- **URL:** https://www.woodmac.com/news/opinion/us-residential-solar-customer-acquisition-costs-set-to-spike-40-in-2026-before-gradual-decline/
- **Date:** March 25, 2026 (author: Max Issokson, Research Analyst, US Distributed Solar)
- **Verified:** Fetched successfully

**TL;DR raw material**
- 2025 residential customer acquisition cost (CAC): **$0.60/W** — described as a five-year low
- 2026 forecast CAC: **$0.84/W** — a **40% year-over-year increase**
- Residential market contraction forecast in this piece: **-19% for 2026** (note: Wood Mackenzie's later June 10, 2026 press release revised this to **-21%** — cite whichever, but date it)
- Three stated drivers: (1) Section 25D 30% federal credit expired Dec 2025, removing the incentive that drove the 2025 demand surge; (2) shrinking addressable market means more installers chasing fewer qualified buyers; (3) transition costs as companies build customer-lifetime-value models — battery integration, EV chargers, referral systems
- Stated channel shift: away from outsourced/dealer sales toward **vertically integrated in-house customer acquisition**, to cut third-party dealer fees
- Quote from the piece: *"Without the 30% ITC buffer, some installers can no longer afford to pass on the costs of high dealer fees"*
- Contrast data point: community solar subscriber acquisition costs **fell 12% in 2025 to $69/kW**, with expected 18% declines through 2030

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS (mine, not the source's):** This is the single number that reprices every solar business in 2026. A 40% CAC increase on a flat-to-declining ticket size eats margin directly, and it lands in the same year the 30% credit that used to justify the sales pitch is gone. Every owner reading this is either already feeling it in their cost-per-sale or is about to. It also frames the whole "your acquisition system is the business now" argument.

## 3. The Safe Harbor Window Closed Seventeen Days Ago

- **Category:** POLICY
- **Source:** The Tax Adviser (AICPA) — "Navigating safe-harbor rules for solar and wind Sec. 48E facilities"
- **URL:** https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2026/feb/navigating-safe-harbor-rules-for-solar-and-wind-sec-48e-facilities/
- **Date:** February 10, 2026 (author: Lynn Mucenski-Keck, CPA)
- **Verified:** Fetched successfully

**TL;DR raw material**
- Under OBBBA, wind and solar facilities beginning construction **after July 4, 2026** must be **placed in service by Dec. 31, 2027** to qualify for the credit
- Placed-in-service deadlines under the continuity safe harbor, by construction start:
  - Started **before Dec 31, 2025** → in service by **Dec 31, 2029**
  - Started **Jan 1 – July 4, 2026** → in service by **Dec 31, 2030**
  - Started **after July 4, 2026** → in service by **Dec 31, 2027**
- **5% cost test** (Notice 2022-61): construction begins if the taxpayer pays or incurs 5%+ of total facility cost and makes continuous efforts to complete
- **Physical-work test**: no fixed minimum dollar or percentage threshold; judged on the nature of the work
- **Notice 2025-42** (issued Aug 15, 2025, applies to construction beginning on or after **Sept. 2, 2025**): for solar/wind, **only the physical-work test applies** — EXCEPT low-output solar (**≤1.5 MW**), which keeps both options
- Prohibited Foreign Entity material-assistance thresholds: **50% non-PFE sourcing in 2026**, rising to **55% in 2027+**
- Executive Order 14315 (July 7, 2025) directed guidance to prevent circumvention of credit termination

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** This is the freshest hard deadline in the industry — it passed on July 4, seventeen days before this issue. Whoever safe-harbored equipment or started physical work before that date now has a four-year runway to 2030 and can quote a 30%-credit-backed TPO price. Whoever didn't is racing an 18-month clock to end-of-2027 with permitting and interconnection eating most of it. That is a real, durable pricing advantage between two installers competing on the same street, and most owners have not yet done the math on what it means for competitive bids in Q4.

## 6. Eight Years Of Panel Tariffs Just Expired

- **Category:** POLICY
- **Source:** Solar Power World — "End of an era: Sec. 201 tariffs on imported solar panels expire"
- **URL:** https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/02/end-of-an-era-sec-201-tariffs-on-imported-solar-panels-expire/
- **Date:** February 9, 2026
- **Verified:** Fetched successfully

**TL;DR raw material**
- **Section 201 tariffs expired February 6, 2026**, ending eight years of protection begun in Trump's first term and extended under Biden
- Rate history: **30% in 2018** with a 2.5 GW cell exemption for US assemblers, stepping down annually to **14% in 2025**, the final year
- Bifacial exemption history: exempt 2018 → removed 2020 → reinstated by the Court of International Trade 2021 → maintained 2022–2026
- **Still in force:** AD/CVD duties on Cambodia, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam (established 2025)
- **Pending:** AD/CVD on India, Indonesia and Laos — decision expected later in 2026
- **Pending:** Section 232 polysilicon investigation, initiated July 2025
- Trade counsel Timothy Brightbill: *"the actual remedies were largely ineffective"* — pointing to bifacial exemptions and country carveouts, with Chinese firms shifting operations to new countries as "tariff circumvention"
- Related pricing context from candidate #5: module prices $0.34/Wdc, down 21% YoY

**Why it matters — ANALYSIS:** Do not read this as "panels get cheaper." The 201 tariff was the least binding of the trade measures; the AD/CVD cases and the pending Section 232 polysilicon decision are the ones that can actually move your equipment cost, and a 232 finding would hit anything containing polysilicon regardless of country of origin. The practical action for an owner is to ask their distributor what happens to their price if 232 lands mid-quarter — because the answer determines whether current signed contracts are still profitable.
